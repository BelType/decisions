# The end gap takes no extension

**Status: Decided 2026-09-15, Executed 2026-09-15.** The round was convened to correct the shell arithmetic and the measurement moved it to the other page; what follows is decided from the operator dumps quoted in the Context, and the commissioning premise is withdrawn in the Decision rather than carried.

## Context

[*The first fragment is constructed*](the-first-fragment-is-constructed.md) deferred one half of §5.3 with its arithmetic already written down: *"A Class C cut in a container's end gap continues on the next page as a blank shell of `bottom − cut`; under §5.3 the container's content box extended to the edge on the page before, so the shell owed is `bottom − edge`. The correction is one subtraction in the driver's Class C branch, and it MOVES CUTS."* That deferral rests on the two records before it — [*the ink stops at the fragmentainer*](the-ink-stops-at-the-fragmentainer.md), which found that no clip can carry the extension, and [*the fragment that wraps itself*](the-fragment-that-wraps-itself.md), which priced the room a cloned block end costs the cut — and the checklist's part-three §5.3 row carries it as *the arithmetic half, open*.

**The premise was never measured, and it does not survive being measured.** Five throwaway documents were put to both engines through the case runner before a line of this entry was written, each a container with block-end padding and a block-end border divided near the page's foot, on the venue's own 200×300px page with 30px margins.

Two of them are not evidence about §5.3 at all, and finding that out is what the other three are for. Where the container holds several breakable children, **Chromium does not cut in the end gap: it retreats to a Class A boundary among the children**, so that the whole end gap lands on the last fragment. A container of nine lines with a 30px block-end padding crossing the edge: Cicero keeps all nine lines and cuts in the gap, Chromium keeps eight and cuts between children. A container of seven lines with a 20px border crossing the edge: Cicero keeps seven, Chromium six. Both partitions diverge, so neither document can adjudicate what a fragment's ink does — the two engines are not looking at the same fragment.

The other three hold their content in one monolithic child, which leaves Chromium nowhere to retreat to, and there the two engines partition **identically** and the readings are comparable:

| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| border 20px, padding 15px, one 180px child; content bottom 215px, gap bottom 230px, box bottom 250px, page edge 240px | background `105×168.75pt` | background `105×161.25pt` | background `105×26.25pt`, block-end border at 22.5,33.75 |

Read the columns. **Chromium's first fragment ends at its last child's bottom, every time** — 161.25pt is exactly 215px, 171pt is exactly 228px, 25.5pt is exactly 34px — and Cicero's ends that far plus the extension the first-fragment round now delivers, 10px and 2px and nothing. **And page two is the same number on both engines in all three**: the container's whole end gap plus its block-end border, `bottom − base`, which is 35px and 14px and 224px. Not `bottom − edge`, which those three geometries would make 10px and 2px and 18px.

So the shell was never too tall, and the round's subject is on the page before it.

## The shape, read off the code

Three facts, none of them guessed.

**The account already discriminates.** A Class C end-gap cut is the one cut that writes a blank shell — `driver.js` sets `shell = { remainingPx: v.box.rect.bottom - v.y, path }` with no `paintPx`, and `pageIntervals` carries it out as `end.shellPx` with `end.shellPaintPx` null. So *did this page's closing cut fall in a container's end gap* is a question the account answers already, and no new literal is owed.

**The extension's formula assumes the cut is the content's end, and for an end-gap cut it is not.** `root.js` computes `contentH − usedPx − cost`, and `usedPx` is the cut's own y. For a Class A or Class B cut that y is where the fragment's content stops, so the difference is exactly the room left. For a Class C end-gap cut the y is `min(gap.bottom, limit)` — inside the box's block-end padding, below its content — so the difference understates the room by the part of the gap the page showed, and the padding `truncateTrailingDecoration` zeroed is then replaced by a number computed as though it had not been. That is why Cicero's first fragment lands at `base + extPx` rather than anywhere either reading of the specification would put it.

**The shell's declared height is inert, and that is measurable.** The blank shell keeps the box's own block-end padding and border — `truncateLeadingDecoration` dresses the leading edge and nothing touches the trailing one — so with `box-sizing: border-box` the browser lays the shell out at no less than `padding-bottom + border-bottom`, which for a gap of pure padding *is* `bottom − base`. The three documents above show it: `remainingPx` said 20px, 4px and 18px, and the pages printed 35px, 14px and 224px. Cicero has been agreeing with Chromium on page two for a reason nobody had written down, which is this venue's own standing complaint about a green run.

## Decision

**One — a box divided in its own end gap takes no §5.3 extension.** The rule's sentence is *"its content box extends to fill any remaining fragmentainer extent … **before the content resumes on the next fragmentainer**"*, and where the cut falls in a container's block-end padding no content resumes: what continues is the box's own block-end decoration. The premise of the clause is absent, so the consequence is not owed — and Chromium, read on three documents it partitions identically with us, delivers none. Specification and oracle agree, which is the only footing this project takes an extension question on.

`extensionPx(page, entry)` is the account's arithmetic, and it moves to `positions.js` where the account's other arithmetic lives, DOM-free and unit-tested: the extension is `contentH − usedPx − cost` clamped at zero when the page's closing cut divided a block, and **zero when the page ends in a shell**. Gated on `shellPx` rather than on the blank shell specifically: a painted shell is the edge cut, whose y is the limit by construction and whose cost is zero, so its extension is provably zero already and the narrower test would buy nothing but a second clause.

**Two — the shell stays `bottom − cut`, and this entry says why rather than leaving it as the number that was there.** It is not read by the page: the browser's own floor for the shell box is the block-end decoration the shell still carries, which already equals the gap plus the border. Restating it as `bottom − base` would change the rendering in exactly one case — a gap holding the last child's bottom margin as well as the padding — and would thereby decide, with no measurement in hand, that a margin adjoining the break belongs to the second fragment, where §5.2 truncates margins adjoining a break to zero. A number that moves cuts in the corpus on a reading nothing has checked is not a correction.

**Three — a zero remainder is already reachable and already right.** A container with block-end padding and no border has `gap.bottom === bottom`, so a cut taken there leaves `remainingPx` zero; the shell then renders at its own block-end padding, which is where §5.4's `slice` puts the decoration of the box's last fragment. Nothing is special-cased, the walk's own progress test counts entering a shell as progress regardless of its height, and the question the round was asked to decide — whether a zero-or-negative shell should be no shell at all, with the next page resuming at the following sibling — **does not arise**, because the shell is measured from a cut that lies inside the box by the Class C guard's own `y > gap.base`. Dropping the shell there would also make the walk's ability to advance depend on whether the divided container happened to contain a word, which is the word space's known under-determination wearing a new coat.

**Refused: the shell measured from the fragmentainer's edge**, `bottom − edge` under `slice` and `bottom − (edge − cost)` under `clone` — the premise this round was convened on. Measured against it: the three comparable documents print 35px, 14px and 224px on page two where that arithmetic asks for 10px, 2px and 18px, on both engines. It follows from an extension that is not delivered.

**Refused: delivering the extension from the box's own content bottom**, `edge − cost − base`, which is what the first fragment would need to reach the fragmentainer at all. It is the same refusal from the other side: Chromium reaches the fragmentainer on a cut whose content resumes and does not on a cut in an end gap, and the three documents show the second.

**Refused: a second case under `clone`, with a proof rather than a shrug.** A Class C end-gap candidate is charged `clonedBlockEnd(box)`; the Class A boundary after the same box is charged `clonedBlockEnd(box.parent)`, which is the same sum less the box's own cloned block-end border and padding, and it stands exactly the box's block-end border lower. So the C candidate fits only if the A candidate fits, and the A candidate is later, so the make-up order takes it every time. Where no such boundary exists — the box is a top-level block, or the last child of one — the charge is what pushes the cut out of the gap entirely and back among the children. **Under `clone` a Class C end-gap cut is not reachable in a document that has anywhere else to go**, which is why the `cost` term is not written into the shell at all and why this round has one case and not two.

**Refused: a unit test that re-spells the guard.** The arithmetic gets one, in `positions.test.js`, because it is genuinely the account's and genuinely DOM-free. What it cannot check is that `root.js` calls it, and the thing that checks that is the case.

## Consequences

The checklist's part-three §5.3 row and the design-opens index stop carrying *the shell arithmetic, open, one subtraction in the driver's Class C branch, and it moves cuts*. What replaces it is shorter and measured: **a box divided in its own end gap takes no extension**, the shell is the whole end gap plus the block-end border on both engines, and the two limits the first-fragment round named — the middle fragment of a block spanning three pages, and a page a parallel flow crosses — are what is left of §5.3 beside the block-size-limits half.

`architecture.md`'s slot-versus-fragment section stops saying that a page whose closing cut divided a block carries the extension and the clip to the page area's edge without qualification: it does so where content resumes, and a shell page carries neither.

Next door, `cases/48-the-end-gap-divided` holds the reading, chapter seven of the book gains it, and `RESULTS.md` records what the battery said.

**What this owes.** Nothing is deferred by this entry that was not already deferred by the one before it. The middle fragment of a block spanning three pages still takes no extension (P34d, page two at `105×160.5pt` against Chromium's `105×180`), and it is **not** the same mechanism as this round's: it wants a fragment that both resumes and ends — the two consumptions composed on one clone, with the reading copy and the `__beltype-cut` dressing decided for it — where this round is one condition on an account literal. It stays open, named here and on the checklist.

### What was too good to be true

Not the arithmetic, which was one condition and a move of two lines into the module that already owned them. What was too good to be true was **the brief**, and it had been written down by a round that had just measured a great deal correctly: *one subtraction in the driver's Class C branch, and it MOVES CUTS*. Both halves were wrong. It is not a subtraction in the driver, it is a condition in the presentation, and it moves no cut anywhere — the battery reproduced all twenty-five gates. The deferral had been reasoned from the extension the round before it had just built, rather than from a document put to the two engines, and one runner invocation would have said so on the day it was written. The venue's own rule names the failure exactly: a claim carried forward without anything that can fail it.

**And one thing was confirmed rather than assumed, which is the round's real find.** Cicero's page two has been printing Chromium's own number for a container's end gap all along, and not because `remainingPx` says so — it says `bottom − cut`, which is smaller — but because the blank shell still wears the box's block-end padding and border, and the browser will not lay a box out shorter than those. A correction to that number would have been made against a reading nothing had checked, and it is left where it is with the reason written beside it in `architecture.md` and in `fragments.js`.
