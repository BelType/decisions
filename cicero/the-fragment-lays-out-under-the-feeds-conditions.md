# The fragment lays out under the feeds' conditions

**Status: Decided 2026-10-08, Executed 2026-10-08.** Drafted and ruled the same day with its sibling [*a parallel flow is a fragmentainer*](a-parallel-flow-is-a-fragmentainer.md): Egildo ruled every sentence of the round's phase 0 on 2026-10-08, I5's fragment clause among them, and decided the two forks below. The survey is [`../../rounds/cicero/stage-0-a-parallel-flow-is-a-fragmentainer.md`](https://github.com/egildo/beltype/blob/42b348b547d269a217a7f193042fe216386a4650/.claude/rounds/cicero/stage-0-a-parallel-flow-is-a-fragmentainer.md) and, before it, [`../../rounds/cicero/stage-0-the-table-lays-out-at-its-measured-widths.md`](https://github.com/egildo/beltype/blob/42b348b547d269a217a7f193042fe216386a4650/.claude/rounds/cicero/stage-0-the-table-lays-out-at-its-measured-widths.md); the table round's own plan, [`../../rounds/cicero/round-plan-the-table-lays-out-at-its-measured-widths.md`](https://github.com/egildo/beltype/blob/42b348b547d269a217a7f193042fe216386a4650/.claude/rounds/cicero/round-plan-the-table-lays-out-at-its-measured-widths.md), is superseded by the round plan and read here only for its cell design.

## Context

I5 says a break decision is valid only under render-identical conditions, and the engine enforces it for the feed and for nothing else. *[The Cast-off bounds the feed](the-cast-off-bounds-the-feed.md)* stated the premise that fails — block layout is forward, so putting away the boxes after a boundary moves nothing before it — named the boxes where it is false (a table, a flex or grid container, a multicol container, a monolith, a parallel flow) and kept the feed whole inside them. Nothing kept the *fragment* whole. *[The clone is hollow outside the interval](the-clone-is-hollow-outside-the-interval.md)* stated its own premise so that it could break — the browser lays out the displayed content of a fragment identically whether the boxes outside its interval are deep and emptied or hollow — and guarded one selector shape, `:has(`, by falling back to deep clones, which keeps an automatic table's widths honest by accident for exactly the documents whose sheet carries that selector and for no other.

The premise breaks for the boxes that read their children. A fragment is a clone with every box outside its interval hollowed and laid out again, and a box whose size depends on content it no longer holds is a different box. **Measured 2026-10-08:**

- **A table.** On the manual's routes table, divided on pages 67/68, the feed's columns are 178.4 / 70.3 / 100.4 / 187.5 / 104.9px and the first fragment's 175.8 / 70.3 / 101.0 / 177.3 / 117.1; four rows above the cut re-wrap one 18.59px line each, the cut row moves 74.38px under a clip that stays, six words and thirty-eight characters fall below it. The arithmetic is exact: 74.38 − 49.52 + 9.50 = 34.36px (P37a). On the synthetic table of case 90: columns 63.2 / 58.4 / 60.0 / 178.4 in the feed, 58.4 / 58.4 / 83.7 / 159.5 in the first fragment, six words. Only the first fragment disagrees with its feed; a continuation always equals the feed that decided its own page.
- **A grid.** Tracks 81.6 / 196.8 / 81.6px in the feed, 120 / 120 / 120 in the first fragment, because the second row — wholly on the next page, holding the chain that kept column two wide — is put away; twelve words under the clip (P37d).
- **A flex row.** Widths drift by a few pixels in 235 of 281 documents and by 125px where an item aligned to the block end is emptied; no shown line re-wraps below the foot in any of them (P37d). A drift, not yet a loss.
- **A divided row's extension.** §5.3 asks a divided box's content box to extend to the fragmentainer's foot. `mutateToFirstFragment` writes it on one element, the innermost box the main path divides, passes it to no row item, and `extensionPx` returns 0 whenever the main path ends in a shell, which a divided row's does when its last item has nothing left but the row's stretch: case 94's cells end 16px above the foot (P37f).
- **The composed mode** keeps one set of widths in all three containers, because its lines are `nowrap` and a cell's or a track's minimum is its widest composed line (P37a, P37d; inferred, not isolated by a run).

The collapse candidates are closed by measurement. `display: none` on a hollow row removes it from the column sizing (58.42px against 231.25); `visibility: collapse` keeps a row's content sizing the columns and generates a box of no height, but only with the content kept, which is the deep clone *the clone is hollow* removed for cost; a hollow collapsed row sizes nothing (P37b). So there is no way to hide a row and keep its width contribution without keeping the row.

## Decision

**A fragment is written the conditions its feed was measured under, as literals.** Every condition that depends on content outside the interval — a table's column widths, a flex or grid container's track sizes, and whatever a probe finds next — is read from the feed's measurement of the page and written into the fragment (I5 amended; C5 admits it, being a fragment hint and never a write to the source). The mechanism, in the order the plan puts the work:

1. **The Gauge answers `tracksOf(box)`** for a box whose display reads its children — a table's column widths from the border boxes of the widest row's cells, a flex or grid container's track sizes from its items or its computed track list — once per box per page, from the report that already exists. `READS_ITS_CHILDREN` moves to `word-space.js` so the Gauge, the Stonehand and the fragments share one predicate (today `neverCutInside` and the fragment code each ask).
2. **`fragments.js` writes them** on every box on a fragment's cut chain and every item that reads its children. A table: each cell of the first displayed row receives its measured width as a literal, with `table-layout: fixed` on the clone and the table's own width — no `<colgroup>`, since one shifts `:nth-child` over a table's children. A grid: the container's `grid-template-columns` as the measured px list. A flex row: **no literal in this round** — the drift loses nothing, and it is filed in the checklist as measured. A grid's emptied or spent item keeps its measured track, so its column holds. Per fragment, from that page's measurement, never cached across widths (C7).
3. **§5.3's extension reaches every divided box of the chain and every divided item** (case 94), and `extensionPx`'s shell test is reconsidered where the shell is a row item's stretch and not a box's end gap. *The end gap takes no extension* stands for the case it was written for: no content resumes past a box cut in its block-end gap.
4. **The hollow row's `display: none` stays** — the collapse measurement closed that candidate — and the continuation's own dress is pinned the same way where its page's feed differs from the table's (case 94's spent cell, inferred to carry `box-sizing: border-box`).

**Refused alternatives.**

- **Deep clones for every box that reads its children.** The `:has(` guard generalised: it keeps the widths honest by accident and costs what *the clone is hollow* saved (the BGD manual 3.02 s against 0.50 s, BelType's 13.6 s against 3.39 s).
- **Collapse instead of hollow** (the table round's candidate 2): needs the row's content kept, which is the deep clone.
- **Estimating the widths decision-side** — table layout is an algorithm in the browser; computing it ourselves is a second layout engine, which I5 forbids.
- **Hollowing the feed too, so the two agree.** The feed is the one that matches the browser's print, which lays the whole table out; the fragment is what must reproduce it. `neverCutInside` exists to keep the feed whole in these boxes for that reason.
- **Fixing only the table**, the table round's scope. A grid loses words by the same cause; the sibling probe found it and the flex row drifts. One premise, one clause.
- **A cache of widths across pages.** C7 is a lemma about runs of equal content width; a width change suspends it, and the literals are re-read per fragment.

4. **Case 94 is green in both modes**: both cells end at the foot (90pt of vertical, not 78), no border is drawn at the cut or at the head, and the continuation's grid line stands where the table's does (182 / 178px, not 188 / 172). *Breaks* where the extension reaches the cells and the continuation's columns still differ — the inferred `box-sizing` cause is then wrong.
5. **No battery record moves.** The generator draws no table, flex row or grid. A moved record is the literals leaking into in-flow adjudication. The acceptance document stays at 12 pages.
6. **The `:has(` guard keeps working**: cases 60, 61 and 69 green; with the guard forced off in scratch, case 69 goes red as it did before (the literals do not stand in for the deep clone where the selector reads inside a hollow box).
7. **`:nth-child` and `>` over a table's children: no element enters the author's tree.** Ruled 2026-10-08: the widths are written on the first displayed row's cells, because a `<colgroup>` shifts `table > :first-child` and the `:nth-child` of a table's sections by one in a document whose sheet addresses them. ## What was not decided, and is

Two forks, ruled by Egildo on 2026-10-08. **The colgroup against the cell widths: the cell widths.** Widths written on the clone's cells of the first displayed row enter no element and touch the clone alone, never the source (C5); `<col>` elements would enter the author's tree and shift structural selectors over the table's children. **A flex row's literal: none in this round.** Nothing is lost by the drift (P37d: no shown line re-wraps below the foot), so the literal waits for a measured loss; it is filed on the checklist as measured.

## Consequences

**What it displaces.** The premise stated by *the clone is hollow outside the interval* — *the browser lays out the displayed content of a fragment identically whether the boxes outside its interval are deep and emptied or hollow* — is measured false for the boxes `neverCutInside` names, and is replaced by a condition the fragment is written. That record's decision, its guard and its verdict stand unchanged; this record adds the clause its premise needed. *The Cast-off bounds the feed* is untouched: the feed is whole in those boxes because the browser's print is, and the fragment now agrees with it.

**What it owes.** The tablet's I5 sentence (ruled 2026-10-08); the architecture's build-loop step 6 and `MeasureReport.tracksOf` (ruled 2026-10-08); the checklist's §4 line (`design, build owed`); phase 2 and its verdict.

**Where the evidence lives.** Probes P37a to P37f in `packages/essay/probes/table-widths.js`, cases 90 and 94, the stage-0 reports above.

## Built, phase 2 (2026-10-08)

What phase 2 built, and the four places it departed from the sentences above or added to them:

- **The mechanism as decided.** `gauge.js`' `tracksOf(box)`: a table's column widths from the border boxes of the first displayed row that has every column once (no cell spanning columns, as many cells as the widest row has columns; a table with no such row answers `null` and is laid out as it always was), plus its own width; a grid's resolved `grid-template-columns`. The driver reads them off the report that decided the page for every table and grid on the chain of the cursor's places and the entry's places, a row item's included, and the account entry carries them (`tracks`, keyed by the box's path). `fragments.js`' `pinTracks` writes them in the three public constructors (`continuationClone`, `firstFragmentClone`, `fragmentOf`), after the cuts: `table-layout: fixed`, `box-sizing: border-box` and the measured width on the table, `box-sizing: border-box` and the measured width (a spanning cell the sum) on each cell of the first displayed row — refused, and the table left as it was, where that row does not cover exactly the measured columns — and the column list on the grid. `READS_ITS_CHILDREN` lives in `word-space.js`, one predicate for the Gauge, the Stonehand and the fragment writer.
- **The feed's own clones are not pinned.** The numbers are read from the feed's layout, so pinning the feed would be circular; the feed holds the box whole and the continuation of the next page is measured in turn. "The feed and the presentation share one construction" holds for the cuts, which is the rule; the literals are the presentation's, from the page's own measurement.
- **A grid is pinned, by judgement.** The ruling named tables and flex; the grid loses words by the same cause (twelve in P37d), so it takes the measured column list. The flex row takes nothing, as ruled.
- **§5.3's extension for the items of a divided row is a floor read off the feed, not the page's number.** The gate was reconsidered as the record asked: `extensionPx` returns 0 for a closing shell only where nothing resumes beside it, and a divided row's last item ending in a shell while another item resumes is not that case (a unit test pins both). That alone moved no pixel in case 94, because a row is cut at the page edge and its `usedPx` is the edge: the extension arithmetic (`contentH − usedPx − cost`) is 0 although the cells' lines end 16px above it. So each item a row cut divides takes `min-height` (a table cell `height`, which is a cell's floor) of the distance from ITS top to the foot, measured in the feed and carried as `extents`; the recursion into the items also passes the page's `extPx` on, so a divided paragraph inside a cell takes its own. No clip exists on a divided row beyond the page's: `setFlow`'s flow clip and the painted shell's slice wrapper are the only two in the engine.

## Verdict

Closed 2026-10-08 on the round's branch. The battery is the sibling record's: one complete `gates:fresh`, 26 gates of 26 in 16.7 minutes, the print references rebuilt, against the merge commit `67d5b94` (engine source tree `5350234`), the pin standing for it; the unmerged branch had read the same 26 gates in 16.1 minutes at `e9e6cc6`. Phase 2's measurements are from the runs of 2026-10-08 at the end of that phase.

**Prediction 1 held.** On the synthetic table (P37a) the first fragment's columns equal the feed's, 63.2 / 58.4 / 60.0 / 178.4px; the divided row moves 0.00px (it moved 20); row 4 stays 40px; no word is under the clip; case 90 is green in both modes, its Chromium differential ("page for page and word for word") passing.

**Prediction 2 held.** On the manual's routes table the first fragment's columns equal the feed's, 178.4 / 70.3 / 100.4 / 187.5 / 104.9px; the cut row moves 0.00px (74.38 before); six words and thirty-eight characters that fell below the clip are on a page. The conservation gate is what keeps it: against the engine at the round's base it reads exactly 38 characters on page 68, the same row, and none with the round.

**Prediction 3 held, with its flex half withdrawn by the ruling.** The grid's first-fragment tracks are the feed's, 81.6 / 196.8 / 81.6px (120 / 120 / 120 before), the twelve words are on the page, lost 0 against Chromium's print; composed was already immune. A grid is pinned by judgement, the ruling having named tables and flex.

**Prediction 4 held, by one more mechanism than predicted.** Case 94 is green in both modes: both cells end at the foot (0px from the clip, not 16), no border at the cut or at the head, the continuation's grid line where the table's is (182 / 178px, not 188 / 172). The extension reached the cells only through a number the account did not have: a row is cut at the page edge, so its `usedPx` is the edge and `contentH − usedPx − cost` is 0 although the cells' lines end 16px above it. Each item a row cut divides takes `min-height` (a table cell, `height`) of the distance from its top to the foot, measured in the feed and carried as `extents`; the recursion into the items also hands the page's `extPx` on, so a divided paragraph inside a cell takes its own. The continuation's columns came right through the pinned widths, so the inferred `box-sizing` cause was not needed to explain the case.

**Prediction 5 held, measured.** No battery record moved in either run, the six differential tallies and the three views included, and the acceptance document stays at twelve pages in both modes.

**Prediction 6 held, measured today.** Cases 60, 61 and 69 are green with the guard on. With the guard forced off in scratch (`sheetsUseHas` returning false, restored by `git checkout` afterwards), 60 and 61 stay green and **69 goes red in both modes** — page 2 carries no fill `#e6f2d9` at 15,15,270,75 — as it did before the round: the literals do not stand in for the deep clone where a selector reads inside a hollow box.

**Prediction 7 held.** No element enters the author's tree: the widths are written on the first displayed row's cells, `table-layout: fixed` and the table's width hold the columns, in the synthetic table, the manual's and case 94, and `:nth-child` and `>` over a table's children address what they addressed.

**Prediction 8 stands as written.** A stranger's table is the fourth witness; the cases are 90 and 94 and the manual is the third, and the manual is the weakest of them now (see the sibling record's *too good to be true*).

**What was too good to be true.** (1) The extension did not travel on the arithmetic that was reconsidered: relaxing `extensionPx`'s shell test moved no pixel in case 94, and the cell's floor had to be read off the feed, which the record's step three did not say. (2) The inferred `box-sizing` cause of the continuation's columns was a story the evidence did not need. (3) A table whose first displayed row does not cover the measured columns (a `rowspan` from an interval's put-away row) is left as laid out: the build refuses rather than guesses, and nothing draws it. (4) The flex literal was withdrawn, so the flex row's drift stays measured and filed (235 of 281 documents move a width, none re-wraps a shown line), and the grid was pinned by judgement beyond the ruling.
