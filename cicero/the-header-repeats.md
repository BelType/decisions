# The header repeats

**Status: Decided 2026-10-14, Executed 2026-10-14.** Ruled by Egildo on 2026-10-14, in the round *counters and reads*, reversing the declining the tablet recorded for CSS 2.1 §17.2 and the checklist's §17.2 entry carried. Built by the same round; the measurement that changed its shape is below.

## Context

CSS 2.1 §17.2 says a print UA *may* repeat header rows, and may repeat footer rows, on each page a table spans. The tablet declined the permission (probe P31 had measured repeated furniture to be a fixed height per fragment, which is why a table is *constant* and files with the floats) and case 25 recorded the divergence from Chromium, which takes it: 9/11/4 against 9/10/5 on the same twenty-four rows. The declining was a choice and not an oversight, and the tablet said it was reversible the day a document needed it. The studio's print round and its real bills needed it. The same ruling gave every copy the engine repeats ONE `data-beltype` word, `artifact`, so that the header would not be a fourth ad hoc spelling of "this is a copy".

## Decision

**A divided table repeats its `<thead>` at the top of every continuation and its `<tfoot>` at the foot of every fragment but the last, as Chromium does; neither repeats when it is taller than a quarter of the page area; and every copy wears `artifact`.** The word is PDF's own name for pagination furniture, so every reader has one rule: Caster tags it as an artifact, Cicero's word space skips it, a host puts no caret in it. It is written on a running element's copies, on a fixed box's copies after the first, and on the table's group copies.

The copy is the clone's own group, kept instead of put away (`fragments.js`: `putAway` keeps a header or footer group and marks it; `cloneInterval` builds it deep, not hollow), by the one implementation that builds a continuation and a first fragment for both the feed and the presentation. The plan expected the measurement to see the header's height and the cut to move **with no new arithmetic**. **The measurement refuted that, and the record keeps the finding: the header reached the feed (22.67 px in the measured clone, the first body row beneath it) and the cut did not move, because `driver.js`'s `spentLeadOf` reads the gap between the resumed row and the table's content edge as a spent lead — the margin that adjoins a break — and moved the page start down by exactly the header's height.** So the round builds three pieces of arithmetic, each with its own reason and its own commit:

1. **The header's footprint is not a spent lead.** A level of the chain that has an `artifact` copy before the child owes no lead (`standsAfterArtifact`). The copy is furniture with a fixed footprint (P31), not a margin. A box with no such copy before it owes the arithmetic exactly what it owed before, which the unit test and the battery hold.
2. **A fragment that is not the table's last gives up the footer's height from its usable extent** (`reservePx`, on the account entry). This is FOP's penalty width, already named in the checklist, and Blink's `repeated_footer_block_size`. "Last" is answered in the same pass, with no fixpoint and no re-run: the remainder, with its own footer at its end, fits the page (`fits`), or it does not and the footer of the table the page's edge crosses is reserved. The first fragment's extension (§5.3) and the flow's clip are taken from the reserved extent, so the footer stands at the page's foot inside the clip. One table's footer per page, the outermost in document order; a nested table's own footer is not reserved (stated, `ponytail:`).
3. **The quarter guard is decided once, at the table's first fragment, from the measured group.** The first feed that shows a group judges it (`decideRepeats`): it repeats when its height is a quarter of the page area or less, and the answer rides that page's account entry (`repeats`), so every later page, a resumed run (seeded from the kept entries) and the presentation read it and never measure again. Header and footer are judged separately. When a COPY of a group that turns out not to repeat is already in the feed, the feed is poured once more without it: the one re-pour the rule costs, and only for a footer or a header too tall to repeat. Blink's second condition, an applicable `break-inside: avoid`, is the UA's own for these groups and reads `auto` in the pinned build's computed style (measured), so it is not asked.

**A table whose footer is written before its rows keeps its old behaviour: neither group repeats.** The engine has never cut such a table well (measured 2026-10-14, main included: the rows are lost), and a repeated header there makes the walk fail to advance — a silent loss would have become no pagination at all. It is recorded in the checklist as a finding of its own. *Amended 2026-10-15, below.*

**Refused:** a repeat decided per page from that page's own measurement (it would contradict a Chromium that decides once, and a header that fits on page two and not on page three is a document nobody wrote on purpose); a footer copy placed by the feed's own arithmetic as a variable height (it is a constant, P31); repeating a group inside a nested table.

## Consequences

- Case 25 is rewritten from a recorded divergence into an agreement (9/10/5 in both columns); case 108 holds the footer and the guard against Chromium's print; its partition is identical in both Cicero modes. Cases 24 and 41 hold unchanged.
- The tablet's and the architecture's declining sentences, the checklist's §17.2 entry and its line of landings say what is true now. Caster prints the copies as repeats (its own DESIGN.md entry).
- The word space skips an `artifact` subtree on the clone side (`joinedWords`, `textsIn`): a copy's words are in no word of the box that carries it, which is what keeps the cut's word arithmetic honest.
- Owed to the studio, not done here: its caret and selection skip `artifact`.
- How the copy joins the rows, the spacing above it and the collapsed border under it, is ruled by [*a repeated header joins the rows as Chromium prints them*](a-repeated-header-joins-the-rows-as-chromium-prints-them.md) (2026-10-22).
- Left standing: a footer in a nested table is not reserved; a collapsed-border table's footer reservation uses the group's height and a border-spacing of zero; the guard measures once per run, so a stylesheet edit that changes a header's height re-decides only when the page that first showed it is cut again.

## Amendment 2026-10-15 — the footer before the rows

The clause that a table whose footer is written before its rows keeps its old behaviour is **withdrawn**: such a table repeats its header and footer like any other. The round *the footer before the rows* found why it had never been cut well, and the reason was the walk's, not the repeat's. The retreat walk stops at the first box whose top lies below the page's edge, on the premise that in-flow tops do not go backward in document order; §17.2 lays a footer group out after every row group wherever it is written, so a footer written second stood forty rows down and the walk stopped at it before it read a row. The Gauge now walks such a footer where it is laid out (`laidOutLast`, behind the last row group, in the procession and in the box list), the Stonehand offers no place before it or inside it, since its words stand before the rows in the word space, and with that the walk advances and the copies cost nothing new. Case 110 holds it against Chromium's print in both modes, word for word, with conservation.

One consequence differs from a footer written last, stated rather than cased. A footer written before its rows that is too tall to repeat is shown once, on the table's **first** fragment, with its height reserved there (the reservation reads it where it is shown, not only where it repeats), where Chromium shows it on the last: its words are the first fragment's in the document's order, and nothing here moves them. No word is lost; the partition diverges. A word space in §17.2's display order inside a table is the change that would remove it, and it is not made here.
