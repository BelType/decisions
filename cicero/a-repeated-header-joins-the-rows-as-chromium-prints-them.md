# A repeated header joins the rows as Chromium prints them

**Status: Decided 2026-10-22, Executed 2026-10-22.** Convened by two findings of *the header row* (P41, `packages/essay/probes/header-row.js`, [the memo](https://github.com/egildo/beltype/blob/42b348b547d269a217a7f193042fe216386a4650/.claude/rounds/cicero/the-header-row-probe.md)), measured 2026-10-22 in both modes against Chromium's print and filed under that date in `.claude/NEEDED-FIXES.md`. Opened by Egildo on 2026-10-22, cases red first.

## Context

A divided table repeats its `<thead>` at the top of every continuation ([*the header repeats*](the-header-repeats.md), 2026-10-14): the continuation is one table, the header's copy wearing `artifact` and then the body from the cut row on (`fragments.js`, `repeatedGroup`). The page row for that header proposed the same day was probed and refused ([*a competing flow bargains the foot of the main flow*](a-competing-flow-bargains-the-foot-of-the-main-flow.md), Refused), and on the way the probe measured the join inside today's copy, where no standing case looks: cases 25 and 108 both set `border-collapse: separate` with `border-spacing: 0`, so neither sees a collapsed border nor a spacing.

Two divergences at the join, both modes equal, each against Chromium's print of the same document:

- **Collapsed borders.** With `border-collapse: collapse` and the header's bottom border narrower than the body's top (red 1 px against blue 2 px in the probe), Chromium's continuation paints the first body row's top border over the repeated header's bottom, and the body row sits where the border arithmetic of the whole table puts it. The engine's copy resolves the join inside the continuation's own table and lands the first body row 1 px low, with different ink at the join (10 368 of 148 480 quarter-pixel samples differ on the probe's strip).
- **Border spacing.** With `border-collapse: separate; border-spacing: 4px`, Chromium drops the spacing above a repeated header: the continuation's first ink is the header's own top border. The engine's copy keeps the table's spacing above the header, and everything on the continuation sits 4 px low (19 840 of 163 840 samples).

What the text says. CSS 2.1 §17.2 lets a header group repeat on each page fragment and says nothing of its borders there. css-tables-3 §6.3 resolves a collapsed border between adjacent cells, rows and the table's edge, and names no fragment. css-break-3 §4.1 slices box decorations at a break by default (`box-decoration-break: slice`): a fragment's edge at the break carries no border and no padding of the box that was cut, which is the nearest the text comes to the spacing above a repeated header, and it is silent on the header's own edge. Where the text is silent the oracle is evidence, not authority (the tablet, The Oracle), and here the engine follows it because the rule it implies is also the only one that keeps a divided table at the page positions the whole table has: a continuation adds no ink and no room at its head beyond the header's own box.

## Decision

**A repeated header joins the rows of its continuation as Chromium prints them: the join is the whole table's, and the continuation adds no room of its own above or below the copy.** Two rulings.

1. **The collapsed join is resolved as the whole table resolves it.** At the join of a repeated header's copy and the first body row of a continuation, the border painted is the one the whole table paints between its header and its first body row, at that width, and the first body row's top edge lands where the border arithmetic of the whole table puts it, never pushed down by a second resolution inside the copy. The mechanism is the round's to find and the Verdict's to state; the probe's correction for its separate header table (the header pulled up by half each collapsed border) is a measurement, not the rule.
2. **No spacing above a repeated header.** On a continuation of a table with `border-collapse: separate`, the `border-spacing` the table puts between its top edge and its first row is not drawn above the repeated header's copy: the continuation's first ink is the copy's own top border, and the first body row lands where Chromium lands it.

**Refused.** A page row for the header (the probe's memo, and the design record's Refused). An author switch for either join (the join is the oracle's, with no second rendering to choose). Reaching the oracle by moving the body copy with a margin (the engine's own sheet strips a continuation's top margin, and the probe measured that a negative margin does nothing). A fix that holds in one mode only: both modes or the case stays red by name.

## Verdict

Executed 2026-10-22 on `round/a-repeated-header-joins-the-rows-as-chromium-prints-them`, in two passes: the first fold lifted every copy whose join differed into the table's caption, and Egildo sent the mechanism back the same day, since the default 2 px spacing put nearly every separated table under the lift and the caption gave up columns and ink for it. The mechanism as it stands is two pieces, one per ruling.

- **The spacing is a spent lead, in the grid.** The words sheet already strips a resumed table's top border, padding and margin, as Chromium slices them; the spacing between the table's edge and its first row is what remained. A negative top margin on the continuation table, even written inline and `!important` past the sheet, moves nothing (the header at 18.375 pt before and after), because a page begins at its first box's border edge and the lead shift puts the table back. So the driver counts the spacing as spent: in `driver.js`'s `spentLeadOf`, at the level where the resumed row stands after the header's copy in a separated table, the distance from the table's content edge to the copy's top is owed, the feed begins that much lower and the presentation lifts the flow by it. The copy never leaves the grid; the header's copy is the one read, never a footer's written before the rows.
- **The lift, for collapse alone.** Where the header's bottom border is wider than the first body row's top under collapsed borders, the copy becomes the table's caption (`fragments.js`, `liftRepeatedHeader`, called on the boxes of the cut's chain in `mutateToContinuation`): the caption draws the header's bottom whole, the table box draws the body row's top at its sliced edge, and the caption's bottom margin `J/2 - a - t/2` (the whole table's join, the caption's own bottom border, the body row's top) puts the second border's centre on the first's. `pinTable` pins the caption's rows with the grid's first row. Every other copy stays in the grid. Two in-grid alternatives were measured on P41b's strip, both modes alike, and neither reaches the ink: narrowing the copy's last row's bottom border to the body row's top width, 2 px, stands the first body row 1 px high and paints 2 px of red, 15 104 of 148 480 samples differing, since at a tie the collapsing model paints the upper cell's colour; narrowing it to half the join plus half the body's top, 3 px, lands the row at 0 px with 3 px of red and no blue, 15 232 differing. The caption reaches 0, so it stays, and Egildo decides between position-only in the grid and the caption on these numbers.

1. **Red first, two cases: held, on the probe's shape rather than the record's wording.** The record named a header whose bottom border is narrower than the body's top (red 1 px against blue 2 px); measured the same day, that shape already joins as Chromium prints it, because the whole table resolves the join to the body's blue and so does the copy, and both prints carry the same pixels. The probe's own shape was the wider header, red 4 px over blue 2 px, and case 120 takes it. Committed red in both modes by the predicted numbers: in 120 the first body row's bottom border at 55.5 pt where Chromium's is at 54.75 pt, 1 px low on pages two to four, and no blue over the red; in 121 the header's cell at 18.375 pt and the first body row's at 40.875 pt where Chromium's are at 15.375 and 37.875 pt, 4 px low. Chromium green on every claim, and the partition identical, four pages each.
2. **Both cases green in both modes: held, by both passes.** Every continuation's first body row within a quarter pixel (0.1875 pt) of Chromium's, columns included (121's cells at 24.37, 99.37 and 174.37 pt, as Chromium's), and in 120 the red 3 pt at 34.5 pt with the blue 1.5 pt at 35.25 pt over it, as Chromium paints them. The lead gives the room back as well as the place: on a 305 px page a continuation of 121's table holds 7 rows and on a 306 px page 8, in Chromium and in both modes.
3. **Cases 25 and 108 do not move: held.** 25 three pages and 108 five, in both modes and in Chromium, the same pages and words; `reservePx` and the quarter-page guard untouched; neither table is lifted, and both separate their borders with a spacing of zero, so neither owes a lead. Case 110 holds too, once the lead read the header's copy and not the footer's written before the rows, which the second pass's first run took for the header (2 pages where 3 were due).
4. **Every standing record reproduces: held.** After the first pass the battery was 25 gates of 26 at its first run (the engine's DOM-free suites build continuations from doubles with no query methods), 26 of 26 at its second against `fd8615f`. After the second pass the lift walks the cut's chain, which the doubles already answer, and the battery is 26 of 26 in 6.8 minutes against `e452134`, the pin standing. Root `npm test` 411 of 411, `npm run check` green, the engine's DOM-free suites 350 of 350; P41b, re-run with `node probes/header-row.js --only b`, measures today's copy at 0 of 148 480 samples differing under collapse and 0 of 163 840 under separate, body 0 px from the oracle's, in both modes.

**Caster.** Cases 120 and 121 printed through the print panel (`packages/caster/test/drive.js`, the editing tools on Cicero), with Source Code Pro for Courier, since the printer embeds font programs and the cases' system face is not one: no error and no refusal, four pages each, the header's words once on every page and every body row's words once in the file, which is *the conservation of words* (`DESIGN.md` S61) holding; one `Table`, one `THead` and 31 `TR`, so the three copies of each table, 120's lifted into the caption and 121's in the grid, are read as pagination artifacts and not as rows. No Caster code changed.

**What the record did not foresee.** First, ruling 1's sentence says the first body row lands where the border arithmetic of the whole table puts it, and Chromium does not do that: the line between header and body is the whole table's, but the row's content stands half the row's own top border below it, not half the shared join, one pixel higher than the whole table's arithmetic on the probe's shape. The fold follows the print, which is what prediction 2 judges, and the sentence should read *the line is the whole table's, the half below it the row's own*. Second, the spacing ruling reaches nearly every separated table, since Chromium's default sheet gives a table `border-spacing: 2px`; in the grid that costs a lead and nothing else. Third, what the passes leave short of the print, stated: a table's own background and side borders paint the spent spacing into the page's top margin (3 pt on a 4 px spacing), because the head of the flow's clip is the page box by ruling, where Chromium starts them at the page area; and the caption keeps its ceilings for the collapsed shape it alone serves: its cells unpinned in the feed, as no feed's are, so a header that wraps differently at its natural widths could stand at another height there than on the page; the header no longer sizing an automatic-layout continuation's columns; a table's own side borders not resolving with the caption's cells; and a `thead`'s own border missing from the ink while the rows stand right.
