# Only the pages near the reader are built

**Status: Decided 2026-10-12, Executed 2026-10-12.** Ruled by Egildo in the `cicero-optimisation` round (the first two rulings 2026-10-08, find-in-page and the scope 2026-10-09); the design note was `.claude/plans/cicero-optimisation/windowing.md`, the brief from prior art `.claude/prior-art/reports/pdf.js-6.4.299-2026-10-08.md`. It changes how the pages are presented and not where any of them breaks.

## Context

After pagination `root.present()` built every page in one synchronous task: a `<beltype-page>` per page with its margin boxes, then its content — the ORIGINAL block where a block stands whole on the page, a constructed COPY where the page divides a block, composes it or holds a nested fixed box, and a copy of every fixed box. Measured in visible headless Chromium (Apple M1 Max, Chromium 153): 173 ms on the BGD manual (123 pages), 960 ms on BelType's manual (280), 9 ms on the specimen; the frame held 13 418, 67 797 and 372 elements, about half of them in copies; and the first page appeared in the same frame as the last.

The note proposed that every page exist from the start as a sheet of the right size and that its content be built when it is needed, and it said the census decided how much that would buy: build every page's sheet and margin boxes with no content and time it. The census (below) came to 35 ms of the BGD manual's 173 and 140 of the manual's 960 — a seventh to a fifth — so the round went on.

## Decision

**Every page is a sheet from the start; a page's content is built when the reader comes near it.** `present()` builds each `<beltype-page>` with its size, its page context, its flow's clip and its margin boxes (the chrome: a plain per-page value the run already holds), and keeps for each page what its content is a function of — the call that cut it (`record.ctx`: the account, the intervals, the blocks, the fixed boxes, the composed records, the facts about which block is presented through a constructed first fragment and which number a piece wears). `#buildContent(record)` is the rest of the old loop, moved whole and reading only that context: the fragments are constructed, appended to the root and seated in the page's two slots, and the originals that stand whole on the page are assigned to its slot. The unbuilt page's originals stay in the parking slot, which is where the engine already kept every original no page shows (*an unplaced original is parked, not unassigned*): they have a computed style and no box.

**What builds a page.** (1) The presentation itself builds the pages in view and one viewport's height above and below, in the same frame, from rectangles read before any page is built. (2) An `IntersectionObserver` on the sheets, with a margin of a viewport above and below, builds a page at once when it comes near. (3) The doors, below. (4) Idle time: after the first view, `requestIdleCallback` (a timer where it is absent) builds the rest in slices of at most 12 ms, nearest the reader first, so find-in-page, select-all, copy and assistive technology reach every page within seconds without anything waiting for it. A run holds the idle build off while it measures and lets it go when it presents.

**Rulings, as taken.** A page once built STAYS built: there is no eviction (pdf.js evicts to free canvas memory; these pages are DOM, a few megabytes). A page that comes into view unbuilt is built at once. Find-in-page is option (b): the idle build above, and not a find bar of our own. It is on everywhere — the CLI's preview and the editing tools alike — with no host opt-in and no configuration key: the doors are the engine's own, and a host asks nothing to get them.

**After an edit.** A resumed run cuts a few pages again and keeps the rest; the pages it cut again are new sheets. Those in view are built in the presentation, as before; those out of view are unbuilt — the note called the state *stale*, and a recut page has no old content to be stale with, since the page it replaces was removed with its fragments — and are built when they come near or by the idle build. A page the call KEPT (built or not) is given the new context and its new index, since the numbers around it moved while its content did not; a kept unbuilt page is therefore built later from the call that is current, not from the one that cut it. A visible page never shows an old cut: either it was kept, and its cut is the one it showed, or it was cut again and is built in the same frame. In the composed mode the call composes only the pages it cut again; a kept unbuilt page asks the host to compose it when it is built (`composeFor`), and the composition cache makes that a run of hits.

**The doors that must build first.**
- **Jumps.** `reveal(el)` on the seam: `pageOf(el)` names the page from the Ledger without its being built, the page is built, and the copy of the element that page draws — the original where it stands whole, the fragment's counterpart where the page divides its block — is scrolled into view. An in-document `<a href="#x">` is not intercepted; the root builds the page that holds the target before the browser follows the link, so the default action (the scroll, the URL, `:target`) finds a box, and a `hashchange` handler and the first presentation of an address that carries a fragment reveal the target after it, which also reaches a target drawn only through copies. The editing tools' source-to-preview follow goes through `reveal`; their anchor builds a page before it searches it (`buildPage`).
- **Print.** `buildAll()` builds every page; the seam's is a promise, the root's is synchronous so that the `beforeprint` handler the root installs can use it. Caster's `print()` awaits it before it reads the strip; the essay's client builds all after presenting; a test or gate that reads presented pages asks first.
- **Find, select-all, copy, accessibility.** The idle build. Until it has finished, an unbuilt page's text is unreachable by all four, and that is the price of the first view.
- **Reading position.** A page's sheet has its size whether built or not, so the page-plus-offset anchor the editing tools already take is true without building anything.

**Refused.** Eviction (ruled out above). A find bar of the engine's own (pdf.js's answer; the most work, and the idle build gives the browser's find the same reach). Intercepting link clicks and navigating by hand (a modified click, a middle click and `:target` all belong to the browser; building first and revealing after loses nothing). Giving the observer its own root (the document is the root, so the margin is the iframe's own viewport in the editing tools' pane).

## Consequences

Measured 2026-10-12 in visible headless Chromium at 1400x1000 (Apple M1 Max, Chromium 153.0.8010.12), the CLI under `--engine cicero`, `.claude/plans/cicero-optimisation/bench/window-census.mjs`, the median of five runs, the old and the new code run back to back on the same machine:

| Document | | `present()` | Frame elements when the page is read | Presentation ended at (ms from navigation) | Idle build done after it |
| :-- | :-- | --: | --: | --: | --: |
| Specimen (9 pages) | before | 7.4 ms | 372 | 84 | — |
| | after | 6.8 ms | 372 (all nine pages are near) | 90 | 18 ms |
| BGD manual (123 pages) | before | 141 ms | 13 418 | 688 | — |
| | after | 34 ms | 6 070 to 8 337 (the pages built so far) | 578 | 133 ms |
| BelType's manual (280 pages) | before | 782 ms | 67 797 | 5 363 | — |
| | after | 123 ms | 34 269 | 4 686 | 344 ms |

The census of the chrome alone, taken first and decisive (every page's sheet and margin boxes, no content, on the machine as it stood for the first baseline, when the full `present()` read 173 ms on BGD and 960 on the manual): 35 ms of 173 on BGD (20 per cent), 140 of 960 on the manual (15), 7 of 9 on the specimen. At the end of the idle build the frame holds what it held before, to the element (13 418 and 67 797). The edit loop, `bench-edit.mjs` on the BGD manual's `delibera-assessorati.md` through the editing tools (eight edits, PUTs aborted, the median): keystroke to painted pages 214 and 209 ms before, 201 and 203 after; `present` within the run 9 ms before, 5 after; first load's engine time 758 and 780 ms before, 647 and 661 after; every edit still on the patch road. Every page's text, after `buildAll()`, hashes the same before and after on the specimen, the BGD manual and BelType's manual (123, 280 and 9 pages, line for line).

**What the numbers say.** `present()` is now the chrome plus a window of a few pages, and the chrome is what the census said it was: the BGD manual's 34 ms against a census of 35, the manual's 123 against 140. The gain is the content: 107 ms on BGD and 659 on the manual, which is the first page shown that much sooner, and it is paid back in idle time (133 and 344 ms after the first view, in slices of at most 12 ms) rather than saved: the work that was done in one task now happens as the browser has the time. What is not gained is the first load's pagination, which is the run and not the presentation, and the edit, where a resumed `present()` already built two or three pages.

Owed to the future: the chrome itself is now the whole of `present()` (each band's `layoutBands` is two forced layouts per page the first time a string is seen); the pages could be sheets alone with their chrome built on approach, and the observer's margin would hide it, but that is a second round with a census of its own. The progressive display of the next round (plan item C4) builds on this: a page can be built as soon as its cut exists. The frame's element count falls to the near pages' until the idle build has run, which is a measurement of the first view and not a saving after it.
