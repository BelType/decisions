# Forcing under sections earns a case

**Status: Decided 2026-09-03, Executed 2026-09-03.**

## Context

The [2026-09-02 investigation](https://github.com/egildo/beltype/blob/42b348b547d269a217a7f193042fe216386a4650/.claude/rounds/cicero/cicero-sections-report.md) measured that Cicero silently drops every forced break and every named-page switch declared anywhere but on a top-level block. The grab in `src/driver.js` is the only place in the engine that reads a forced break, and it compares consecutive feed items — one item per entry of `topLevelBlocks(doc)`. It reads each item's own computed `break-before` and its own computed `page`, so a value declared on a descendant is invisible to it. Every other path was traced and none of them catches forcing: the Stonehand reads `break-before`/`break-after` only through its AVOID set, the Gauge carries no break vocabulary at all, and presentation actively neutralizes both properties with `!important`, so nothing downstream could recover a missed break even in principle.

This was unreachable until BelType's belt made headings stop being top-level blocks. The belt articulates a document body into nested `<section>` elements, so the shape `section > h2` is now what every BelType document is, and it is precisely the shape the grab cannot see through: measured over the belt's own output, `test/specimen.md` reduces to eight top-level blocks, `docs/index.md` to sixty-six, the manual starter to seven, and `test/index.md` to two. Those documents' stylesheets then declare their chapter breaks on `h1` and `h2` — the manual starter, the docs' own `syntax.css` and `test/bleed.css` all do — so under `--engine cicero` chapters run together, the page count is wrong in the direction of too few, and nothing warns. That is a C1 violation, the mirror of the one the edge round fixed for `break-after` on 2026-07-27.

The [capability checklist's](https://github.com/egildo/beltype/blob/42b348b547d269a217a7f193042fe216386a4650/packages/engine-cicero/docs/css-paged-media-checklist.md) §3.1.1 row says the propagation rule is **built**, and names three walks: `forcedBreakAfter`, `breakAfterAvoid`, `breakBeforeAvoid`. The rule generates four. The fourth — a `forcedBreakBefore` walking a box's first in-flow block-descendant chain — was never written, because nothing needed it while every heading was its own top-level block. The venue's own scar applies exactly: *a green run must be green for a reason somebody can name*, and this cell was green because the corpus could not reach the missing half. Seeds are flat by construction — the generator emits one block per body line — so no fuzzed document in any era has ever drawn a forcing carrier below the top level, and the differential could not have raised this at any corpus size.

The order is the cycle's, and the precedent is [*the margin box leak earns a case*](the-margin-box-leak-earns-a-case.md): a fault is not fixed in the round that authored its case, because a case written after the fix only tells you the fix agrees with itself.

## Decision

**Author `cases/19-forcing-under-sections` in the venue before touching `driver.js`.** One document, three sections, three commands, and not one of them declared on a child of the body:

- `break-before: page` on a section's **first** child — the common shape, and the one §3.1.1 propagates to the container;
- `break-after: page` on a paragraph in the **middle** of a section — strictly interior to a top-level block, neither a first child nor a last, so reachable by no propagation walk at all (§4.1's class A points);
- `page: plate` on a **heading** inside a section that declares no name, with `@page plate` landscape where every other page is portrait — §4.3, where a page-name disagreement across a class A boundary is itself a forced break.

No `section` selector in the case's stylesheet carries a break property or a page name, and the stylesheet says so beside each declaration. The known-working shape — the name and the break on `section:has(> h1)`, which is what BelType's own `test/specimen.css` does — is deliberately not drawn, because it is not what an ordinary author writes.

**Refused: fixing it in this round.** The case has to be watched going red for the reasons it names before it is worth anything.

**Refused: two cases, or three.** One document is what a real stylesheet is, and all three shapes have the same cause in the grab: they go red together, and the first two go green together on the propagation walk. The third is expected to lag, and a case that stays red on one claim while the other two pass is the case doing its job rather than a case that needs splitting.

**Refused: declaring `pageSizePt`.** `cases/runner.js` checks a declared page size against every printed page, and this document has one page that is deliberately a different shape. Declaring the portrait default would make that check pass while the plate name is being dropped and fail once it is honoured — green for the defect and red for the cure. Case 10 omits it for the same reason. The geometry is claimed through `native.partition: "identical"` instead: a portrait plate page cannot divide the same words at the same places as a landscape one, so Chromium's own partition pins the switch, and pins it against the oracle rather than against our arithmetic.

## Predictions

Frozen on write, break-versus-hold decided in advance.

1. **Chromium prints all three page starts, in one document.** The investigation measured each of the three shapes separately, in isolation; this is the first time they are asked together, and a document is not the sum of three fixtures. *Breaks* if the native print disagrees with any of the three `startsAPage` claims — in which case css-break-3 is opened at the section that claim cites before the case is touched at all.
2. **Cicero fails all three `startsAPage` claims and prints fewer pages than Chromium, and the plate renders at the default geometry.** *Breaks* if any one of the three holds, which would mean a path the investigation's trace missed.
3. **No standing tally moves.** `src/` is untouched by this unit of work and `gates/PIN.json` is content-addressed on the source tree, so every seed, probe and record stands where it stood. The `cases` gate is red until the fix, by design. *Breaks* on any change in `gates/records.js` or a red `npm run pin`.
4. **The battery is not run.** Per [cycle.md](https://github.com/egildo/beltype/blob/42b348b547d269a217a7f193042fe216386a4650/packages/engine-cicero/docs/cycle.md)'s "skip it, and say so in the entry": the diff cannot reach a page — a new case directory, a chapter seating, and prose — and the one gate it changes is the one meant to go red. A run would tell us nothing we do not already know, and the fix's round is where a complete green run is the thing being claimed.

## Verdict

**Prediction 1 held, and the measurement was worth taking.** Chromium prints **eight pages**, and all three claims land: page 4 opens *Chapter two, opened from inside its section*, page 5 opens *Quis nostrum exercitationem ullam corporis suscipit laboriosam*, and page 6 opens *The plate, named from a heading inside a section* at **420.00 × 298.08 pt** where the other seven pages are 298.08 × 420.00. The folio is absent on page 1 and counts 2 to 8.

**What the native print corrected in the case's own design, before Cicero ever saw it.** The plate page carries the heading and nothing else, and the flow returns to the default page immediately after it. `page` is not an inherited property, so the name reaches the heading and stops — the boundary is really two forced breaks, into the plate and straight back out. That was not anticipated when the document was drafted, and the first draft's closing paragraph claimed *this section begins a page at a different geometry*, which is false about the section and true only about its heading. The prose was corrected to what was measured rather than the measurement explained away, and the README now states it outright, because a landscape page holding one heading reads as a defect until somebody says it is not. It also exhibits §4.3's *a force beats an avoid* for free: the heading carries `break-after: avoid` and is separated from its own body anyway.

**Prediction 2 held in full.** Cicero presents and prints **six pages** against Chromium's eight, in both the native and the composed mode. All three `startsAPage` checks fail in both modes — none of the three strings begins any page — and the partition diverges from page 3. Every one of the six pages measures **298.08 × 420.00 pt**: the plate geometry appears nowhere, which was measured rather than inferred from the page count. Nine checks fail; `no page errors`, `folios` and `every word printed once, in order` all pass, which is the finding stated precisely — the Ledger is internally consistent, loses no content, and is wrong about all three of the author's commands.

**Prediction 3 held.** `npm run pin` reports the engine source byte-for-byte identical to what the records were taken against; `gates/records.js` is untouched; the venue's own suite closes 78/78 with the new case validated from disk and seated in the `commands` chapter.

**Prediction 4 held**, as a decision rather than an outcome: the battery was not run, and this is the entry saying so.

**Too good to be true, checked.** The three claims failing together could mean one cause or three, and a case that fails for a reason it does not name is worth nothing. The three are separable on the evidence already in hand: the page count is short by exactly two, and the document commands exactly three breaks of which Cicero honours none — so the missing pages are the first two commands, while the third costs no page of its own because the plate boundary's two breaks would have added the plate page and its return. The geometry measurement separates the third claim from the other two independently: a dropped `break-before` cannot explain six portrait pages where one should be landscape. And the `every word printed once, in order` pass rules out the alternative reading that content is being lost rather than mis-divided.

## Consequences

The venue gains a twentieth case, `cases/19-forcing-under-sections`, seated in the book's **Commands** chapter beside `06-forced-breaks`, and `cases/README.md`'s table — stale by one, missing `18-relative-page-margins` — gains both rows. The `cases` gate is **red until the fix**, which is what the case is for.

`src/` is untouched and stays that way until the next entry. The checklist's §3.1.1 row no longer says **built**: it names three walks built and the fourth missing, and adds what neither the row nor the rule covers — that a forced break interior to a top-level block is reachable by no propagation walk at all. The §4 break-properties row gains the 2026-09-02 correction beside the 2026-07-27 one: same fault, other half, found this time by reading BelType's own output rather than by the corpus. The design opens gain the corpus's flatness as a named v5 dimension owed, deferred to a battery-run round exactly as `forcing-both-spellings-drawn` already is, and `lib/chapters.js`'s `commands` corpus note says the same thing where the book will show it.

Two record corrections the same investigation produced, folded in here because they are corrections and not code. [`.claude/NEEDED-FIXES.md`](https://github.com/egildo/beltype/blob/42b348b547d269a217a7f193042fe216386a4650/.claude/NEEDED-FIXES.md) §3.1b is **falsified**: 205 is `test/specimen.md`'s complete and correct flow word count, the driver's console line was telling the truth, and the entry's own "how you would know" has been carried out with the answer that no body child is absent. The page-count half survives with a different suspect — four `break-before: recto` chapters against unbuilt parity forcing, 7 + 4 = 11 — stated as a lead and not a finding, and one probe would settle it. And a second entry records a content-loss defect measured in passing: the word space joins across whitespace-free element seams, so a cut landing exactly on such a seam consumes a word belonging to the next element, with a two-line reproducer.

The fix is the next branch and it is two halves of different sizes. The propagation walk is small and known: a `forcedBreakBefore(win, el)` in [`driver.js`](https://github.com/egildo/beltype/blob/42b348b547d269a217a7f193042fe216386a4650/packages/engine-cicero/src/driver.js) mirroring `forcedBreakAfter`, walking the first in-flow block-descendant chain and skipping out-of-flow children as `breakBeforeAvoid` already does, with `nameOf` following the same descent because forcing and the named-page switch are one mechanism in the grab and should stay one. That closes claims one and three. Claim two is not a patch: a command interior to a feed item is reachable by no propagation rule, and whether the grab descends into a top-level item's interior class A boundaries or the feed stops being the granularity of forcing is a design question for a round with Egildo. If the two land separately the case stays red on that claim alone, which is the case working.
