# The hyphen is written at the cut

**Status: Decided 2026-10-06, Executed 2026-10-06.**

## Context

`packages/essay/cases/83-the-hyphen-across`, authored red on 2026-10-05 and left unfixed in the round that wrote it, puts one question: a word with a soft hyphen stands where a page's last line has room for its first half and its hyphen and not for the whole, so the browser breaks the line at the soft hyphen and draws the hyphen there — **does the page end on that line, with the hyphen, and the next page open on the rest of the word?** Chromium's print says yes (page one's last line `p0w022 abcdefgh-` ends at 108.9pt, page two opens on `ijklmnop`), and so does Vivliostyle 2.45.2 on the same document. Both Cicero modes cut at the right height, between the twelfth and thirteenth lines, and name the cut by the whole word: the first mode ends page one on `p0w022` alone and sets the whole word on page two; the composed mode opens page two on the word's first half and its hyphen alone on a line. The finding, with its arithmetic, is the case's README and the checklist's entry *a word set on several lines has no break point between its lines*; this record does not restate it.

**What the rivals do.** Vivliostyle owns the cut and so writes the hyphen into the text at it: `TextNodeBreaker` (`layout.ts`) splits the text node at the chosen character, and `breakAfterSoftHyphen` replaces a trailing soft hyphen with the hyphenate character (a joiner first, where the preceding letter joins cursively — Arabic, Mongolian, N'Ko and the rest), while `breakAfterOtherCharacter` adds one where the browser hyphenated between two letters (both neighbours letters and the break not a word break); `resolveHyphenateCharacter` falls back to `-`. WeasyPrint owns its lines, so the question never arises as a text edit: `text/line_break.py` decides a hyphenation while it breaks, the first line carries `first_line_text + hyphenate_character` (a manual hyphenation checks the line ends on the soft hyphen and adds the missing character), and `resume_at` names where the next line begins, so the hyphen stays on the line it was drawn on by construction. Cicero sits with Vivliostyle: its pages are the browser's lines, and what the browser drew at a line's end it does not draw at the end of a fragment — a trailing soft hyphen at a block's end is invisible — so the fragment must write it.

**What the code already holds, measured on 2026-10-06 on a scratch page.** The address can already carry the character (*a line inside a word is a break point* minted `char`), and `fragments.js` already consumes to a word and a character at BOTH ends. What refused was one predicate, `word-space.js`' `mayBeHyphenated`: a break after U+00AD, or between two letters under `hyphens: auto`, opened no piece, so the Gauge homed the word on its last line. The browser leaves its drawing in the range rects — at a soft-hyphen break the soft hyphen's range has an inked rect on the line it ends, and the first character of the next line has a second, equal rect on that same previous line; at an `auto` break the same; at an `overflow-wrap: anywhere` or `break-all` break neither — which says that *whether the browser drew one* is readable, and that an address needs only a source character to say where. The composed mode is the other half: its Compositor writes `abcdefgh`, a U+2010 text node, its own `<br>`, then `­ijklmnop` (its offset is the soft hyphen's own, `candidatesFor`), and the Gauge refuses to split a box carrying its breaks, so a cut inside such a word has no address.

## Decision

**One — the soft hyphen is a break the fragment can write, so it stops being refused.** `mayBeHyphenated` becomes `mayBeAutoHyphenated`: a break is refused only where the browser may have drawn a hyphen the SOURCE does not carry — `hyphens: auto` between two letters of a script that hyphenates. A break after U+00AD is minted as a piece like any other, at the character after the soft hyphen: Chromium's own line two begins at `i`, and the address says so, in the source's own coordinates.

**Two — the first fragment ends with the hyphen, written where the cut falls.** `consumeTrailingWords`, which already keeps the first `char` characters of a word, replaces a trailing soft hyphen of the kept part by the hyphenation character — the author's `hyphenate-character` read from the source box, else U+2010 (css-text-4 §6.3.2, the reading `compose-dom.js`' `hyphenCharOf` already makes, exported and shared) — and an empty one removes it. A replacement and not an addition, so the text the fragment shows is the source's own letters with the one character the browser drew in place of the one it hid, and a word keeps the same length. The continuation is the existing consumption: its first `char` characters, the soft hyphen included, are blanked.

**Three — the composed mode's break is the Compositor's own, named in its own coordinates.** The Gauge now splits a word of a hinted box where the Compositor's `<br>` stands inside it, at the character its record gave: the source character the break precedes, which is the `char` the Compositor wrote it at. This asks the word space one thing it did not say: **text the engine wrote is not in the source's word space.** The hyphen character the Compositor materializes is registered when it is written (`word-space.js`, `noteEngineText`, a registry beside `noteSource` that changes nothing in the DOM), and the clone-side tokenization and the Gauge's count carry it as a part of the word that holds no characters — so a character offset means the same text in a hinted clone and in its source, a whole-word consumption still takes the hyphen with its word, and a cut at the Compositor's break keeps the hyphen on the page it ends. That is a change to what the engine registers, not to how the Compositor materializes its division: the DOM it writes is byte for byte the one it wrote.

**Refused: the address carrying a `hyphen` mark.** It would let the Gauge's measured fact travel with the cut and close the `auto` half too, and it widens the wire form of the address — `{ path, word, char, px }` — which is a design decision about what the address is. A soft hyphen needs none: the source says where it is. What the address names for `auto` is deferred, with the measurement above, to its own round.

**Refused: writing the hyphen by keeping the dropped half in a hidden element.** It would let the browser draw its own hyphen at a break it still makes, and it is the clipped overflow the engine's whole construction exists to leave behind (*the first fragment is constructed*).

**Refused: moving the Compositor's break after the soft hyphen** (`op.char + 1`), which would make both modes' addresses equal. It changes what the Compositor materializes; the soft hyphen sits after the `<br>` in its output and is invisible there, which is why the two modes' characters differ by one — the native mode's line begins after the soft hyphen, the Compositor's before it, and each fragment consumes by its own.

**Where the law stands.** I2 reads *a character, where a line box begins inside that word at a break that drew nothing*. After this decision a character is also minted where the break drew a hyphen. The reason the law gives — the line box is the atom, and a word may span several — covers it, but the clause *that drew nothing* describes a case no longer the only one. **That wording is the tablet's, and this round does not change the tablet**; the proposal to Egildo is in the report that closes the round: drop the clause, or say *at a break whose hyphen, if any, is the fragment's to write*. **Ruled 2026-10-06, Egildo: the clause is dropped**; the law now reads *a character, where a line box begins inside that word*, with the amendment noted beside it.
