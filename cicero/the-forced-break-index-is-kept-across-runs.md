# The forced-break index is kept across runs

**Status: Decided 2026-10-09, Executed 2026-10-09.** Amended 2026-10-25 under Consequences: the reach is read for a unit of replacement. Decided in the `cicero-optimisation` round (item 1.3), on the survey `.claude/prior-art/reports/incremental-indexes-2026-10-08.md` and a measurement of the editing loop; it amends the last sentence of *the resumable run*'s architecture section (*what stays whole*), and changes no cut.

## Context

After *a resumed run starts from the numbers it left*, a resumed edit on the BGD manual (123 pages, one paragraph, two pages cut again) cost about 80 ms of engine time, of which `cicero:forcing-index` was 25 ms and `cicero:rewrite` 7 ms, and more on a slower machine (47 and 6–68 in the survey's sitting). Both are rebuilt whole on every run, resumed or not. The forced-break index (`forcingIndexOf`) walks every element once and asks the browser the same few things of each; the stylesheet rewrite (`rewriteDocumentSheets`) reads every `<link>` over the wire again, one after another, to rewrite sheets whose text cannot have changed. Neither depends on anything an edit touches except one box.

A gap beside them: the resume gate's conditions key (`conditionsKey` in `seam/client.js`) walked `document.styleSheets`, which holds no adopted sheet, and the editing tools dress the pane by adopting the author's whole stylesheet. The gate did not see those sheets; only the host's reload contract protected it.

## Decision

**The index keeps its reads, not its structure.** A `ForcingMemo` (`src/forcing-memo.js`) holds `WeakMap`s keyed by the element: nine computed style values copied into a plain object, the effective page name, the block-children list and the element's in-flow child index. The seam keeps one beside `previous` and offers it to `paginate`; the driver wraps the reads it already injects and `forcingIndexOf` is the same code. The flat arrays, the positions and the normalisation of the address are rebuilt every run from the new position index (about a millisecond). Nothing is recorded dynamically, as comemo does: validating a recorded `getComputedStyle` read costs the read, which is what recomputation costs.

**What an edit makes stale is stated once, as a scan of the sheets.** `sheetReach` (`src/capture.js`, beside `sheetsUseHas`) reads every selector, every run: 0 where no selector reaches beyond a box's ancestors; 1 where one reaches a sibling (`+`, `~`, `:nth-child(… of …)`; arguments of `:nth-*` and attribute selectors are stripped first, so `2n+1` and `[lang~=en]` are not combinators); 2 where one reaches an ancestor or anything at all (`:has(`, an `@container` rule, a sheet the engine cannot read). The memo learns which boxes were replaced from `patchBox`, the one door through which the seam replaces an element, and forgets at the start of the next run: at 0, the block-children list of every ancestor of the new box and the child indices of its siblings; at 1, the whole subtree of the box's parent besides; at 2 the memo is emptied and refused for the run, the console says so once, and the reads are the browser's, as before. A box that replaced another is a new element, so it and its subtree miss by construction. This is Blink's invalidation statement made once, with Blink's fallback.

**The memo is born empty and filled only on a presented frame.** The first call after load is offered no memo and fills none: its realm differs from the later ones' (the sources are children of `body`, not of `<beltype-root>`) and a `body > …` or `:first-child` selector could answer otherwise. A call that does not resume empties it and fills it afresh; so does a changed condition and a thrown run. The first edit after each load therefore pays the full walk, once.

**The rewrite stops re-judging a sheet.** `rewriteDocumentSheets(doc, judged)` takes a memo the caller owns: node to the text it was judged against. A `<style>` is skipped when its text equals the remembered one (a `<style>` edited in place is still judged); a `<link>` is skipped, without a fetch, once it has been. The rewrite is a pure function of one sheet's text and a frame's sheet text cannot change without a reload; skipping the re-fetch also makes the engine agree with the page, which wears the text it loaded. The seam replaces the memo on the event that clears the composition cache, a change of conditions. The links of a call that has any to judge are fetched together and applied in document order.

**The conditions key reads the adopted sheets**, with the engine's own two (the dressing sheet, the delivery sheet) left out as litter, and names an adopted sheet as such when it says which part moved.

Refused: a cache per top-level block (the BGD manual is one block, 2 275 elements); splicing the event array and patching `end`, `closing`, `nextForced` and `nextName` by hand (the arrays are integer arithmetic and a splice is where an off-by-one would live); a text-keyed rewrite cache across sessions (IndexedDB was dropped, and the rewrite itself is 4.6 ms over 64 KB); skipping the memo whenever any sibling combinator appears (BGD's own `dl + p figure` would have switched it off on the document it was built for).

## Consequences

Measured on the BGD manual in headless Chromium at a visible page, on the worktree editor, edits to different paragraphs of `delibera-assessorati.md` (the buffer restored after, nothing saved): the resumed run's `run.forcing-index` median **24 ms to 6**, `run.rewrite` **7 to 0**, `run` **80 to 57 ms** over twelve resumed edits per arm (the "before" arm empties both memos before each edit through the harness door). Every cut the same: `pageOf` of the 1 057 stamped originals and the whole Ledger (class, position, address, used height, cost, page number, parity, name and counters of all 123 pages) equal to a fresh run after the same edit, six edits, and equal to the baseline after the buffer was restored. An inserted heading with `break-before: page` under a warm memo (a scratch document outside the repository) forces its break exactly where a fresh run puts it, and removing then restoring its class moves it to the page before and back, each equal to a fresh run, with 693 of 702 reads answered from the memo; the same under a sheet with `h3 + p` and `.pb + p` rules (reach 1) and under one with `:has(` (reach 2, memo refused, console says so once), all equal to a fresh run.

Owed to the host: any code that replaces an element another way than `patchBox` must call `forcingMemo.replaced(el)` or ask for a fresh memo. The memo leans on the patch contract (the replaced box's ancestors keep their name, attributes and child count); a planner that resumes with an ancestor's attributes changed would be wrong silently, and `settle` could be made to forget that ancestor's facts then. `ATOM_OF` and `FIXED` in `src/word-space.js` are older caches of the same shape (per source element, never invalidated) and leave a reach-1 sheet unaccounted for in the same way; they are the next thing to look at if an atomic inline's display is ever set by a sibling selector. A reach-1 sheet and an edit to a top-level block forgets the root's whole subtree, the presented copies included, which is correct and slow. Nothing in the tablet moves.

**Amended 2026-10-25: the reach is a statement about the sheets and the unit of replacement** (the round `round/reach-for-a-whole-box`, on `prosemirror-beltype`'s finding and Egildo's ruling the same day). `sheetReach` answers for `patchBox`'s unit, a box replaced at any depth with its ancestors kept. A host that holds its own pages replaces only whole top-level boxes and reads each one again whole, so the one question left is whether another top-level box's style can move, and it can only through a selector relating top-level boxes or matching their ancestors. `topLevelReach(doc, boxes, before)` (`src/capture.js`) answers it, and `ForcingMemo.settle(reach, boxes)` forgets accordingly, keeping the boxes it was settled with as the next call's `before`. The rule: 0 where nothing crosses, and only the replaced and moved boxes are read again; 1 where a box's style can depend on the boxes before it, and every box from the earliest change on (the first place the order differs, or a replaced box's place if earlier) is read again: a `+` or `~` whose left side can be a top-level box, `:nth-child(… of …)` on one, and, given a change of shape, `:first-child` and `:nth-child()` on one (`:first-of-type` and `:nth-of-type` given any change); 2 where it can depend on the boxes after it or on an ancestor, and the memo is emptied and refused: a `:has()` whose subject can be an ancestor of the boxes, a `:has()` opening with `+` or `~` whose subject can be a top-level box, `:nth-last-child(… of …)` and the last and only structural forms on one, `@container`, `@scope`, an unreadable sheet, and whatever the reading cannot place. The brief had a sideways `:has()` staling the boxes after; it stales the boxes before, since its subject depends on what follows it, so it is answered 2. Whether a compound can be a top-level box is asked of the document: the selector up to it with its pseudo-classes taken out (but a `:is()`, `:where()` or `:not()` of plain compounds, kept since 2026-10-28, CHANGELOG), matched against each box, and the compound alone against each box an edit took out. A nested rule reads as CSS Nesting defines it, its `&` the parent's selector in `:is()`. `settle(reach)` without boxes is unchanged, and the battery was 26 of 26 with the pin standing. Measured 2026-10-25 in the pinned Chromium, the reading per call (200 calls, five runs): BelType's showcase (`test/index.md`, 10 boxes, 471 rules) 0, 0.16 ms, against 0.30 for `sheetReach`; the specimen (14 boxes, 154 rules) 0 where `sheetReach` reads 2 for `section:has(> h1)`, 0.09 ms; the studio's showcase served by the CLI (196 boxes, 208 rules) 0 where `sheetReach` reads 2, 0.22 ms; the manual (326 boxes, 8 701 rules) 0, 3.0 to 3.2 ms, of which 2.5 is the walk over the rules alone and none a selector query, against 6.1 to 6.9 for `sheetReach`.
