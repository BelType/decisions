# The first fragment is constructed

**Status: Decided 2026-09-15, Executed 2026-09-15.** Egildo ruled for all three of this record's recommendations — construct the first fragment, deliver the extension as bottom padding from the account's `cost`, defer the shell arithmetic to its own round — and the Verdict at the end closes every prediction with the measured numbers.

## Context

Three rules of css-break-3 ended the look-of-a-fragment round at the same sentence. §5.3's extension of a divided box to the fragmentainer's edge ([*the ink stops at the fragmentainer*](the-ink-stops-at-the-fragmentainer.md)), §5.4's cloned block-end decoration on the first fragment ([*the fragment that wraps itself*](the-fragment-that-wraps-itself.md)) and §5.5's per-fragment rotation origin ([*fragmentation comes first*](fragmentation-comes-first.md)) each want a property of the first fragment of a straddling box that is not simply *less content* — and the first fragment of a straddling box is not a fragment at all. It is the ORIGINAL, assigned to the page where it begins and clipped by the flow at the cut's own extent: the architecture's slot-versus-fragment reconciliation, *"the original straddling box is assigned to the Chase where it begins; the flow clips its tail"*.

The round measured what that costs. A clip cannot deliver the extension, because the straddling box's ink and its own overflow content are one subtree: a clip at the page area admits the next page's first line along with the background, and four sound documents lost words to it, case 07 printing 683 of 700. A separately painted strip would be a second rendering engine. A per-page rule cannot reach an original's children above the box's own padding edge. What is left is the one thing the continuation already has: **a clone built by `fragments.js`**.

`cases/42-clone-decoration` and `cases/43-the-ink-at-the-edge` stand red on one page-one ink claim each — by 19.5pt and by 4.5pt — and P34g records the rotation origins of the two engines about two points apart on case 45. The battery closed at 24 of 25 gates on that account, and the pin was deliberately not rewritten.

## The question

Whether the first fragment of a straddling box should be constructed — a clone cut at the boundary, as the continuation is a clone resumed at it — and what that costs the architecture's contract that originals are what pages show.

## The shape, read off the code before it is proposed

Three facts make this smaller than the withdrawn records feared.

**The construction is the dual of one that exists.** `mutateToContinuation` consumes the first `n` words of a clone in the one word space, removes the elements left wordless before the resume point, and dresses the resume chain (`truncateLeadingDecoration`); its element-boundary form is the shell path. The first fragment is the same three moves mirrored: consume the words FROM `n`, remove the elements standing wholly after the boundary, dress the chain the cut passes through with `truncateTrailingDecoration` — which already exists, written for the measure feed's truncated clone, and already splits on `clonesDecoration`. `truncateFrom` is that form for an element boundary and already exists too. Both consumers of `fragments.js` stay one implementation.

**The reading copy already moves to a clone for two reasons, and this is a third.** `root.js#present` assigns a clone rather than the original for a composed block and for a block holding a nested fixed box, with the original left in the light DOM unassigned and unrendered. A block the page's closing cut divides joins that branch. So the reversal is not of a law — I6 says content lives once and pages are views, and a clone is a decision product under C5 exactly as every continuation is — but of one sentence of the architecture, and of the cost note beneath it.

**The extension is one number the account nearly carries.** §5.3 extends the divided box's content box to the fragmentainer, *leaving room* for cloned decoration. The Stonehand already prices that room on the winning candidate as `cost`, the sum of every broken ancestor's cloned bottom border and padding; the account carries `usedPx`, the cut's own extent; the page's conditions carry `contentH`. The first fragment's extension is `contentH − usedPx − cost`, clamped at zero, and it lands as **bottom padding on the innermost box the cut divided** — under `slice` the padding `truncateTrailingDecoration` zeroed becomes exactly the extension, under `clone` the box's own padding plus it. Every ancestor grows by wrapping its child; under `clone` each keeps its cloned bottom border and padding and the sum closes at the page area's edge by the fit's own arithmetic; under `slice` the ancestors' bottoms are zero and the sum closes at the same edge. No wrapper enters the author's tree, no element is measured at present time, and no fact is derived twice: `cost` becomes one more view literal on the account, beside `usedPx` and `leadPx`, exactly as the 2026-08-04 account ruling wants it.

## Decision

**One — a block the page's closing cut divides is presented on that page through its first fragment, a clone.** `pageIntervals` already gives each page its `end` literal — the block, the word, the shell path — and `present` reads it the way it reads `start` for the continuation: when the end falls inside the last block of the interval, that block is assigned as `firstFragmentClone(source, { endWord, shellPath, composition })` rather than as the original. The clone carries `data-beltype-fragment` and a class of its own, `__beltype-first`, and it is NOT `aria-hidden`: it is the reading copy. The original stays in the light DOM, unassigned, canonical.

**Two — the continuation of a constructed first fragment is read too.** The continuation's `aria-hidden` exists because the original it continues is already read whole on the page before. When the page before shows a first fragment, nothing reads the tail unless the continuation does, so on that page the continuation loses the attribute. Assistive technology then reads a divided paragraph as two, in order, once — the cost composed mode's note already owns — and find-in-page lands on the page a word is actually on, which the old cost note recorded as a defect and which this closes for free.

**Three — the flow clips at the page area's edge on a page whose closing cut divided a block, and at the cut otherwise.** This is the withdrawn decision of *the ink stops at the fragmentainer*, and it is safe now for the reason it was not then: nothing of the divided block lies below the fragment's extended bottom, because the words after the cut are not in the clone. A Class A cut between two top-level blocks keeps clipping at the cut, since nothing this page owns lies below it and the reason to move the clip is the extension alone. **On a page a parallel flow crosses, the clip stays at the cut and no extension is delivered**: the carried float is still an original with its tail under the clip, and moving the clip would show that tail. Stated as a limit, cased by nothing today, and the symmetric construction for a carried flow is the round after this one if it is wanted.

**Four — the first fragment is dressed as a beginning that does not end.** Its `::before` and `::first-letter` stand, since it IS the box's start; its `::after` is suppressed by the dressing sheet as the continuation's `::before` is, since the box does not end here. And a justified block's cut line stays justified: a line before a fragmentation break is not the block's last line (css-text-3 §7.2), but in the clone it is, so the innermost divided box gets `text-align-last: justify` when its source computes `text-align: justify` — the Compositor's own move in `compose-dom.js`, applied here through `word-space.js`'s source map, since a detached clone has no computed style to ask.

**Five — the construction composes with the two clone reasons that exist.** A composed block's first fragment applies its records before the trailing consumption, as the continuation does; a block holding a fixed box strips it first, as `continuationClone` does. One function, `firstFragmentClone`, mirrors `continuationClone` step for step.

**Refused: a wrapper around the box's children.** The withdrawn record proposed *"a clone whose children are clipped at the cut by a wrapper and whose own border box is extended to the fragmentainer"*. The clip is unnecessary once the words after the cut are gone from the clone, and the wrapper would change `>` and `:nth-child` matching for every child of every divided block on every page — a structural cost the padding delivery does not incur at all.

**Refused: extending by stretching the flow.** Making `.flow` a column flex container with its last item stretched would reach the edge with no arithmetic and no literal, and would change the formatting context every block is measured in — a break decision is valid only under render-identical conditions, and this is not one.

**Refused: measuring the clone at present time.** Reading the divided box's bottom off the slotted clone and setting a height from it is a second layout pass and a fact derived at present time from a tree the driver never measured. The account carries the cut; the account carries the cost; the extension is their difference.

**Refused, and deferred with its reason: §5.3's other half, the shell arithmetic.** A Class C cut in a container's end gap continues on the next page as a blank shell of `bottom − cut`; under §5.3 the container's content box extended to the edge on the page before, so the shell owed is `bottom − edge`. With this round's presentation alone, a container divided in its end gap will show its extension on page one AND its full end gap as a shell on page two — taller than the specification by the extension, where today it is shorter by the same amount on page one. The correction is one subtraction in the driver's Class C branch, and it MOVES CUTS: the corpus draws `.outer` containers with padding and border, so it is a re-adjudication of standing numbers rather than a presentation change, and it gets its own round with its own predictions. Named on the checklist's §5.3 row as the arithmetic half, as it already is.

**Refused: §5.3's second sentence.** The extension's contribution to `height`/`max-height` limits stays unmodelled, as the withdrawn record left it.

**Out of scope, named:** a carried parallel flow's first fragment (decision Three's limit); §5.5's anti-clipping sentence, which a rectangular flow clip cannot honour for a rotated fragment; §5.4.1's composite-box backgrounds, which are a different construction.

## Consequences

**Caster pairs a continuation with a source it recognises by the absence of the fragment attribute**, then verifies the pairing by the continuation's words being a suffix of the source's (`packages/caster/src/adapters/cicero.js`, R4). Under this decision the earlier page shows a first fragment that carries the attribute and whose words are a PREFIX of the original's, so both the rule and the verification fail on every divided block and the printer refuses the ordinary document. The adapter's rule becomes *a source is a first fragment or an original — anything in the flow that is neither a continuation nor a fixed clone*, and the verification becomes *first fragment plus continuations partition the original*, the original being reachable in the root's light DOM by the belt's `data-beltype-pos` stamp where the host asked for one and by tag adjacency where it did not. That is a numbered entry in Caster's `DESIGN.md`, its acceptance test is the gate, and it lands in this round because the printer must not be red on `main` for a day.

**The architecture's slot-versus-fragment section** rewrites its straddler sentence and its cost note: a straddler's tail no longer exists twice in the render tree; the continuation is hidden from readers only where an original is read whole; find-in-page lands where the word is; the structural-pseudo cost — a clone matching `:nth-child` and `:last-child` against its cloned position — now touches every divided block's first page as well as its continuation, and stays a stated cost until a case bites.

**The checklist's** §5.3, §5.4 and §5.5 rows and the design-opens index move from *design open* to *built, presentation half*, with the shell arithmetic and the parallel-flow limit named where the open was.

## Rulings asked

1. **Construct the first fragment, as decision One through Five describe** — or keep the original under the clip and park the three rules as wants. The record recommends constructing: the machinery is the continuation's own, and three rules of the specification wait on nothing else.
2. **Deliver the extension as bottom padding on the innermost divided box, from the account's `cost`** — or carry per-box tops and set heights. The record recommends padding: one literal, no wrapper, no measurement at present time. The visible difference is confined to `background-clip: content-box` and `background-origin`, and is stated as a limit.
3. **Defer the shell arithmetic to its own round** — or take it here as a second commit with its own battery run. The record recommends deferring: it moves cuts in the corpus, and a round that moves cuts and changes presentation cannot attribute what the battery says.

### What was too good to be true

Nothing in the arithmetic. The extension is three account literals and a subtraction, and it landed on the first run; the two cases the round was named for went green together, and the probe that reads the same documents without claims agreed. What was too good to be true was the SCOPE — the record's Consequences section reads as though one adapter rule stood between this construction and a printer that goes on working, and the printer's whole ink model was anchored on the original box being on a page. That is the round's one real surprise, and it is recorded where a reader of Caster will meet it rather than only here.
