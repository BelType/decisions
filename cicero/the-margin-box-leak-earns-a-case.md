# The margin-box leak earns a case

**Status: Decided 2026-08-25, Executed 2026-08-25.**

## Context

The capture fault measured on 2026-08-25 — recorded on the [capability checklist's](https://github.com/egildo/beltype/blob/42b348b547d269a217a7f193042fe216386a4650/packages/engine-cicero/docs/css-paged-media-checklist.md) design opens, with its arithmetic — is that `readGeometryTable` matches `/size:/` and `/margin-<side>:/` by regex against a `@page` rule's whole `cssText`, which the CSSOM serializes with the nested margin-box blocks inside it. The instrument that measured it is `src/probe-geometry.js` in the ZENTE manualistica, a two-column reporter that reads the same CSSOM through `capture.js` and through each rule's own declaration block and prints both beside the declaration responsible for any difference.

Three things that reading settled and this entry inherits rather than re-argues. The mechanism is the **longhand** regexes, not the shorthand: Chromium serializes a page rule's four margin longhands back out as a shorthand, so `marginShort` reads correctly and `longhand(side)` then overwrites it with whatever a margin box declares. The `size` fault is **order-dependent luck**: `/size:/` matches the tail of `font-size:`, and bites only where no earlier `size:` occurs in the same rule — which is exactly the difference between a venue whose bare `@page` opens with a size (correct page box, wrong margins) and one whose conditional variant declares none (a 12 × 12 px page). And Chromium is **unambiguous** about whose margin it is: in that venue's own PDF the body's ink begins at the declared margin to the pixel while the folio's ink sits 32–38 px outside the content box, which is css-page-3 §5 behaving exactly as written.

The fix is known and clean — `rule.style` exposes the `size` descriptor and separates margin boxes into `rule.cssRules` — and by this venue's cycle it is owed a case first. **No seed and no case covers this shape**, which is the finding underneath the finding: seeds are generated by our generator and cases are hand-authored by us, and neither family had ever put a `font-size` inside a margin box. Every real document does.

## Decision

**Author `cases/15-the-margin-box` in the battleground before touching `capture.js`.** It carries the shape the corpus cannot generate, in the two forms the fault actually takes in the wild, in one document because one document is what a real stylesheet is:

- a **base** `@page` whose folio box declares `content`, a `font-size` **and its own margin** — the ZENTE shape, where `size` survives by declaration order and the page's margin does not;
- a **conditional** `@page :left` whose running-head box declares a `font-size` and no `size` of its own — the `test/book.md` shape, where the page box itself is destroyed.

What it claims: `pageSizePt` pins the page box against the `size` leak, and `pages` plus `native.partition: identical` pin the content box against the margin leak, since a content box wrong by tens of pixels cannot divide the same words at the same places. Both are ordinary keys the runner already reads — this case needs no new vocabulary, which is the test of whether it belongs in this family at all.

Refused: **asserting `contentW` directly**. The venue's ground truth is a printed page's word partition, and adding a geometry assertion to `expect.json` would introduce a check whose oracle is our own arithmetic rather than Chromium's paper. The page count is the honest proxy, and it is honest precisely because the fault is large.

Also refused: **fixing `capture.js` in this unit of work.** The case has to be seen to fail for the reason it names before it is worth anything, and a case authored and fixed in one motion is a case nobody ever watched go red.

## Predictions

Frozen on write, break-versus-hold decided in advance.

1. **Cicero will THROW on this case rather than fail a check.** The `:left` size leak yields an 8 pt page whose content box is negative once the declared margins are subtracted; nothing can be placed, the cursor cannot advance, and C2 has no progress to report. *Breaks* if the run instead produces a finite, merely-wrong page count.
2. **The runner will abort the whole battery when it does.** `cases/runner.js` wraps each case in `try/finally` for the server alone — there is no `catch` — so an exception inside `printLiveThroughCicero` propagates out of the loop. This is a prediction about the instrument, and if it holds the instrument is what gets fixed first: a case that crashes the battery cannot be watched going red. *Breaks* if the remaining cases still run.
3. **Chromium prints the document unaffected**, because a margin-box declaration stays in its margin box; the native page count will be the one the geometry's arithmetic predicts, and the running head and folio will both appear in their bands. *Breaks* if the native print's page count needs the case's design to be revised.
4. **No standing tally moves.** `src/` is untouched by this unit of work, and `gates/PIN.json` is content-addressed on the source tree, so every other case, seed and probe stands exactly where it stood. *Breaks* on any change in `gates/records.js`.

## Verdict

**Prediction 1 broke, and it is the one worth having been wrong about.** Cicero does not throw on an eight-point page. It paginates it — twenty-three intervals presented, twenty-nine pages printed, every band full of fragments, and `no page errors` reported green. The reasoning behind the prediction was that a negative content box leaves nothing placeable, so the cursor cannot advance and C2 has no progress to report. What actually happens is that the walk advances anyway, one scrap per page, indefinitely. **That is a fact about the engine and not about this case:** an absurd geometry degrades to garbage rather than to a refusal, and the one law that exists to notice a walk going nowhere does not fire, because the walk is not going nowhere — it is going somewhere useless. Filed to the checklist's frontier as its own line; it is not this unit of work's to fix, and it would have gone unnoticed for as long as nothing fed the engine an absurd geometry.

**Prediction 2 was never tested, and says so.** It was conditional on the throw that did not happen. The reading it rests on still stands — `cases/runner.js` wraps each case in `try/finally` for the server alone, with no `catch`, so an exception inside `printLiveThroughCicero` would still take the battery down — but no run has demonstrated it and none was manufactured to. An untested prediction recorded as held would be exactly the "green for a reason nobody can name" this venue keeps paying for.

**Prediction 3 held, and was measured rather than assumed.** Chromium prints six pages at 340.08 × 481.92 pt, the running head in the top band of all six and the folio counting 1 to 6 in the foot band. The figure that carries the case: the body's ink begins at **x = 45.00 pt on every page, left and right alike** — the declared 16 mm — while the verso's running head carries `margin-right: 4mm`. The margin box moves; the text block does not.

**Prediction 4 held, and the pin proved it in a second rather than in sixteen minutes.** `npm run pin` reports the engine source byte-for-byte identical to what the records were taken against (`src def811a`) though the repo has moved from `0f2e7ba` to `023e88d` — which is the content-addressed pin earning its design: the beta-seam round added `seam/` and touched nothing under `src/`, and no tally can have moved. The full battery is deliberately **not** run here; it belongs to the fix's unit of work, where a complete green run is the thing being claimed.

**Too good to be true, checked.** The case failing eleven checks proves nothing on its own — a case can fail for a reason it does not name. So the two faults were separated by hand: with the `font-size` leak removed from both variants, the geometry check goes **green** and the case still fails at **five pages against Chromium's six**, diverging from page one, because the folio's six millimetres are still standing in for the page's twenty. Both halves are pinned independently, and the case cannot go green on half a fix.

**One thing the venue asked for that the plan had not anticipated:** `test/book.test.js` asserts every case on disk belongs to exactly one book chapter, so authoring a case is also a book act. It is seated in **the dressed page**, whose `wanted` list already carries `margin-box-type` — "a folio set in its own face and size, margin-box font descriptors applied, not merely captured". That question turns out to have had a sharper neighbour all along: the descriptors are not merely uncaptured, they are captured and applied **to the wrong box**.

## Consequences

The battleground gains a fifteenth case, `cases/15-the-margin-box` (venue branch `case-the-margin-box`, commit `d4fca1a`), seated in the book's dressed-page chapter. The `cases` gate is **red until the fix**, which is what the case is for. The runner hardening prediction 2 anticipated is **not** done — the throw never came, so the change would be speculative; the reading is recorded above for whoever meets it.

`capture.js` is untouched and stays that way until the next entry, which is the fix: read a page rule's descriptors from `rule.style` — measured to expose the `size` descriptor despite it being a descriptor — and its margin boxes from `rule.cssRules`, never from serialized text. The checklist's design-open for this fault now points at the case, which is where its numbers live; and it gains one new line of its own, the absurd-geometry finding from prediction 1.
