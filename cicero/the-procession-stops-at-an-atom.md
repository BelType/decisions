# The procession stops at an atom

**Status: Decided 2026-09-27, Executed 2026-09-27.** Convened by the plan for the fix round of case 70 (`cicero-the-procession-stops-at-an-atom`), on the branch of the same name stacked on the case's own; the rivals were read before any source changed, and the audit of the other walkers found one the plan had left to the round to decide.

## Context

*[The paragraph is what the browser breaks](the-paragraph-is-what-the-browser-breaks.md)* taught the Compositor that an atom — an SVG or MathML element, a replaced element, an atomic inline — is one unit of its paragraph and is never entered. The procession did not learn it. `word-space.js`'s `blockChildrenOf` reads computed display and descends through every inline box, and SVG's `<text>` computes `display: block` (Chromium 153, measured), so the walk went through an inline `<svg>` and seated its `<text>` elements as the paragraph's block children. Both modes share the procession, so both were wrong the same way.

[`packages/essay/cases/70-the-drawing-in-the-divided-paragraph`](https://github.com/egildo/beltype/blob/42b348b547d269a217a7f193042fe216386a4650/packages/essay/cases/70-the-drawing-in-the-divided-paragraph/README.md) draws it on the venue's small page, twelve 20px lines to a 240px page area. A paragraph of fourteen words takes seven lines; the next paragraph opens on line eight, puts case 68's 120×20px drawing alone on line nine, and runs to line twelve with five of its eight lines on page one. Chromium divides it after line twelve, the deepest clean Class B point, and page two opens on `p1w008`. The engine saw a composite with no line stack: what it was offered inside the paragraph was the Class A point between the two `<text>` elements, side by side on line nine, and the paragraph's end gap from the drawing's text to its end at 300px, which *the end gap breaks only at the edge* offers because the paragraph ends past the page edge. The end gap was deepest, so the account read `class C, y 240, path [1], word null, 60px remaining`: page two carried a blank shell 60px tall, and `p1w008` to `p1w012` were on no page.

**The second face, measured by the case as information.** `stonehand.js`'s `isMonolith` asked `REPLACED.has(el.tagName)` of a set spelled `['IMG', 'VIDEO', 'CANVAS', 'IFRAME', 'OBJECT', 'EMBED', 'SVG']`. HTML upper-cases `tagName` only in its own namespace, so an inline `<svg>` reports `svg` and the entry never matched a drawing the parser made. A block-level 120×100px drawing across the page foot is moved whole by Chromium's print (css-break-3 §4.1: a replaced element is monolithic) and was cut by the engine between its two `<text>` elements, `class A, y 199, path [1, 1]`.

## What the rivals do

Read before designing, at the paths the plan names; none was run.

**WeasyPrint** never looks inside an SVG while paginating. `html.py` registers a handler for `{http://www.w3.org/2000/svg}svg` — the namespace-qualified tag, not a spelling — which hands the element's whole subtree to `SVGImage` and returns `make_replaced_box`: an `InlineReplacedBox` or a `BlockReplacedBox` by the element's display, carrying no children; the drawing is drawn from its own tree at paint time and has no box inside it. `boxes.py`'s `is_monolithic` is true for every `AtomicInlineLevelBox` and every `ReplacedBox`. Inline, `layout/inline.py`'s `split_inline_level` sends an atomic box to `atomic_box`, which calls `inline_replaced_box_layout` for a replaced one and places it on the line as one item, breaking around it *"like ideographic characters"* (css-text-3); a line box offers nothing inside. Block-level, `layout/block.py`'s `block_level_layout_switch` hands a `BlockReplacedBox` to `block_replaced_box_layout` (`layout/replaced.py`), which sizes it and never splits it; `_in_flow_layout` then finds its content overflowing, and while the page holds something above it (`page_is_empty_with_no_children` false) sets the child to `None`, so it moves whole to the next page. On a page with nothing above it the box is placed and overflows, clipped rather than sliced.

**fragmentainers** agrees on both faces by a different route. Block-level: `layout-node.js`'s `REPLACED_ELEMENTS` is asked of `tagName?.toLowerCase()` and lists `svg`, so a block-level drawing is `isMonolithic` (`layout-helpers.js`); `block-container.js` pushes it to the next fragmentainer when it does not fit and content stands above it, and otherwise lays it out through `#layoutMonolithic`, which slices it in page mode as §4.4's last resort. Inline: a paragraph holding an inline `<svg>` is an inline formatting context, because its direct children are text and an inline element; `collect-inlines.js` walks into the inline `<svg>` by its display, and every descendant that is neither inline nor a `<br>` — each block-level `<text>` — becomes an `INLINE_ATOMIC` item, U+FFFC, on the line. So the procession never meets the drawing's text: the paragraph keeps one anonymous block of lines and breaks only between line boxes (`inline-content.js`), whose extents come from Range rects. Two differences from the rule below, neither contradicting it: fragmentainers enters the drawing and atomizes its non-inline descendants one by one rather than stopping at the drawing, and it atomizes a `<div>` inside a `<span>` the same way, where CSS 2.1 §9.2.1.1 and this engine seat that block in the enclosing procession (case 54).

**pagedNG** (`/Users/egildo/Privata/dev/pagedjs`, package `pagedjs` 0.6.0) does not fragment for itself: `src/index.js` re-exports `Fragmenter` and `LayoutHandler` from `fragmentainers`, its dependency, so its answer is fragmentainers'. Its own `REPLACED` set, in `handlers/occurrences.js`, is asked of `localName` and lists `svg`, `img`, `canvas` and the rest — only to decide whether a page opens with a string-set element.

**Paged.js classic** (`dev/pagedjs-classic`) has no monolith and no atom. `chunker/layout.js`'s `findOverflow` finds the first overflowing node through `startOfNewOverflow` and `firstOverflowingChild`, which walk `childNodes` of whatever node they are handed with no test of tag or display, so nothing stops the walk at an `<svg>`, a `<math>` or an `<img>` (an `<img>` has no children to enter); `avoidBreakInside` reads only an author's `break-inside: avoid`. `utils/dom.js`'s `isContainer` is a `switch` over upper-case `tagName` — `IMG`, `VIDEO`, `CANVAS`, `OBJECT` among the non-containers — and an inline `<svg>` reports `svg`, falls to `default` and is a container: the capital-letter trap `REPLACED` fell into, in a second codebase. By reading, a block-level drawing across the foot would be divided between its `<text>` elements as this engine divided it; not run.

**Verdict of the reading.** The two engines with a fragmentation model — WeasyPrint natively, fragmentainers and so pagedNG — both make a replaced element, SVG included, monolithic by a lower-case or namespace-qualified name, and both keep an inline drawing on its line with no break point inside it. The one that does neither has no model at all. Nothing read contradicts the rule, so the round proceeded. Where they differ from this engine they differ outside the rule: WeasyPrint clips a monolith taller than an empty page where this engine paints its remainder on the next (C10, case 08), and fragmentainers' block-in-inline treatment is not case 54's.

## Decision

**One predicate, two faces.** An atom — a foreign-namespace element (SVG, MathML), a replaced element, an atomic inline-level box — is one unit to every walk the engine makes, and is never entered.

1. **The procession stops at an atom.** `blockChildrenOf` asks `isAtom` of a child before the inline descent, after the `none` and `contents` skip: an atom whose own box is inline-level is part of its paragraph's line stack and contributes no block child; an atom whose own box is block-level is one block child, a box of the procession, and nothing inside it is.
2. **A block-level atom is a monolith.** `isMonolith`'s first clause is `isAtom(el)`, by local name and namespace, never by `tagName`. It moves whole, or is sliced by the edge with its remainder painted, as `<img>` already is (case 08).

`isAtom` moves from `compose-dom.js` to `word-space.js`, beside the other predicates over computed display, with its constants — `HTML_NS`, the one lower-case list of replaced local names, `ATOMIC_INLINE` — and its per-source cache. `compose-dom.js` and `stonehand.js` import it. `stonehand.js`'s capitalised `REPLACED` is deleted. The predicate reads an element with no namespace at all as HTML, which is what a plain object tree in the DOM-free suites is.

**3. The Gauge does not enter an atom either — the audit's finding, and the round's one widening of the plan.** Stopping the procession at an atom moved a question onto the Gauge that the procession had been answering by accident. The box walk that makes the report's boxes stops at an atom: what an atom holds generates no box of the measured flow, and nothing could address one. And **the words an inline atom holds are read at the atom's own box**, and homed to the line holding it, whose line box is extended to contain it — css-break-3 §4.1's *"line boxes contain no possible break points"*, read in the one organ that recovers line boxes from rects. Two scratch documents on case 70's page, run before this clause was written and after the procession stopped, are why it is here:

- **An inline drawing taller than its line**, 120×60px with its two words at y 15 and y 55, standing alone on the lines from 200px to 260px of a page ending at 240px. Chromium moves that line whole and opens page two on `s0w0`. The Gauge read the two words at their glyphs as two lines and the Stonehand took the Class B point between them: page one ended under `s0w0` with the drawing clipped through, page two opened on `s0w1`. No word was lost, and the drawing was divided where Chromium's print does not divide it.
- **The conservation probe's inline-blocks** (`packages/essay/probes/conservation.js`, *inline-blocks on one line*, a survey that does not gate). Before the round, an inline-block's paragraphs joined the enclosing procession and were held by the monolith rule (`notYetDivided`'s *inline block container*): nine cells, three `moved` and six `same`. With the procession stopped and the Gauge unchanged, the holder became a leaf whose line stack was the inline-blocks' own paragraphs, and a cut between them **lost a word** — `plate, room 6: LOST 1`. With this clause: all nine `same`, in both modes. A lost word is the one thing a fix round may not buy, so the plan's *do not widen beyond what the audit needs* is what put the clause in.

The line arithmetic for text is untouched: a line holding no atom is clustered and recovered exactly as before, byte for byte, and the tokenization is untouched everywhere — every word inside an atom is still counted, in order, by all four tokenizations. An atom that holds no text, an `<img>` or an empty inline-block, contributes no word and so, as before, no line of its own.

## The audit — every walker that enters elements

**Changed**: `blockChildrenOf` (the rule's first face); `isMonolith` (its second), which also reaches `neverCutInside` and so the Cast-off's bound and the forcing index's sealing; the Gauge's box walk and its word reading (clause 3).

**Unchanged, and why.**

- `driver.js`'s forcing descent (`forcingIndexOf`, `nameAt`, `rootParityDemand`) walks through `blockChildrenOf` and asks `isMonolith` before descending, so it inherits both faces with no edit of its own.
- `positions.js`'s `indexBlocks` and `wordsBeforeChar`, and `fragments.js`'s `joinedWords`, are tokenizations: they count words, not boxes, and must agree with each other and with the Gauge's count, which they still do. The words a drawing holds are text Chromium prints and the venue counts; making an atom one U+FFFC word, as the Compositor's `collectWords` does, would move the word space of every document holding a drawing with text and would ask the fragment writer to consume an element rather than blank a run — a round of its own with no case asking for it. An address inside an atom cannot arise from a cut: a Class B cut resumes at the first word of a line, and every word an atom holds is homed to the atom's one line, so the only word inside an atom a cut can name is its first, which is the boundary before it. `wordsBeforeChar` answers a character inside an atom with its word, whose page is the page the atom's line is on.
- `compose-dom.js`'s `collectWords` already stops at an atom; it is where the rule came from.

## Consequences

- **Every replaced element is a monolith to the Stonehand now**, not only the six it listed: `audio`, `input`, `textarea`, `select`, `meter` and `progress` join, because the Compositor's list was the longer one and there is one list.
- **A block-level MathML element is a monolith**, and a block-level Mermaid diagram — an `<svg>` a page foot crosses — moves whole or is sliced by the edge, where it used to be cut between its own boxes. Neither is measured on BelType's manual in this round.
- **Inline MathML is not closed by this — measured.** Chromium computes `display: math` for an inline `<math>`, CSS Display's `math`, whose outer display is inline, and `isInlineBox` reads only displays beginning with `inline`, so an inline formula is seated as one block child of its paragraph: never entered now, but still a composite. A scratch document on case 70's page, thirteen words with `<math><mi>x</mi></math>` after the fourth, was cut in its end gap at the edge (`class C, y 240, 40px remaining`) in both modes after this round, and its last three words were on no page. Filed on the checklist and in `.claude/NEEDED-FIXES.md` rather than fixed here, because `isInlineBox` is what all four tokenizations ask, so the fix moves the word space around every inline formula.
- The Gauge measures fewer boxes wherever a document holds drawings, since nothing inside an atom is measured; unmeasured here.
- The tablet is untouched. The Cut's monolith already names replaced elements; this says which elements those are, and that the rule holds in every walk rather than in one.
