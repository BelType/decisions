# A parity is worth a page

**Status: Decided 2026-09-07, Executed 2026-09-07.**

## Context

`break-before: left | right | recto | verso` has been accepted as forcing since the edge round and has never performed a parity. The break happened; the side did not. [P26c–P26e](https://github.com/egildo/beltype/blob/42b348b547d269a217a7f193042fe216386a4650/packages/essay/probes/README.md) measured the shape from both directions in 2026-07-28 and the [checklist](https://github.com/egildo/beltype/blob/42b348b547d269a217a7f193042fe216386a4650/packages/engine-cicero/docs/css-paged-media-checklist.md)'s *Parity forcing* design open has carried it since: no engine the venue runs honours the parity, so **the oracle cannot adjudicate it**, and the differential is structurally blind — drawing the values into the corpus would test nothing, because both columns would agree, wrongly. That is orphans-and-widows' own shape: a spec-literal feature no surveyed browser provides, and therefore ours everywhere or nowhere.

Seven standing rows hang on the one build. css-break-3 §3.1 defines the four values: *"Force one or two page breaks before/after the principal box so that the next page is formatted as a left page"* — and the same for right, with recto and verso naming the second and first page of a spread *according to the page progression*. §4.3 adds the combination rule: *"When multiple forced break values apply to a single break point, they combine such that all types of break are honored. When left, right, recto, and/or verso are combined, the value specified on the latest element in the flow wins."* css-page-3 §3.3 gives the document's own first page: *"To explicitly force a document to begin printing on a left or right page, authors can specify a break-before value that propagates a page break to the root. The UA must suppress the first (empty) page(s) in this case."* And §4.2.3 is what the second break is *for*: *"The :blank pseudo-class matches content-empty pages that appear as a result of forced page breaks. Only the left, right, recto and verso values … can generate pages that match :blank."*

The checklist had already named the shape the build wants, in the same sentence that parked `:blank` downstream of it rather than beside it: **a blank page that is a real Ledger entry — an interval of zero content that the account carries, the Chase presents, and `:blank` can then style.** This round builds that sentence.

## Decision

**The second break is taken, and what it costs is an entry.** Six changes, in four files, and one of the seven rows turns out to be blocked one specification over.

**One — a forced value carries a parity, and the parity is read against the progression the engine already has.** `parityOf` maps `left`/`verso` to left and `right`/`recto` to right, and `page` to nothing. The recto/verso half is a reading rather than a table: §3.1 defines them against the *page progression*, css-page-3 §3.3 makes the first page of a left-to-right document a right page, and `parityAt` — the driver's page-index-to-parity arithmetic — **is** that progression. So the two live in one edit by construction, and the day a right-to-left progression is modelled they move together or the engine is describing two documents.

**Two — §4.3's combination rule is one function, because the section is one sentence.** `combineParity(earlier, later)` returns the later parity where there is one and the earlier otherwise. Two things fall out of that and both are stated rather than implied. A value carrying no parity — a plain `page`, or a named-page switch, which §4.3 makes forcing under another name — **honours** a parity declared beside it rather than cancelling it, which is what *all types of break are honored* means when one of the two types is unqualified. And down the descent, a container's own `break-after` is read after its last in-flow child's, so the container's parity replaces the child's at the position the two share. §4.3 does not say which of two elements ending at one point is "latest"; the descent's own order is the answer taken, and it is written down.

**Three — the demand rides the cursor and is spent at the loop's top.** A forced entry's `next` carries `demandParity`. The next iteration reads it, clears it, and — if the page it is about to build has the wrong parity — writes a **blank entry** instead: same main position as the entry before it (I1 admits equal positions; that equality *is* an empty interval), `cls: 'A'`, `trace: { forced: true, blank: true }`, closing conditions of its own with `blank` in them. The cursor does not move, so the content resumes exactly where the forcing said.

**One per forcing, by construction rather than by a counter.** The demand is consumed whether or not it is spent, and one page always flips the parity. A parity arithmetic error can therefore cost a wrong page and can never cost a loop — which is the property C2 wants, and it is why the blank branch is a consumed flag rather than a `while` over a predicate. **Refused: a `blank` clause in the C2 advance check**, which the assessment proposed. The blank branch `continue`s before the check is reached, so a clause there would be describing a path that cannot arrive — a check that cannot fail, which is the venue's own standing complaint about itself.

**Four — the root's demand is a parity OFFSET, not an entry.** §3.3 says the UA *must suppress* the first empty pages, and suppressing a page is not producing one and hiding it: the page never exists, `counter(page)` still starts at 1 on the page the reader holds, and `:first` still matches page index 0. What changes is `parityAt`, once, for the whole document. The chain walked is §3.1.1's own — a `break-before` on a first in-flow child propagates to its parent — so the values reaching the root are those declared down the first-in-flow-child chain from the root element, the deepest winning as the latest element at the one shared position. Nothing is double-counted: the grab refuses anything not strictly after the cursor, and the cursor starts at the document's first word.

**Five — `:blank` is a fourth condition on the capture's cascade, and §4.4's specificity becomes a tuple.** The filter is one clause: a `:blank` rule applies only to a blank page, and **nothing else about such a page changes** — it keeps its parity and its page name, so a blank right page is matched by `@page :blank` and `@page :right` alike, which is §4.2.3's own worked example. The specificity moves from a weighted sum to css-page-3 §4.4's (f, g, h) triple compared lexicographically. The sum reproduced the right order for exactly as long as `g` could only ever be 1, and `:blank` is the value that breaks it: `:first:blank` counts 2 at `g` and would outrank a page NAME under any weighting that also lets `:first` outrank parity. Nothing that stood moves — for every selector without `:blank` the two orders agree term for term.

**Six — the account carries `parity` and `blank`, and the presentation stops re-deriving.** `root.js#present` computed a page's parity from its own page number, which agreed with the driver's derivation for exactly as long as that derivation had no offset in it. §3.3 gives it one. Two derivations of one fact is the fork the 2026-08-04 account ruling exists to forbid, so both are named in the account's `closes` whitelist and read. `pageIntervals` carries `blank` beside them, and **the view's blank guard is load-bearing rather than tidy**: a forcing interior to a top-level block leaves the blank page's interval starting mid-block, so the block loop would build a continuation clone for it and print the same words twice. An empty interval does not say *assign nothing*; the flag does.

**Refused: naming the blank page after the page before it.** css-page-3 says nothing about which page name a generated blank page takes. It takes the name of the content that resumes — the same `nameAt(cursor)` every other page's conditions come from — because the cursor is where the page's conditions are read from and a second rule for one entry kind is a second thing to get wrong. Recorded as a choice, and reversible: a document that wants the closing chapter's dress on the page after it will say so.

**Refused: laws.** None moves. I4 holds and is what makes this cheap — the blank page is an entry, so `length` is still the page count and `counter(page)` still counts it. I1 holds on equal positions. C1 holds: the forcing is consumed by the grab, once, and the second break is bookkeeping over what it left. C8 holds: the blank page's conditions are derived from the entry before it like every other page's.

## Predictions

Frozen before `src/` was touched, each with its break criterion.

1. **`cases/23-the-page-a-rule-selects` reaches four pages on both Cicero columns and stays at three on Chromium's.** *Breaks* on any other pair — three on ours would mean the demand never reached the loop, five would mean it was spent twice.
2. **`test/specimen.md` under the seam goes from six pages to ten** — the assessment's own arithmetic, four `break-before: recto` chapters each costing one page. *Breaks* on any other number, and the number is the interesting part either way: a smaller one means a chapter's demand was already satisfied by the parity it landed on, which would make the arithmetic wrong rather than the build.
3. **Every other case is unmoved.** No case but 23 declares a parity value. *Breaks* on any case whose result differs from the same run against the pre-round `src/`.
4. **`cases/acceptance.js` keeps its pinned 12 pages in both modes.** *Breaks* on any other number.
5. **The DOM-free suite stays green and gains a pin per rule.** *Breaks* on a rule whose pin cannot be written without a browser — which would be a finding about where the rule lives.
6. **`@page :blank` prints its running head on the new blank page.** The case declares the rule and the page now exists. *Breaks* if it prints nothing, which would mean the cascade never reached it.

## Verdict

**Prediction 1 held.** Case 23 prints **4 pages** on `cicero` and on `cicero-composed`, **3** on `chromium`, and the partition divergence begins at page three exactly as declared. `startsAPage` puts "Section two" on page four; the folio and the top band hold on every page; the fourth page host reads the `:left` ground the parity says it must.

**Prediction 2 BROKE, in both of its terms, and the correction is the round's best measurement.** `test/specimen.md` under the seam goes from six pages to **nine**, not ten: three blank pages, not four, because the fourth chapter's `recto` demand lands on a page that is already right. And the number it was being compared against was stale. **Paged.js prints nine as well** — measured the same day, on the same document, through BelType's own dev server: nine pages, three of them carrying `pagedjs_blank_page`, **at page indices 3, 5 and 7**. Cicero's blank pages are at indices 3, 5 and 7. The two engines agree page for page and blank for blank, and [NEEDED-FIXES](https://github.com/egildo/beltype/blob/42b348b547d269a217a7f193042fe216386a4650/.claude/NEEDED-FIXES.md) §3.1b's surviving half — *seven pages against Paged.js's eleven*, then *six against eleven* — is closed by the two of them meeting at nine. The eleven was never re-measured after the document was renamed, and the `6 + 4 = 10` beside it was arithmetic nobody had run.

**Prediction 3 held, and the check is the useful part.** The whole case suite was run twice on the same machine — once against this `src/` and once against the pre-round `src/` extracted to a scratch directory and reached through `ENGINE_DIR`. The two failure lists are **identical line for line outside case 23**: 27 checks failing in both, on cases 01, 03, 05, 06, 07, 09, 11, 12, 13, 15, 17, 18 and **29**, before and after. Case 23 accounts for the whole difference — five checks failing against the pre-round source, because the case is re-declared at four pages, and none against this one. *(A correction to round 1's record, which listed twelve of those cases and omitted 29: the failure list is the authority, and 29 was in it then too.)* Every one of the 27 is environmental — Georgia and Times are absent on this machine — and each page-count failure hits Chromium's own column as hard as the engine's.

**Prediction 4 held.** `cases/acceptance.js` passes at 12 pages presented and printed in the native mode and with `--composed` alike.

**Prediction 5 held.** 68 tests to **74**: four in a new `test/driver.test.js` for §3.1's parity map and §4.3's combination, two in `test/positions.test.js` for the blank page's empty interval and its invisibility to a position query. Two of the six fail against the previous source and the rest state invariants rather than pin defects.

**Prediction 6 BROKE, and it is the round's one wall.** The `@page :blank` rule prints nothing, and the reason is not this engine's. **Chromium's CSS parser refuses the selector**: measured with a throwaway probe on a three-rule stylesheet, `document.styleSheets` hands back the base `@page` rule and the `@page :right` rule and **no third rule at all** — `@page :blank { … }` never enters the CSSOM. The capture reads the browser's own parsed page rules, by the ruling in [*a page rule reads its own declarations*](a-page-rule-reads-its-own-declarations.md), so there is nothing there to read. The engine's own half is built and unreachable: `:blank` is a condition `parseSelector` carries, `pageFor` filters on, and the driver supplies. What is missing is the same thing GCPM's `string-set` is missing — **a reader for the declarations the browser drops** — which the architecture already names as the source-text pass and which the checklist already carries as a design round on placement, since `src/` carries no dependency and css-tree cannot live there. So the blank page comes out wearing what the base rule and `@page :right` give it: a running head and a folio, which is exactly what §4.2.3's own example warns an author to remove.

**Too good to be true, checked.** Two things.

The first was real and is the strongest evidence in the round: Cicero and Paged.js agreeing on nine pages *and on which three are blank*, on a document neither was tuned against. Paged.js is a second implementation that does perform the parity, so this is the one place in the round where an oracle exists at all — and it is not the venue's usual oracle, which is why it was measured rather than assumed.

The second was not. Case 23's folio and top-band checks passed on the very first run at four pages, which looked like the `:blank` rule working. It was the opposite: the checks passed because page three shows *`PAGE SELECTORS`* and the folio *`3`* — the base rule's furniture, on a page whose own rule had never arrived. A green that would have read as `:blank` delivered, and it took reading the band contents rather than the check's verdict to see it.

**One thing was found and not fixed.** A forced break drops a straddling parallel flow's carried paths: the two branches that write a forced entry (`fits && forcedNext`, and the no-verdict branch) build `paths` from the forcing alone and pass `next = forcedNext`, which carries no `carried`. On the first branch nothing can straddle — the feed fit — so it is only reachable on the second, where an uncuttable run overflows and a float beside it crosses the limit. No case draws it and no corpus era draws an out-of-flow box at all. It belongs to the flows, not to the parity, and it is recorded here rather than acted on.

**The battery ran at the close of the rounds (2026-09-07), and no standing number moved for this round — checked by a counterfactual rather than by a tally.** The three gates whose tallies the run disagrees with — `native` (0–199), `composed-v2` (200–299 `--composed`) and `v3-native` (400–449) — were each run a second time against the **pre-round `src/`**, the tree at `1cce345` reached through `ENGINE_DIR`, and the two runs' reports are **identical seed for seed**: 164/18/11/7 native, 84/12/4 composed-v2, 42/6/1/1 v3-native. So the argument above is measured rather than argued: `seeds/generate.js` draws forcing exclusively as `break-before: page`, no seed reaches the branch this round adds, `parityAt` reduces to the previous `pageNumber % 2` arithmetic wherever no root demand exists, and the specificity tuple is term-for-term the sum it replaced on every selector without `:blank`, which no seed writes. **The three disagreements with `gates/records.js` are identical before and after these rounds and are therefore not the engine's**; they are the machine's, the record is left standing, and the account is in the repo [CHANGELOG](https://github.com/egildo/beltype/blob/42b348b547d269a217a7f193042fe216386a4650/CHANGELOG.md) and in the [checklist](https://github.com/egildo/beltype/blob/42b348b547d269a217a7f193042fe216386a4650/packages/engine-cicero/docs/css-paged-media-checklist.md) §7. The pin is not rewritten — only a complete run with every gate green may write one, and `cases` and `suite` are red here on a font substitution.

## Consequences

The [tablet](https://github.com/egildo/beltype/blob/42b348b547d269a217a7f193042fe216386a4650/packages/engine-cicero/docs/design-tablet.md) does not move. The Page's *per-page conditions — first, parity, blank, a named page's geometry — are derived from the ledger* was already written for this; it is built now rather than amended.

[`architecture.md`](https://github.com/egildo/beltype/blob/42b348b547d269a217a7f193042fe216386a4650/packages/engine-cicero/docs/architecture.md) moves in two places. The build loop's step 1 gains the blank page: a page the loop writes without measuring, and the one place the cursor does not advance. The Ledger's account paragraph records that `parity` and `blank` are the account's literals now and why the presentation may not re-derive the first.

The [checklist](https://github.com/egildo/beltype/blob/42b348b547d269a217a7f193042fe216386a4650/packages/engine-cicero/docs/css-paged-media-checklist.md) moves in five. Part one §1's `:blank` line and §4's parity paragraph go from *designed*/*not honored by either side* to built, with the oracle's parse refusal named. Part three's §3.1, §4.2 and §4.3 rows move with them. The *Parity forcing* design open closes and is replaced by the one that outlived it — the reader for declarations the browser drops, which is now blocking two capabilities rather than one.

Next door, [`cases/23-the-page-a-rule-selects`](https://github.com/egildo/beltype/blob/42b348b547d269a217a7f193042fe216386a4650/packages/essay/cases/23-the-page-a-rule-selects/README.md) is re-declared at four pages with a divergent Chromium column and its argument, a fourth page host style, and a README that says what its `:blank` rule is blocked on now rather than what it was blocked on before. `lib/chapters.js`' rows move in three chapters: `forced-breaks`' §3.1 and §4.3 rows, `which-page-this-is`' §4.2.3 row and its precondition, and `the-page-box`' §3.2, §3.3-root and §4.2 spread-break rows.

At the repo root, [NEEDED-FIXES](https://github.com/egildo/beltype/blob/42b348b547d269a217a7f193042fe216386a4650/.claude/NEEDED-FIXES.md) §3.1b's surviving half is struck: the two engines meet at nine pages with the same three blanks, and the entry's own *how you would know* — one probe counting Paged.js's blank pages — is what answered it.

What is owed and not paid here: the battery and the pin, at the close of the rounds; the source-text reader `:blank` and `string-set` now both wait on; and the carried-flow path a forced break drops.
