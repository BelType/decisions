# A competing flow bargains the foot of the main flow

**Status: Decided 2026-10-22.** A design round with Egildo, 2026-10-22, opened on his proposal that the flows into a page want one object, the fragmentainer, with contextual tweaks for different jobs. It amends the tablet's The Flows, two sentences, and the architecture's guard between the parallel and the competing kind. It decides no solver: the three rulings the footnote seat owes him ([*the footnote area is a row of the page*](the-footnote-area-is-a-row-of-the-page.md)) are restated below as the open questions, with the canon beside each.

## Context

The tablet classifies content by one question, how its footprint depends on the cut, and answers it four ways: zero (chrome), constant (a parallel flow), the cut's own subject (the flow) and a function of the cut (a competing flow, footnotes). It then adds that a shared classification is never a shared mechanism, and in The Flows that a competing flow "is the other thing, and it has a fixpoint": which notes belong to a page depends on the cut, the room the cut has depends on the notes, and a solver, greedy with one bounded backtrack, is owed for that loop alone. The architecture's The Flows section exists, in its own words, to keep the two kinds from sharing code.

Two things moved since those sentences were written. [*A parallel flow is a fragmentainer*](a-parallel-flow-is-a-fragmentainer.md) (2026-10-08) made the fragmentainer one object, a geometry, a flow, a resume address in and an entry out, with the page, a cell, a float's continuation and a column that object under different feet, and the architecture says the kinds differ "in the foot and the relation of the entries, and nothing else". And on 2026-10-21, in the round that decided [*the page carries its conditions*](the-page-carries-its-conditions.md), Egildo collapsed the footnote's loop into arithmetic: what came before is in the feed, and what the cut will cost is a function of the candidate. The foot at a candidate is the content height less the footer's demand and the notes' demand at that candidate. A footprint that is a function of the candidate is not circular, since the retreat walk already evaluates candidates one by one; the fixpoint the tablet named is the fixpoint of a solver that asked the room before it chose the cut.

The showcase of 2026-10-21 was looked at for what its asides do: its engine notes are kept whole by `break-inside: avoid`, its tinted "An aside" is divided and dressed as a continuation, and neither takes room from anything. Nothing in a BelType document today is a competing flow; the footnote is the first, and the repeated table header, built 2026-10-14 as a reservation (`reservePx`), is the same shape already in the engine under another name.

## What the text says

- css-break-3 §2.1: fragmentation is performed independently in each formatting context, and a forced break inside a float affects nothing outside it except insofar as it may increase the float's height. The channel across the wall is height; the text says nothing about who fragments the interior.
- css-gcpm-3 (W3C Working Draft, 25 January 2024, the latest) §2.2: `float: footnote` removes the element from the flow, leaves a `::footnote-call` in its place, puts a `::footnote-marker` at the body's start, increments the footnote counter and places the body in the page's footnote area, "footnote elements from a given page are placed in the footnote area of that page in document order". §2.3: `footnote-display` is `block`, the initial value, `inline` or `compact`, and under the last the user agent packs a one-line note inline. §2.4: the `@footnote` rule in the page context defines the area, which can only contain footnotes; its bottom margin edge touches the bottom of the page area; `max-height` limits it "unless the page contains only footnotes", and user agents may set a default `max-height`. §2.5: the footnote counter's value depends on the element's position in the tree, never on where the body lands, and it may reset on each page. §2.7: `::footnote-marker` behaves like `::marker`, inline, `list-style-position: inside`. §2.8: `footnote-policy` is `auto`, the initial value, `line` or `block`: under `auto` the user agent chooses how to render and "a footnote body must never be placed on a page before the footnote reference"; under `line`, a body that cannot fit forces a page break at the start of the line holding the call, and the user agent "must honor widow and orphan settings"; under `block`, the break falls before the paragraph. Appendix B's default sheet gives `@footnote` `float: bottom`, `column-span: all` and `height: auto`: the area is itself a page float in the spec's own model. Open issues: no layout algorithm ("we need an algorithm for laying out footnotes"), footnotes in multicol unspecified (Prince's `float: prince-column-footnote` cited), the relation of other page floats to the area unasked; sidenotes, column footnotes and several areas are the next level's. Nothing on nested footnotes.
- CSS 2.1 §17.2: a table's header group may be repeated at the top of each page fragment, which the engine reads as a demand on every page the table reaches after its first.

## Decision

**A competing flow bargains the foot of the main flow: the one object, carrying a demand on the page, and the main flow's foot at a candidate is the content height less the demands at it.** The competing flow's own foot is a constant, its `max-height`; what varies with the candidate is the room it uses, and the foot that varies is the main flow's. Six rulings.

1. **One object.** A flow into the page is a fragmentainer: it owns a geometry inside the page area and a flow of its own, it receives a resume address into that flow and a foot, and it closes with an entry of its own under the page's. The footnote area is that object with the row the seat record gave it and the bodies as its flow; a repeated header is that object with the header's clone; a page float, when one comes, with the float. A cell, a column and a float's continuation stay what they are.
2. **Two properties, not one.** The fragmentainer is the thing with a flow to cut: geometry, flow, an address in, an entry out. The demand is the height a box takes from the main flow's foot at a candidate. They are separate properties of one box, and the footnote area is the one case that has both in full, which is why it looked like one object. A repeated header carries a demand and has no flow of its own to cut (no address, no entry: it rides the table's), so it is a demand on a box, and a fragmentainer only in the degenerate sense. A parallel flow is a fragmentainer whose demand is zero. Chrome is neither. A flow-in's membership, which bodies it holds on a page, may depend on the candidate apart from its demand: a sidenote area, the next GCPM level's, would hold the notes called above the candidate, carry overflow to the next page's margin and demand nothing of the main flow. So the general shape is a fragmentainer with `flow(candidate)` and `demand(candidate)`, and the footnote is the one where the two are the same function.
3. **The main flow's foot is a function of the candidate.** A flow-in has a demand, `demand(candidate)`, the room it takes from the page when the cut falls at that candidate: for the footnote area, the heights of the bodies whose calls lie above the candidate, in document order (§2.2), capped by `max-height`, a sum that holds for `footnote-display: block`, the initial value, and not for `inline` or `compact`, where the area is a paragraph the calls' set lays out and the demand is that paragraph's height, monotone still and no longer a sum (refused below until cased); for a repeated header, the header's height on every page after the table's first, known once the first fragment decides it and so a constant on the page's foot; for a parallel flow, zero. The page's foot at a candidate is the content height less the sum of the demands at it, and the Stonehand's fit arithmetic takes that number where it takes a constant today.
4. **The walk answers; no solver is owed, and `footnote-policy` says what the walk is asked.** A demand that is monotone in the candidate, growing or staying as the candidate moves down the page, is answered by the retreat walk as it stands: the lowest candidate whose flow fits its foot is the cut. The footnote's demand is monotone by construction, since moving a candidate up removes calls and never adds one. What the walk is asked is the author's `footnote-policy` (§2.8). Under `auto`, the initial value, a body the room refuses splits or is carried (ruling five) and the cut stands: the demand at a candidate is what fits, and the walk never retreats for a note. Under `line` the body must fit whole or the candidate is refused, and the walk retreats to the start of the line holding the call, orphans and widows kept; under `block`, to the start of the paragraph. So the bargain has teeth only where the author asks for them, and the default is the canon's: text first, the note gives way. A demand that is not monotone is refused until a case shows one; a page float deferred to the next page when it does not fit is that demand (its height above the anchor, zero once deferred), and Appendix B makes the footnote area itself a `float: bottom`, so when GCPM's page floats are cased the deferral is a second pass over the walk, the float carried at zero demand, and not a demand on this one.
5. **The cap and the carry.** A demand the cap refuses, a note that does not fit the area's `max-height` (which yields when the page holds only footnotes, §2.4) or a remainder the page cannot hold, is carried: a remainder entry under the page's, resumed first on the next page's area before that page's own notes, by the same continuation machinery a divided block uses. The "one bounded backtrack" the tablet promised its solver is this carry and nothing more.
6. **Chrome stays chrome.** Margin boxes, running elements and the folio stand outside the page area at zero footprint and are no flow-in; the object gains no slot for them. A parallel flow's demand is zero, which is the old guard stated as a number: nothing inside a cell or a float reaches the page's ranking, and nothing is re-coupled.

**Amended in the tablet** (The Flows, two sentences, and the taxonomy's closing sentence; the old wording kept in the parenthetical as the house does): *a competing flow is the other thing, and it has a fixpoint* becomes *a competing flow bargains the foot of the main flow*; *the two may not share a mechanism* becomes *they share the object and differ in the demand*; *a shared classification is never a shared mechanism* keeps its warning for the parallel kind, whose demand is zero, and drops the claim that the competing kind needs machinery of its own. **Amended in the architecture**: The Flows' opening sentence and its competing-flow paragraph say the same, and the fragmentainer's table gains the two flow-in rows.

**Refused.** A solver with a fixpoint (its loop was the solver's own; the walk has none). A second object for the competing kind (the differences are the foot and the carry, both expressible on the one object). A flow-in for chrome. Generalising the demand to anything a page may hold before a case asks for it: the footnote and the repeated header are the two, a page float the third when GCPM's page floats are cased, with the deferral pass ruling four names. `footnote-display: inline` and `compact` until a case asks, since the sum of ruling three is not their demand. A footnote inside a footnote, on which the text is silent, refused by name. Column footnotes, an open issue in the text, until the column round. A row of the page for the repeated header, a twin of the footnote area's at the top of the content area, proposed by Egildo and probed the same day (*the header row*, P41, [the memo](https://github.com/egildo/beltype/blob/42b348b547d269a217a7f193042fe216386a4650/.claude/rounds/cicero/the-header-row-probe.md)): refused by him on the measurement. The row holds the header only as the continuation's own fragment sawn between `thead` and `tbody`, so one table becomes two boxes on one page and the join is paid twice, and it cannot hold a nested header, two side-by-side headers or the footer under its last row; the demand is a number the copy already gives (`reservePx`). The header stays in the copy.

## Open, owed by Egildo before the footnote solver is built

The seat record's three questions, restated with the canon and a recommendation beside each. Each is one ruling; none is taken here.

1. **Under `footnote-policy: auto`, split a long note, or carry it whole?** The text leaves it to the user agent (§2.8, and the open issue that owes an algorithm); under `line` and `block` the author has answered, the body fits whole or the cut retreats, and the question is only the default's. TeX splits an insertion: the first part stays on the call's page, the remainder goes first on the next page's area, and a later note never overtakes an earlier one. WeasyPrint splits, Vivliostyle splits, Paged.js carries whole and loses the call's page when it must. Under ruling five a split is a remainder entry, a carry is a remainder that is the whole note. **Recommendation**: split the TeX way, at a line boundary, with §2.8 kept (the first part never above its call), since a carried whole note on a page of short lines empties the area for one call.
2. **A floor under the text?** Whether a page may be mostly notes. The canon has no floor: TeX, LaTeX and every browser engine let the area's cap be the one lever, GCPM lets the user agent choose a default `max-height` and lifts the cap on a page that holds only footnotes (§2.4). A floor is a price in the make-up order, a reason to retreat the cut above a candidate whose notes would leave fewer than some lines of text. **Recommendation**: no floor; a default `max-height` the engine states (the canon's is none, so the engine's default is the page area), and the author's `max-height` the lever. A document for which that is too little is the case that would add a floor.
3. **A call inside a cell, a float or a column.** Its body is a demand on the outer page, so "only height crosses the wall" does not hold for it: the inner walk must report its calls up, not their height. **Recommendation**: a call reports across the wall as a demand with the call's address, the inner fragmentainer passes it to the outer, and the outer page's foot counts it as it counts a call in the flow. The tablet's sentence gains one clause, *and a demand crosses the wall beside the height*. Until built, a call inside a wall is a stated limit, not a silent loss: the body prints at the foot of the page where the wall's box begins.

## Consequences

- **Displaced**: the solver with a fixpoint; the guard paragraph that kept the two kinds from sharing code; `reservePx` as a special case, which becomes the repeated header's demand under ruling three when the footnote round is built.
- **Owed by the footnote round**: the three rulings above, taken; the probe the seat record owes on what Chromium's parser keeps (`float: footnote`, `footnote-policy`, `footnote-display`, `@footnote`, the two pseudo-elements); a case red first, a note at the page's last line, under `auto` and under `line`; the Stonehand's fit against a foot that is a function, and the refusal of a candidate under `line` and `block`; the measure feed's footnote row, bodies measured once at the area's width; the remainder entry and its carry; the two pseudo-elements and the counter through the host, the counter's value by tree position and its reset per page a page condition (§2.5); Caster's region for the area.
- **Where the content lives**: the object in the tablet's The Flows and the architecture's fragmentainer section, the demand in `stonehand.js` and `driver.js` when built, the status in the checklist's footnotes row, the story in the CHANGELOG.
