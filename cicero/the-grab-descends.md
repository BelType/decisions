# The grab descends

**Status: Decided 2026-09-03, Executed 2026-09-03.**

## Context

[*Forcing under sections earns a case*](forcing-under-sections-earns-a-case.md) authored `cases/19-forcing-under-sections` red and watched it fail for the reasons it names: Chromium prints eight pages, Cicero six, and all three `startsAPage` claims fail in both modes — a forced `break-before` on a section's first child, a forced `break-after` on a paragraph in the middle of a section, and a `page: plate` on a heading inside a section that names none. The [2026-09-02 investigation](https://github.com/egildo/beltype/blob/42b348b547d269a217a7f193042fe216386a4650/.claude/rounds/cicero/cicero-sections-report.md) had traced the cause to one code path and only one: the grab in [`src/driver.js`](https://github.com/egildo/beltype/blob/42b348b547d269a217a7f193042fe216386a4650/packages/engine-cicero/src/driver.js) reads forcing by comparing consecutive feed items, and a feed item is a top-level block, so a command declared on a descendant is invisible to it. Because BelType's belt wraps every heading in a `<section>`, that is the shape every real BelType document now has, and under `--engine cicero` every one of them loses its chapter breaks with no warning and no failed assertion.

The tablet is not amended, because it already says what is required. C1 reads *no forced break may lie inside an interval; forcing belongs to the forward grab*, and The Cut says *forced breaks already consumed by the grab*. What was narrower than the law was the architecture's grab, which found forcing only between top-level feed items. The law ranges over the document; the mechanism ranged over one list.

The case round's own closing sketch named the small half — a `forcedBreakBefore` mirroring `forcedBreakAfter` — and then named the half that is not a patch: a command interior to a feed item is reachable by no propagation walk at all, and whether the grab descends or the feed stops being the granularity of forcing is a design question. This entry answers it.

## Decision

**The grab finds forcing at word granularity, by descending the in-flow block tree, and truncates the feed at the position it finds.** One new question is asked once per page: *what is the first forcing event strictly after the cursor?* An event is a flat position in the document's word space plus its kind, and there are three sources, all of them at css-break-3 §4.1's class A points:

- a forced `break-before` on an in-flow block box, at that box's start word;
- a forced `break-after` on an in-flow block box whose end is not the document's end, at the word after its last;
- a named-page switch (§4.3), at the start word of the first in-flow block box whose *effective* page name differs from the page's own — which makes `effectivePageName` an ancestor walk, per css-page-3 §7.1's rule that `auto` takes the nearest ancestor's non-auto value and the root's `auto` is the unnamed page. For a top-level block, whose ancestors are `body` and `html` at `auto`, the walk answers exactly what the single lookup answered.

The walk runs in document order from the cursor's own top-level block, descending into children that generate block boxes. It does not read forcing from, and does not descend into, absolutely positioned boxes (§3.1's MUST NOT — such a box neither carries forcing nor receives it, and §4.3's name switch is forcing under another name). It does not descend into a float's interior (§2.1 — a forced break inside a parallel flow does not affect the content outside it) and it does not descend into a monolith (I3, and §4.1 in as many words: a forced break inside monolithic content must be ignored); the box's own commands are read in both cases, which is what keeps case 14's declared divergence exactly where the sixth round left it. The earliest position wins, and it must be strictly greater than the cursor's — which is the same rule as the old loop's `i = 1`, said at word granularity rather than at item granularity: the box a page begins with never breaks before itself.

**§3.1.1's propagation is a corollary of the descent rather than a fourth walk.** A `break-before` on a first in-flow child sits at the same word position as its container's start edge, and a `break-after` on a last in-flow child at the same position as its container's end, so the two propagations the rule generates for forced values are found by the descent at the positions they would have been promoted to. The missing `forcedBreakBefore` is therefore never written, and `forcedBreakAfter` — which was the third of the four walks — is deleted, its reading subsumed.

**Consuming the event.** Where the grab used to set `forcedNext = { b, w: 0 }` and cut the feed between items, it now sets `forcedNext = { b, w }` and cuts the feed *at the position*: items after the boundary's block are dropped, and that block's own clone is truncated at the boundary element. The truncation is new in `fragments.js` as `truncateFrom` — remove everything from (or after) the boundary element in document order within the clone, then truncate trailing decoration on the ancestors the cut passes through, which is §5.2's mirror of the leading truncation the module has always done. The boundary element is located in the clone through the feed's own source map, never by a child-index path, because composition inserts its hints into the clone and every path index after one of them lies. Everything downstream is unchanged: a truncated feed that fits writes a Class A forced entry at the found position, one that does not sends the walk to retreat and the event is found again from the new cursor, and the continuation on the next page is the `mutateToContinuation` the module already provides.

**Conditions at a position.** `conditionsFor` read the cursor block's own name; it reads the effective name at the cursor's *position* now — the deepest in-flow block containing that word — because a page may now begin inside a block whose interior named a different page. Name and forcing stay one mechanism in the grab, as they have been since the third round.

**The composition region ends at the next name switch, found by the same descent.** A name switch is the only forcing that changes the measure, so it is the only forcing a composition region must stop at: lines past a forced break are composed at the right width and simply unused, while lines past a name switch are not. The region therefore runs from the cursor's block through the block containing the next switch, inclusive, and the block that straddles the switch is recomposed at the next page's width through the cache's width key — C7 suspended locally at the switch, exactly as the tablet says.

**Refused: the fourth propagation walk alone.** It is the small, obvious half, and it closes two of the case's three claims while leaving the third red — a `break-after` in the middle of a section is neither a first child's `break-before` nor a last child's `break-after`, so no propagation rule reaches it however many walks are written. A fix that leaves the case red on a claim the case exists to make is not a fix.

**Refused: changing the feed's granularity away from `topLevelBlocks`.** The other branch the case round named. It was refused because nothing needs it: the position index, the presentation's `posBlock` literal, the account, the differential's judge measure and the harness's own ground truth all key on the top-level block, and the descent gives the grab word-granular reach without moving any of them. Forcing is found at word granularity; the feed stays what it was.

**Refused: amending the tablet.** C1 already requires this, and the architecture's grab paragraph is what was narrower than the law. The change is a shape, so it lands in [`architecture.md`](https://github.com/egildo/beltype/blob/42b348b547d269a217a7f193042fe216386a4650/packages/engine-cicero/docs/architecture.md).

**Refused, and recorded as owed: §5.2's preserved margin after an interior forced break.** A forced break preserves the margins after it, and the driver has honoured that since 2026-07-29 through `cursor.forced`. It still does wherever a forced break lands at a block's start, which is everywhere it could land until this entry. Where one now lands *inside* a block, the continuation is built by `mutateToContinuation`, which truncates leading decoration unconditionally — correct for the unforced mid-block cut it was written for, and one notch conservative here. Fixing it means plumbing a preserve flag through the shared fragment path and the account into presentation, which is a second change to the same organ in a round that already has one moving; the measure and the presentation build continuations with the same code, so the two cannot disagree and no page is wrong about itself. It goes to the checklist's frontier.

**Refused, and stated so nobody reads it as an oversight: parity forcing and §4.3's combination rule.** `left`/`right`/`recto`/`verso` are still consumed as forced and their parity is still unbuilt, on both sides of the differential (P26c–P26e). The combination rule is downstream of parity. Neither moves here.

**Refused: corpus v5.** The generator's flatness is why this survived, and drawing carriers below the top level is owed — but a generator change rewrites seeds 600 and up along with their records, and this round has one thing moving already. It stays in the `commands` chapter's wanted register beside `forcing-both-spellings-drawn`, which waits there for the same reason.

## Predictions

Frozen on write, break-versus-hold decided in advance. The two baselines below were measured on this branch **before `src/` was touched**, by driving the real dev server under `--engine cicero` at DPR 1 and reading the engine's own `window.__beltypeCicero.last`.

1. **Case 19 goes green.** Eight pages, `native.partition: identical`, all three `startsAPage` claims holding, in the native and the composed mode alike. *Breaks* on any check failing.
2. **Every other standing tally in `gates/records.js` is unmoved.** The corpus is flat by construction — the generator emits one block per body line and draws no out-of-flow box at all — and cases 00 through 18 carry every forced value and every page name on a child of the body, so the descent finds exactly what the old loop found. *Breaks* on any tally moving; a moved tally is a claim in conflict, and the specification is read before either side is touched.
3. **`test/specimen.md` under Cicero stays at 6 pages.** Its forcing is `break-before: recto` and `page: chapter` on `section:has(> h1)`, which is already a top-level block, so the descent changes nothing there. *Breaks* if the count moves, which would mean the specimen's shape was misread. **Recorded, because it corrects a number in circulation:** the baseline is **6 pages and 195 flow words**, not the seven pages [`.claude/NEEDED-FIXES.md`](https://github.com/egildo/beltype/blob/42b348b547d269a217a7f193042fe216386a4650/.claude/NEEDED-FIXES.md) §3.1b quotes. That seven was never re-measured by the sections investigation — the investigation cited the entry rather than the document — and this is the measurement the entry's page-count half has been waiting for. It does not settle the lead: four `break-before: recto` chapters against unbuilt parity forcing gives 6 + 4 = 10 where Paged.js prints 11, which is one page closer to arithmetic than seven was and still not a finding.
4. **`docs/index.md` under Cicero gains pages.** Its `h2 { break-before: page }` is declared inside sections and is honoured for the first time. The baseline is **105 pages**. *Breaks* if the count does not rise.
5. **The `--view` differential holds** — the presented pages ARE the Ledger's intervals — on case 19 and on the three view gates' seeds. An interior forced cut is an ordinary Class A cut mid-block, which the Stonehand already produces and the Chase already presents, so presentation should need nothing. *Breaks* on any view mismatch, which would mean the claim that presentation needs nothing was an assumption rather than a reading.

## Verdict

**Prediction 1 held.** `cases/19-forcing-under-sections` holds in both modes: eight pages presented and printed against Chromium's own eight, `native.partition` identical — *page for page and word for word with Chromium* — and all three `startsAPage` claims landing, *Chapter two, opened from inside its section* on page 4, *Quis nostrum exercitationem ullam corporis suscipit laboriosam* on page 5, *The plate, named from a heading inside a section* on page 6. Nine checks in the native mode, seven in the composed, none failing.

**Prediction 2 held, on the second run.** A complete battery of 25 gates in 19.9 minutes reproduced every standing record: native 163 agree / 18 legal-divergence / 7 theirs-illegal / 11 unclassified / 1 reference-invalid; composed-v1 89 / 8 / 3; composed-v2 86 / 10 / 4; v3-native 43 / 5 / 1 unclassified / 1 theirs-illegal; v3-composed 28 / 1 / 1; v4 43 / 4 / 2 unclassified / 1 theirs-illegal; the three view gates 50, 40 and 30 view-agree; every pass gate ok. `cases` is the only gate that moved, red to green, which is what the case round built it to do. **On the first run it did not hold, and that is the entry's most useful sentence** — see the second defect below.

**Prediction 3 held, and it caught the first defect.** `test/specimen.md` under the seam is 6 pages and 195 flow words after the descent, exactly as before it. It was **7** on the first cut of the code, and the prediction is the only reason anyone looked.

**Prediction 4 held.** `docs/index.md` under the seam goes from **105 pages to 136**, its `h2 { break-before: page }` honoured for the first time. It also got substantially faster — **8153 ms to about 1100 ms** — which was not predicted and is not a mystery: a feed truncated at the next command measures far less content per page than one poured to the end of a 44 529-word document. `test/index.md` (6 pages) and `test/bleed.md` (2 pages) were measured beside them.

**Prediction 5 held.** All three view gates are at their recorded counts, and case 19's presented page count equals its printed one in both modes. Presentation needed nothing, as claimed — but the claim was checked rather than assumed, which is the reason it was frozen.

**Too good to be true, checked — and it was not, twice.**

**The first defect: a forced `break-after` resumed on the wrong side of a block seam.** The walk emitted the event at the closing box's *end* position and left the cursor there. That is the same flat word position as the next box's start and it is not the same place: the cursor is a block-and-word pair, `conditionsFor` reads the block tree at it, and the end of block *b* and the start of block *b+1* read differently. On the specimen — whose second table of contents ends with `break-after: page` — a cursor parked at that nav's end made the first paragraph of the chapter after it look like a named-page switch, and forced a page nobody asked for. Seven where six was owed. A forced `break-after` is carried forward as pending now and spent on the box that resumes, which also retires the document-end guard for free: a command with nothing after it reaches no next box and so commands nothing, which is the item-granular grab's own silence kept rather than replaced.

**The second defect: the same mistake again, one layer down, and only the battery could see it.** *Strictly after the cursor* was asked of the flat position. The position index's header says why that is wrong in its own words — **the word space under-determines block seams** — and a shell or Class C end-gap cut leaves the cursor at block *b*'s last word, which is block *b+1*'s first position. The descent answered *not after* about a box the page had not begun with, and dropped its `break-before`. The first battery run said so in the only currency it has: five seeds moved from `agree` to `legal-divergence` on the `native` gate and two on `composed-v1`, in exactly the neighbourhoods where a Class C end-gap cut precedes a flat `.force` paragraph. Asked of the block-and-word pair, both gates return to their records. **Neither defect was findable by reading**, and both were found by an instrument that had been aimed in advance: the first by a frozen prediction about a document nobody expected to move, the second by a tally nobody was allowed to edit.

**The specimen's seven pages were never a measurement.** [`.claude/NEEDED-FIXES.md`](https://github.com/egildo/beltype/blob/42b348b547d269a217a7f193042fe216386a4650/.claude/NEEDED-FIXES.md) §3.1b has quoted seven since the beta round, and the sections investigation cited the entry rather than re-measuring the document. It is six, on both sides of this fix. That does not settle the parity lead — 6 + 4 = 10 against Paged.js's eleven, one page closer to arithmetic than seven was — and the entry now carries the measured number instead of the inherited one.

**What was not run, and why nothing rides on it:** the book-scale benchmark, which is not a gate and whose numbers are machine-dependent. The manual's 8.2 s to 1.1 s is the same phenomenon observed on a real document and is recorded above as an observation, not as a record.

## Consequences

The grab is the one organ that moves. [`architecture.md`](https://github.com/egildo/beltype/blob/42b348b547d269a217a7f193042fe216386a4650/packages/engine-cicero/docs/architecture.md)'s build loop says forcing is found by descent at word granularity, monoliths and parallel flows excluded, and that §3.1.1's propagation is a corollary of it. The [checklist](https://github.com/egildo/beltype/blob/42b348b547d269a217a7f193042fe216386a4650/packages/engine-cicero/docs/css-paged-media-checklist.md)'s §3.1.1 row stops naming a missing fourth walk and says what was built instead; its §4 break-properties row gains the 2026-09-03 correction beside the 2026-09-02 and 2026-07-27 ones; the flat corpus stays a design open, and §5.2's preserved margin after an interior forced break joins it.

[`.claude/NEEDED-FIXES.md`](https://github.com/egildo/beltype/blob/42b348b547d269a217a7f193042fe216386a4650/.claude/NEEDED-FIXES.md)'s §3.1c moves to *Already fixed in this line*, citing this entry by name. Its §3.1b keeps its lead and gains the measured baseline that replaces the seven pages it quotes; §3.1d, the word-space seam, is untouched and still open.

`fragments.js` gains `truncateFrom` and `truncateTrailingDecoration` — the trailing mirror of a leading truncation it has carried since the third round — and `driver.js` loses `forcedBreakAfter`, whose reading the descent subsumes.

The venue's twentieth case, [`cases/19-forcing-under-sections`](https://github.com/egildo/beltype/blob/42b348b547d269a217a7f193042fe216386a4650/packages/essay/cases/19-forcing-under-sections/README.md), stops being red, and its README says when and under which record.
