# The lines that cannot win are not measured

**Status: Decided 2026-10-12, Executed 2026-10-12.** The `cicero-optimisation` round's C5; the brief was `.claude/prior-art/reports/break-search-order-2026-10-09.md` section 3 (the middle ground, in Cicero's terms), and the numbers below are the round's own.

## Context

*The page asks the index* stopped the Stonehand's walk at the edge, so a page enumerates only the boxes from its cursor to the first in-flow box whose top lies below the limit. What it still did for every one of them was ask `report.linesOf(box)`, a `Range.getClientRects` per word, to offer a Class B reading between each pair of lines, and `endGapOf`, which lines a leaf a second time (cached). On the BGD manual `cicero:adjudicate` was 115 to 131 ms of a 450 to 490 ms full run, of which the collection was 95 and the lines inside it 64 to 67: `linesOf` was asked of 2 467 boxes for 786 between-line candidates. The ranking was 4 ms and the driver's own work after the verdict about 12.

Almost all of that measuring could not change the cut. The make-up order is lexicographic, `(avoid, ow, -y, -docIndex)`, and when the page offers a clean reading that fits at some height `y*` (no avoid spent, no orphans or widows shortfall), a between-line reading of a box that stands wholly above `y*` has a lower `y`, so on the third key it loses to the reading at `y*` whatever its own first two say. The boxes that matter are the ones whose lines can reach `y*`: the straddler and what stands below the last clean boundary, a handful a page.

## Decision

**The collection runs in two phases, and the second only as far as it can matter.** `collect` offers first everything that needs no line measure: Class A (the boundary between blocks), the start gap and the end gap of Class C, and the rows. A box that would offer between-line readings holds a place for them in the candidate list (`Pending`) in the order its readings would stand. `cleanFloorOf` then takes `y*` over the readings so far, filtered exactly as `decide` filters (`fitsOf`, now shared: the fit with §5.3's cost in it, `y` below the flow's start, no edge cut, and, where a row has a forced break in one of its items, nothing the row leaves out), and every place is either expanded into its readings (`betweenLines`, the code as it was) or dropped. A place is dropped when `reachOf(box) < y*`, strictly. With no clean reading that fits there is no `y*` and every place is expanded: the cost is what it was, by design. The order of the list is the order it had, so a tie in the sort resolves as before. `decide` is untouched but for taking its filter from `fitsOf`.

**`reachOf`, the Gauge's.** The argument needs a bound on how low a box's between-line readings can stand, and the bound has to come without measuring the lines. A reading's `y` is the bottom of a line that is not the box's last, so it lies a line's height above the bottom of its box when the box holds its content, and the box's border-box bottom is the bound. It is not the bound when the content overflows the box (a `height` or `max-height` shorter than the lines, a flex item shrunk under them): the lines then stand below the box, and a reading there can be lower than a clean boundary that the box's rect says is further down. The Gauge's `reachOf(box)` answers the rect's bottom, and infinity where the element's `scrollHeight` exceeds its `clientHeight`, a property read that measures no word. An inline box and an anonymous box are answered by their rect (an inline box's is the union of its fragments, an anonymous box's is its lines' own extent). A doubt is infinity: the box is measured. The rule that the line bottoms lie within the box is the Gauge's and is stated there; the unit tests hold the failure it guards (`stonehand.test.js`: with the doubt withheld, the cut is 90 where the line at 94 is the answer).

**The end gap is asked only past the edge** (a pure reordering of a test the code already made). `endGapOf` of a leaf lines it, so it is asked only of a box whose border-box end lies past the limit, which is where its Class C could ever be offered. The plan had this reorder as refuted for the single-phase collection, where the lines were cached anyway (0.3 ms); in the two-phase one it is what keeps the first phase from measuring every stack, so it is part of the change, and it moves no candidate.

### Why the verdict is identical, claim by claim

1. *A skipped reading cannot win.* Let `c0` be the reading that sets `y*`: it fits, violates nothing, and survives `fitsOf`'s row filter (a forced row removes the readings below its top, and a floor taken before the filter could be set by a reading it removes). A skipped reading `b` has `y_b <= reach < y*`. Then `c0`'s key is `(0, 0, -y*, ...)` and `b`'s is `(avoid_b, ow_b, -y_b, ...)`; `0 <= avoid_b`, `0 <= ow_b` and `-y* < -y_b`, so `c0` sorts strictly before `b` and `b` is not the minimum. No claim about the order of the walk or the monotonicity of `y` in it is made: it is a dominance over the set, and strict, so an equal-`y` reading with a later `docIndex`, which would win the tie, is never skipped.
2. *The fits are the same set where it matters.* `cleanFloorOf` and `decide` call the one `fitsOf`, so a reading that sets the floor is a reading `decide` ranks; a floor taken over readings the row filter or the cost removes would skip boxes the verdict needs. Both are in the tests (a forced row beyond which a clean boundary stands; a cloned border pushing a boundary past the foot).
3. *The fallbacks are unreachable when anything is skipped.* The edge's row, the straddler and the `beyond` boundary are reached only when nothing fits, and a floor means a reading fits.
4. *Rows and forced breaks.* A row is a first-phase reading; a forced row's candidates are built unpruned; the forced selection in `flowVerdict` reads `forced`, which no between-line reading has. A row item's own collection is the same code at its own limit.
5. *The bound.* Measured 2026-10-12, over a full run in each mode of the BGD manual and of BelType's manual: the second-last line's bottom stands at least 16 px (BGD) and 22 px (the manual) above its box's bottom, in every box with two lines or more. The only overshoot found was 1.77 px, in a one-line paragraph, which offers no between-line reading. The overflow guard exists for the box those documents do not contain.

## Consequences

`collect`'s doc and `gauge.js`'s `reachOf` say what the code does; the architecture's Stonehand section carries a paragraph. Two tests guard the claim from two sides: `stonehand.test.js` runs every case, a fuzz of three thousand random pages (keeps, orphans and widows, cloned borders, end gaps, overflowing boxes, limits anywhere) and the hand cases for a forced row, the cost and the overflow through a stand-in report whose `reachOf` says infinity, which makes the collection measure everything, and asserts the cuts equal. A mutation of any of the five parts (the floor over the unfiltered fits, without the cost, over a reading that is not clean, a non-strict test, `reachOf` without the guard) fails a test.

The measures are kept: `cicero:before-verdict`, `cicero:stonehand` (the call itself), `cicero:collect` (detail: boxes visited, stacks lined and skipped, the milliseconds the lines and the end gaps took), `cicero:decide` and `cicero:address`, so the next round reads the break search in its parts. `.claude/plans/cicero-optimisation/bench/break-search-measure.mjs` sums them.

## Verdict

(Frozen in the plan: about 42 ms a full run on the BGD manual and 1 371 boxes skipped; about 1 ms an edit.)

**Verdict identity, by the check that runs both collections.** For the round a temporary line in the driver ran `adjudicate` a second time on every adjudicated page through a report whose `reachOf` said infinity and compared the two verdicts field by field (kind, box and its position, `y`, `k`, `T`, class, what it violates, its cost, a row's items): 263 of 263 pages on BelType's manual in the default mode and 268 of 268 composed, 120 of 120 on the BGD manual in either mode, 5 of 5 on the feature showcase `test/index.md` in either mode, none different. The specimen adjudicates nothing (its pages end at forced breaks). The line is not shipped.

**Cut identity.** Every page's text hashed after `buildAll()`, before and after, in both modes: BelType's manual 280 and 285 pages, the BGD manual 123 and 123, the specimen 9 and 9, byte for byte the same hashes.

On the BGD manual the full run's adjudication fell 36 ms (the plan expected 42): 39 ms in the lines and the end gaps together (the straddlers' lines now ride the end gap's call, cached for the Class B that follows) less about 3 for the floor and the places held. The whole pagination moved less than the run-to-run noise (450 to 427 ms default, 1 669 to 1 661 composed on the BGD manual; the manual's 2.4 s moved by less than its spread): the lines were a tenth of it. In the editing tools, the BGD edit loop (`bench-edit.mjs`, eight edits, three runs) read median wall 253, 241, 246 ms before and 244, 246, 239 after, the resumed pagination 42 ms in both: not slower, and the roughly 1 ms the plan expected is below what it can resolve.

**The driver's work after the verdict, measured and not touched.** `cicero:address` is 11.6 ms on the BGD manual default (26.8 composed) and 21.4 on BelType's (39.6 composed). Almost all of it is `nestedFlows`: the walk down the resume block's procession that looks for parallel flows declared inside it (6.7 of 6.9 ms of `carriedFlows`; 21.9 of 22 composed), which reads a computed style of every block child of the block the page resumes in, on every page, and finds none in these documents. `placesOf`, `within` and `flowPlaceOf` are never reached (0.0 ms: no document here has a carried float or an unverdicted row), and `flattenResume` is 0.2 to 1.0 ms. The cost is the resume block's size, which is why composed (where one block is the whole chapter) pays three times more. What would buy it is a question for another round: the boxes `nestedFlows` can find are the ones whose computed `float` or `position` is not static, and a once-per-run list of them, in the manner of the forcing index, would make the walk a lookup.
