# The line is read by overlap

**Status: Decided 2026-09-29, Executed 2026-09-29.** Convened by the fix round for cases 73 and 74, on the branch `cicero/the-line-is-read-by-overlap` cut from `c6c198b`; one of three parallel branches, this one owning `gauge.js`'s line reading and nothing else. The rivals were read before any source changed. The pitch under `normal` was ruled by Egildo on 2026-09-29: the Gauge reads the pitch; the tablet's intake normalisation stays as ruled and unbuilt.

## Context

`gauge.js`'s `computeLines` is the one organ that turns word rects into line boxes, and it read a line in two steps that each trusted one number. It **homed** a word onto the line above it when the word's rect top lay within `LINE_CLUSTER_TOLERANCE`, 1.5px, of that line's FIRST top; and it **built** the line box from that first word's rect centred on `parseFloat(getComputedStyle(box.el).lineHeight)`. Two cases, filed from two readings on 2026-09-27, found each step's fault.

- **[`packages/essay/cases/73-the-smaller-span-in-the-line`](https://github.com/egildo/beltype/blob/42b348b547d269a217a7f193042fe216386a4650/packages/essay/cases/73-the-smaller-span-in-the-line/README.md).** An inline `<code>` at 0.75em in the text's own face stands on its baseline 3.0px below the text's rect top (BelType's manual's own number) and 4px shorter. Mid-line, it opened a second line and the text after it, 3px higher, joined that one, so one rendered line was two to the Gauge and both modes cut the page between them (`class B, k 11, y 240, word 23`). First on the line, it did not split the line, but the line box was built from its rect, a pixel low, and the whole line moved to the next page. One misreading, two faces: a line was read off its first word.
- **[`packages/essay/cases/74-the-line-height-nobody-set`](https://github.com/egildo/beltype/blob/42b348b547d269a217a7f193042fe216386a4650/packages/essay/cases/74-the-line-height-nobody-set/README.md).** A paragraph whose own line-height is `normal` — written, or reset without being named by the `font` shorthand — answers `getComputedStyle` with the string `normal`, so every line box was `top − (NaN − height) / 2`. The Stonehand offered a Class B point after each line, its fit test refused every `NaN`, and the page stopped before the paragraph: three pages against Chromium's two, no word lost, nothing said. A `normal` paragraph taller than a page had no Class A boundary to fall back on and was sliced at the edge as a last resort, through a line, its continuations creeping 2px up a page. The root is refused by name when its line-height is not a length (`measure.js`'s `assertRenderConditions`); a descendant never was, and the comment *"(I5 guarantees it exists)"* promised what the check does not give.

Both touch the same function, so the round makes them one reading of a line.

## What the rivals do

Read before designing, at the paths the case agents named.

**fragmentainers** (`/Users/egildo/Privata/dev/fragmentainers/package/src/measurement/line-box.js`, read through `algorithms/inline-content.js`'s `#layoutLines`) is the model. `reduceRectsToLines` puts a rect on a line when `sharesLine` finds it overlapping the line's anchor rect by more than half the shorter of the two, and the comment names this construct: *"main text plus `<sub>`, `<sup>`, `<small>` or smaller font-size runs"*. The line's ink top is its topmost rect (`Math.min`), not its first. `computeLineExtents` then sizes every line but the last from the gap between consecutive ink tops, the last from the block's content box checked against a ceiling (at least the strut, at most its own ink plus a strut of leading), and `liftShortLines` moves sub-strut residue back. `getLineHeight` resolves `normal` by measuring: the gap between the last two line tops, else the first line's rect height, else the font size, floored to a whole pixel at a device pixel ratio of 1 because Blink floors it there. **Taken**: the overlap predicate, word for word; the topmost rect as the line's top; `normal` measured from the layout rather than from the property; the smallest gap as the pitch, which is their `strutFloor`'s reasoning (a gap is a rendered line, so the smallest bounds the strut from above); and, for a box of one line, the content box checked against a ceiling. **Refused**: line extents from the gaps between ink tops for every box — an explicit line-height keeps the centred arithmetic, because that arithmetic is what every standing record was measured on and it is byte-identical for an ordinary line; the DPR-1 floor, because this engine reads a layout the browser has already rounded, so flooring would be a second model of Blink's rounding on top of Blink's own; the last two tops as the pitch, which a taller last line inflates; and the first line's rect height as the one-line fallback, which is short of the line by the font's line gap (measured below: Arial at 12px has a 12px rect on a 14px line).

**pagedNG** (`/Users/egildo/Privata/dev/pagedjs`) fragments through fragmentainers (`src/index.js` re-exports its `Fragmenter`), so its answer is the one above.

**Paged.js classic** (`/Users/egildo/Privata/dev/pagedjs-classic/src/chunker/layout.js`) never groups rects into lines. `findOverflow` descends to the first overflowing node and `textBreak` walks the words of an overflowing text node, breaking at the first word whose own bounding rect's top lies past the page bottom, or whose bottom does, and then letter by letter. Each word is asked about its own glyphs, never about its line box, so a smaller span mid-line cannot split a line there and `normal` means nothing to it. **The same walk carries a mirrored risk of its own**, by reading and not measured: a span set smaller than its text and FIRST on a line, in a text node of its own, whose glyph bottom lies above the page bottom where the text's lies below, fits, and the break falls after it — mid-line, the face of this fault that case 73's README measured on this engine with the span first. Reading rects word by word is not reading lines.

**WeasyPrint** (`/Users/egildo/Privata/dev/weasyprint/weasyprint`) lays out its own lines: `layout/inline.py`'s `line_box_verticality` and `inline_box_verticality` place every inline box on the line by its baseline, so the line box's height is computed from all of them; `text/line_break.py`'s `strut` lays a single space out through Pango and, for `normal`, uses that line's logical height; `layout/block.py`'s `_linebox_layout` tests `line.position_y + line.height` against the page. It never reads a line back from rects. **Taken**: the shape of the answer — a line box is the union of the inline boxes on it, each on its own line-height, which is CSS 2.1 §10.8.1 — read here backwards from the rects the browser drew.

## Decision

**A line is read by overlap, and its box is the union of what stands on it.** A word joins the line above it when its rect shares that line's extent — they overlap by more than half the shorter of the two — and opens a line otherwise; the line's extent is every rect homed onto it, so its top is its topmost rect. The line box is the union of the inline boxes on the line (CSS 2.1 §10.8.1): each text word's rect, the content area of the face it is set in, centred on the line-height it is set on — the box's own pitch for the box's own text, its parent element's line-height for text inside an inline element, and no leading where that is a keyword — extended to hold every atom on the line. And where the box's own line-height computes to `normal`, its pitch is measured: the smallest distance between consecutive lines' strut rects.

Four clauses, and how they compose.

1. **Homing** (`home`, `sharesLine`). The predicate is the rival's. An atom's test is *the procession stops at an atom*'s, unchanged — it joins the line whose box it overlaps at all, since an atom's box lies inside its line box even where it lies outside the line's text ink (a small box set `vertical-align: top` does) — so the two rules compose by kind: a text word asks the line's extent, an atom asks the line's box. The special branch for text standing beside an atom that opened its line is gone, because it is the overlap rule's own answer: a text rect beside an atom overlaps the atom's box by its whole height. `LINE_CLUSTER_TOLERANCE` is retired: the overlap rule replaces it here and in `piecesOf`, which clusters one word's own rects into the lines it is set on by the same predicate and finds where each later line begins as the first character whose ink no longer shares the line before — so a word read in pieces (*a line inside a word is a break point*) and a line read in words agree about what a line is. No tolerance is kept.
2. **The line box** (`lineBoxOf`). On a line of one face and one line-height the union is one rect centred on the line-height, which is exactly what the Gauge computed from the first word before this round, so an ordinary line's box is unmoved to the bit. On case 73's line the text's box, 220 to 240px, contains the span's, which is set on `line-height: 1`, and the line stays 20px; on the same span with the text's 20px inherited, the span's box reaches 241px, as Chromium's does (its line is 21px, measured below). The atom clause is the same union with the atom's box.
3. **The pitch** (`pitchOf`). A length where the property is one. Under `normal`, the CSSOM answers the keyword and the length exists only in the layout, so it is read there: the text words are homed once on their own, each line's strut rect taken — its tallest rect of the box's own text (`own`: a text node whose parent is the box, set in the box's own face, so its rect is the strut's content area), or its tallest text rect where it holds none — and the pitch is the smallest distance between consecutive ones. The smallest, because a line holding something taller than the font widens only its own gap. It is not floored (above). **A box of one line** has no distance to read, and takes its content box's height, which is its one line box's when its height is automatic (CSS 2.1 §10.6.3), wherever that lies between the line's rect and twice it; outside that — a `height`, a `min-height`, a stretch — the content box is not the line's, and the rect itself is taken. Nothing is written on the source: *The Line*'s intake normalisation — *"its computed value is read once from the oracle and written back explicit"* — stays ruled and unbuilt, and this reads the same value without writing it (zero footprint; *the intake writes on the source* is an open concern of its own in NEEDED-FIXES).
4. **What stays refused.** `measure.js`'s `assertRenderConditions` still refuses a document root whose line-height is not in pixels, unchanged: the root carries the DPR ruling's promise, and NEEDED-FIXES' *the line-height check reaches the root and no further* stays true and stays by design. What changes is that the Gauge no longer relies on the check for a descendant, which it never covered.

## The audit — every consumer of a line's `top`, `bottom` or height

**Changed**: `computeLines` (all four clauses) and `measureWords` (its word pieces clustered by overlap; each text word now carries `own` and the line-height of the element it is set in, read once per element).

**Read, and unchanged in code** — each consumer reads the same `LineBox` value, now finite and one per rendered line.

- **The Stonehand's Class B candidates** (`stonehand.js`, `y: lines[k].bottom` for `k < T − 1`). `T` now counts rendered lines where a smaller span inflated it by one, so the orphans and widows window is computed over the lines the reader sees; and under `normal` every `y` is a number.
- **The Stonehand's fit test** (`c.y + (c.cost ?? 0) <= limit + eps`). It refused every `NaN` candidate silently; it now ranks them like any other. The 0.25px tolerance is untouched.
- **The row's progress test** (`stonehand.js`, `report.linesOf(d)[0].bottom <= limit + eps`) reads the first line's bottom the same way.
- **`endGapOf`** (`base` is the last line's `bottom`). Under `normal` it was `NaN`, so no end gap existed; it is now the last line's bottom, which for an automatic-height box is the content bottom (measured below: no phantom gap on any face). Where a smaller span on an inherited line-height makes the last line taller, the base follows it down, as Chromium's line does. Its caller `gapAt` is another agent's this round and was not touched.
- **The driver's addressing** (`driver.js`'s `placesOf`: `shown = lines.filter((l) => l.bottom <= cutY + eps)`, then `placeAt(report.wordsOf(b), shown)`; and the Class B mint, `placeAt(report.wordsOf(v.box), v.k + 1)`). `placeAt` takes the first word homed on the line after the cut, so with the span homed onto the text's line the resume is the next rendered line's first word — `word 25`, `p0w024`, where it was `word 23`, the span.
- **The Compositor** (`compose-dom.js`) reads no Gauge line: it measures widths through Ranges and chooses lines itself. What reads its lines against the Gauge is the venue's composed cross-check (`packages/essay/lib/client.js`: the Gauge's `linesOf` count per composed paragraph against the Compositor's chosen count), which now counts a line holding a smaller span once.
- **The presentation** (`positions.js`, `fragments.js`) places words by address — a path, a word index, a character — and never reads line geometry; it is reached only through the addresses above.
- **`rectTop`** is now the line's topmost rect; nothing reads it but the Stonehand suite's fixtures.

## Consequences

- **The strut on a line holding none of the box's own text is invisible.** A line set wholly inside a smaller span reads the span's inline box alone, where the line box also holds the strut; a line inside an `<em>` of the same face is exact, because its rects are the strut's.
- **Under `normal`, an odd leading is split evenly** by the centred arithmetic where Blink puts the odd pixel below: half a pixel, measured on Arial and Times. The alternative — every line box from the gap between consecutive tops, the rival's `computeLineExtents` — would move the arithmetic every standing record was taken on, for half a pixel on a keyword the venue forbids.
- **A descendant inline under `normal`** — a span in another face inside a `normal` paragraph — contributes its rect with no leading: its own `normal` line-height is not measurable from one line.
- **A one-line `normal` box whose height is constrained** reads its rect as its line, a pixel short at each end on Arial at 12px.
- **Lines set on less than about half their own content area merge**: two rects overlapping by more than half of the shorter are one line to the predicate, which is its stated ceiling in the rival too. No document in the venue sets a line-height under 0.6.
- **Cost, unmeasured**: one `getComputedStyle` per distinct element that holds text in a measured box, read once per report; the box walk already asks one per element.
- **The tablet's intake normalisation** is still ruled and unbuilt, and the checklist's *`line-height: normal` DPR leak* says so. The root check stays by design. The tablet is untouched.
