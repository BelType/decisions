# The cut divides its ancestors

**Status: Decided 2026-09-29, Executed 2026-09-29.** The law was ruled by Egildo. The fix round for cases 71 and 75, on the branch `cicero/the-cut-divides-its-ancestors` cut from `cicero/five-cases-earned` (`c6c198b`), where both cases were authored red; the rivals were read before any source changed. It closes, beside the two cases, the venue's own *a refused case reads two checks green over nothing*.

## Context

A continuation is dressed: a fragment that resumes a box carries no leading margin, no indent, under `slice` no leading border or padding, and none of what the box generates at its beginning. Two cases found the engine dressing the wrong set of boxes, from opposite sides.

[`packages/essay/cases/71-the-box-that-begins-a-page`](https://github.com/egildo/beltype/blob/42b348b547d269a217a7f193042fe216386a4650/packages/essay/cases/71-the-box-that-begins-a-page/README.md) dressed too many. `fragments.js`'s `mutateToContinuation`, in its bare-path branch, dressed the box the page resumes at and every first child below it with the whole of `truncateLeadingDecoration` — margin, indent, border, padding — so a box that opened a page WHOLE, after a Class A cut or a forced break, was printed as the continuation of a box it had never been. It showed on every page of a real venue's manual (the first box of each of 123 pages lost its padding; a contents heading lost a 95px border). The same case found two faults of the start-edge Class C behind it: the Gauge measured a box's start gap from its padding-box top, so a box's own padding was a gap and the Stonehand offered a break point §4.1 does not provide (box c); and a bare path normalises by dropping trailing zeros, so a start-edge Class C minted as the path to a first child collapsed onto the path before its box, and the page before closed ahead of a box it should have ended inside (box d: its border and padding printed on no page).

[`packages/essay/cases/75-the-marker-printed-twice`](https://github.com/egildo/beltype/blob/42b348b547d269a217a7f193042fe216386a4650/packages/essay/cases/75-the-marker-printed-twice/README.md) marked too few. The dressing sheet withheld `::before` and `::first-letter` over `__beltype-cont`, and `root.js` put that class on the top-level clone alone, so a list item or a paragraph divided inside a `<section>` — every BelType document's shape — printed its marker, its `::first-line` and its `::before` again on the page that continued it. The sheet said nothing of `::marker` or `::first-line` at all.

The two are one question: **which boxes does a cut divide?** Case 71 answers it at the dressing of a box's border; case 75 at the dressing of what it generates.

## Decision

**A cut names a place in the tree. The boxes it divides are the proper ancestors of that place, and nothing else. A box carries its start decoration on a page when the page's opening cut does not divide it, and its end decoration when the closing cut does not. Margins adjoining the cut are truncated (css-break-3 §5.2), forced breaks aside. Class never enters the dressing; class only says whether the place exists.**

Read against the classes, which follow from it rather than beside it: a Class A cut and a forced break name the place BEFORE a box, so that box is whole and only its ancestors are divided; a start-edge Class C names the place between a box's content edge and its first in-flow child, so the box itself is divided; a Class B and an end-gap Class C name a place inside the box, which is divided.

Built in five places.

1. **The dressing walks the proper ancestors, and nothing else** (`fragments.js`). `mutateToContinuation`'s shared branch dresses the clone and the chain above the target with `truncateLeadingDecoration`, and a Class B leaf with it too, as before. The target of a bare path is not divided, so it gets `truncateAdjoiningMargin`: its `margin-top` and that of each first child whose margin would collapse through it into the break, the descent stopping at the first box with a top border or padding, where CSS 2.1 §8.3.1 stops the collapse. Border, padding, `text-indent` and generated content stay. The first-child chain is the one [*the cut resumes at the box*](the-cut-resumes-at-the-box.md) measured as load-bearing (native seeds 71 and 173); it is narrowed to the one property that chain carries into the break, and the differential says the seeds did not move (below). The END side needed no change: `truncateFrom` and `consumeTrailingWords` already dressed only the chain above the place.
2. **The dressing marks what it dresses** (`fragments.js`, `root.js`). `truncateLeadingDecoration` adds `__beltype-cont` and `truncateTrailingDecoration` adds `__beltype-cut`, so one walk dresses and marks, at every depth. The two constants live in `fragments.js` and `root.js` imports them. `root.js` still marks every top-level fragment itself: that mark is also the identity Caster's adapter reads (`isContinuation` over a page's flow), and a painted shell of a top-level block returns a slice wrapper nothing in `fragments.js` dresses. The two-edge distinction is kept at every depth: a fragment that RESUMES a box withholds its beginnings, a fragment the cut passes THROUGH withholds its `::after`, a middle fragment carries both marks.
3. **The dressing sheet withholds all four beginnings** (`root.js`). Added beside `::before` and `::first-letter`: `.__beltype-cont::marker { content: none !important }` and `.__beltype-cont::first-line { all: unset !important }`. Measured in Chromium 153 on a scratch page: `content: none` on `::marker` withholds a `square`, a `decimal` and an author's `::marker { content: "…" }` placed inside (the text moved to the content edge, 62→40, 69→40, 88→40px), and case 75 proves a `square` placed outside; no `list-style` was needed, which is what reaches the author's own `::marker` content that `list-style: none` would leave. `all: unset` on `::first-line` is honoured: a first line set at 40px on a 50px line came back to the paragraph's own 20px (glyph height 46→18px, paragraph 90→60px), so no property had to be named.
4. **The start gap is §4.1's** (`gauge.js`'s `gapAt`, start branch; `stonehand.js`). §4.1's Class C lies *"between the content edge of a block container box and the outer edges of its child content (margin edges of block-level children …) if there is a (non-zero) gap between them"*. So the gap is measured from the CONTENT edge to the first IN-FLOW child's MARGIN edge: padding lies outside the content edge and is never a gap (case 65's correction read again at the box's other end), a child's margin is its outer edge and is never a gap, and a float or an absolutely positioned child stands in no procession, so what stands before the first in-flow child — box d's empty float and the clearance under it — is gap. The candidate's `y` is the place the cut names, the content edge plus the gap, which is the first in-flow child's outer top; it rides out on the verdict with that child (`child`) and the gap (`gap`), so the driver mints the path from what the Stonehand priced and derives nothing again.
5. **A trailing zero collapses only where no gap stands there** (`positions.js`, `driver.js`). Before a first child is the same place as before its parent in the word space, and in the geometry too wherever nothing stands between the two — every Class A point, every forced break. A start-edge Class C exists only where something does, so `cutTo` takes `gap: true` from the start-edge branch and mints the path unnormalised: `[…X, 0]` keeps its zero, `divides()` reads true, the page before ends INSIDE X showing its border and padding, and the page after resumes X stripped with its first child whole. And the gap is not extent the page USED: what stands there gives the box no height once the child after it has left the first fragment, so the account's `usedPx` stops at the content edge and §5.3's extension — *"its content box extends to fill any remaining fragmentainer extent"* — carries the divided box to the cut. The flow's clip is the same sum either way (`usedPx + extPx`); without it box d's first fragment was 15pt tall where Chromium's is 30pt.

`positions.js`'s header now states the law as its sentence and the class list as its consequence.

## What the rivals do

Read before designing, at the paths the case READMEs named; none was run.

**WeasyPrint** (`layout/block.py`, `block_container_layout`) removes a box's START decoration exactly when the box is resuming — `box.remove_decoration(start=not is_start, …)` with `is_start = skip_stack is None` — and nothing about the child it resumes at: that child's own layout starts fresh, its own decoration its own. A box whose first child cannot be placed on the page aborts and moves whole. So a box that opens a page whole keeps its border and padding, and a box divided before its first child keeps them on the page it began on and drops them on the one that resumes it: the law, by construction, because WeasyPrint has a resumed box tree rather than a dressing. Its `::marker` is the list item's first child box (`build.py`'s `marker_to_box`), so it is laid out on the first page only; its `::first-line`, read not run, is applied to the first line of a resumed box as well (`block_container_layout` passes `first_line_style` whether or not it is starting), so it repeats as Cicero did.

**fragmentainers** (`src/algorithms/block-container.js`) holds a box's block-start inset — padding plus border — as the box's own on its first fragment and repeats it on a continuation only under `clone` (`#clonedStartInset`: *"The first fragment's block-start inset is the box's own"*), and zeroes an empty container's extent when all its children were pushed (*"avoids rendering an empty padding/border shell"*), which is box c moving whole. `Fragment#applySplitAttributes` marks every continuing clone at any depth with `data-split-from`, and `styles/overrides.js` withholds under it `::before`, `::first-letter`, the indent, the top margin and, under `slice`, the top border and padding, and the marker by `li[data-split-from] { list-style: none }`. **Paged.js classic** marks every rebuilt ancestor `data-split-from` (`utils/dom.js`'s `rebuildAncestors`) and `data-split-to` at the other edge, and `polisher/base.js` withholds `::before`, `::first-letter`, the indent, top margin and padding under the first and `::after` under the second, and the marker by `li[data-split-from]:first-of-type { list-style: none }`. **pagedNG** is fragmentainers.

**Taken**: the mark on every divided clone at every depth, which both cloning engines have; the two-edge split, which Paged.js classic spells `split-from`/`split-to` and this engine already spelled `cont`/`cut`; fragmentainers' first-fragment inset and its pushed-container rule, which are this law read from the layout side. **Refused**: `list-style: none` as the marker's withholding — it leaves an author's `::marker { content }` generating, which `content: none` on `::marker` does not. **Gone one better**: none of the four withholds `::first-line`, and css-pseudo-4 §2.1 makes it the block's first formatted line, which a resumed fragment's first line is not; Chromium's own print agrees with the text (case 75), and so does this engine now.

## The audit — every consumer of a bare path, for the new shape `[…X, 0]`

- **`divides`** — true for any path longer than the block's, so `[…X, 0]` divides the block, and the law in `fragments.js` says which boxes below it.
- **`pathAfter`, `cutAfter`** — `[…X, 0]` orders after `[…X]` (a shorter path is the earlier place) and before `[…X, 1]`, so a page opening before X and closing inside it has progressed, and C2's check holds.
- **`comparePos`, `posOfCut`** — the flat position is `wordsBefore(child 0)`, which equals `wordsBefore(X)`; I1 admits an equal position, and the Ledger's order is the address after it.
- **The end-side dressing** — a page that ENDS at `[…X, 0]` is `mutateToFirstFragment`'s bare branch: `truncateFrom(clone, child 0)` puts away child 0 and everything after it and dresses X and its ancestors as fragments that end there, marked `__beltype-cut`; X keeps its top border and padding. The same shape as a Class A cut `[…X, k]` with k > 0, and pinned DOM-free for k = 0 (`test/fragments.test.js`, *a start-edge cut keeps its zero*).
- **`fragmentOf`** — a middle fragment ending at `[…X, 0]` cuts its end first by the same `mutateToFirstFragment`, then its start; the start's put-aways stand before the start's place and cannot reach a box the end marked, which contains the end's place.
- **The hollow put-away** (`cloneInterval`) — X is on the end chain and is built; child 0 is the end's own box and is built, then put away by `truncateFrom`; for a start at `[…X, 0]` nothing stands before child 0 inside X.
- **`constructed`** (`root.js`) — `divides` is true, so the page before presents X's block through a constructed first fragment, exactly as for any divided block.
- **§5.3's extension** (`extensionPx`) — owed, since the cut divides and carries no `px`; the start gap is left out of `usedPx` so the extension carries X's content box to the cut (Decision, 5).
- **`pageOf`, `pageOfPos`** — `{ b, w }` containment: the page closing at `[…X, 0]` ends at `wordsBefore(X)`, so X's first word is on the page after, which is where it prints. A zero-word block is refused there as before.
- **`sourceOf`** — the clone-to-source map; no path is read.
- **The Ledger's projection** — `pageIntervals` reads the block as `cut.path[0]` and `presentationWidths` the same; neither reads the rest of a path.
- **The driver's feed** — `buildFeed` resumes by `mutateToContinuation`, so a resumed child 0 stands at the stripped X's top; `spentLeadOf`'s `gapAbove` reads zero there. The forcing index and the Cast-off mint through `cutTo` without `gap`, so they normalise as before.
- **`putAway`'s engine-mark test** removes a node with a `__beltype-` class rather than concealing it. A divided box now carries one, and is never put away in its own fragment: every box a cut marks contains that cut's place, and what either end puts away stands wholly outside it.
- **Caster** — a nested mark is on an element `pairTrees` pairs with its twin, and the structure walk asks `twinOf` before `transparentOf`, so the `__beltype-` prefix that makes an element transparent is never read for it; `isContinuation` is asked of a page's top-level flow only.

## Consequences

- **Every nested box that opens a page whole keeps its own border, padding, indent and generated content**, which is every page of every BelType document: the belt wraps the body in a `<section>`, so every cut is nested.
- **A start-edge Class C is rarer**: padding no longer offers one, and nor does a first child's margin. It exists where something that takes no place in the procession — a float, an absolutely positioned box, clearance — stands before the first in-flow child, and there it is the cut Chromium takes.
- **The preserved margin after a forced break that lands inside a block** is unchanged and still open (the checklist's own entry): the target's margin is truncated at a forced break as at any other, as before.
- **Found by the two scratch measurements, not filed as an engine fault**: each read words missing from a page in the engine's two columns while Chromium's partition was identical to the engine's — four words of a list item carrying an inline-block `::before`, and the last line of a page followed by a box that moved whole. Whether the print reader or the documents lose them is unasked; it is a question for the venue, not for this round.
- **The venue's runner reports what it did not run**: where no page was printed, *every page is W×Hpt* and *every word printed once* are `skip … not run` lines rather than `ok` (`packages/essay/cases/runner.js`). Measured on a scratch copy of case 35's document declared as if it paginated, and deleted: before, `ok [cicero] every page is 170.08×113.39pt` and `ok [cicero] every word printed once, in order` under the engine's refusal; after, `skip [cicero] every page is 170.08×113.39pt — not run: no page was printed (the engine refused)` and the same for the words.
- **The tablet is untouched.** The Cut already names a cut as a place; this says which boxes that place divides.
