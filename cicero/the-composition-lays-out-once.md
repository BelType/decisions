# The composition lays out once

**Status: Decided 2026-09-27, Executed 2026-09-27.** Convened by Egildo on the measurement below; one round, two mechanism fixes and a counter.

## Context

BelType's own manual (`docs/index.md --engine cicero`) is 266 pages, 86 303 words and about 52 000 elements. Measured 2026-09-27 in the composed mode, the run takes 108 s, and `cicero:compose` — one measure, on page one, composing all 24 top-level blocks — takes 84 s of it. The BGD manual (`bgd-assessorati.md`, 15 558 words in short paragraphs, 133 pages) composes in 2.6 s. A CPU profile and a Range-read counter, both scratch and reverted, found three costs on the manual, and two scratch fixes applied together took the composition to 10.5 s and the run to 34 s with the engine's suite green:

- **The hyphen probe.** `compose-dom.js`'s `measureHyphenWidth` appends a `<span>` holding the hyphenation character to the paragraph's nowrap clone inside the shared measure container, reads it and removes it, one paragraph at a time. A read after a write lays out the whole container again, which holds every nowrap clone of the region, so every paragraph forced the whole region to be laid out again at least twice — once for the probe's read, once for the space width's read after the probe's removal — and the profile put about three such layouts on each paragraph: about 63 s of the 84. Seating every probe before the one `ctx.measure` layout, reading them all in one pass and removing them all in one pass took the composition to 21 s.
- **The Compositor's scan.** For each node `k`, `compose` reads the width of the span from every `j` below it and skips the overfull ones with `continue`, so a paragraph of `n` candidates costs about `n²/2` Range reads whatever the measure. With the probe fix in place, a downward scan that stops at the first overfull span took the composition to 10.5 s and the reads from 6.2 million to 4.8 million.
- **The reads themselves.** What is left is 4.8 million `Range.getBoundingClientRect` calls, about 56 a word at about 2 µs each: 9 s. The way under that is Knuth and Plass's own — measure each word and each space once and sum — and it is not a mechanism question. It is parked below.

The benchmark saw none of it: `seeds/benchmark.js` wrapped `Range.prototype.getClientRects`, which the Gauge reads, and the Compositor reads `Range.prototype.getBoundingClientRect`, so the `rangeRects` column read zero for the composition.

## Decision

Two mechanism fixes and a counter. **Neither fix may change what is measured, only how often and after how many layouts: no law moves, no reading moves, no chosen line moves**, so every composed standing record must come back unmoved, and that is the proof. A record that moves is a defect in the fix, never a number to edit.

**One layout for the composition.** In `composeDocument`, a probe holding the job's hyphenation character is created and appended to each nowrap clone before `ctx.measure(nowrapClones)` lays them out; all the probes are read in one pass after that layout, through `rangeOf` as before, and removed in one pass before any job runs. `measureHyphenWidth` as a per-job write goes. Each probe sits where it sat before — the last child of its own paragraph's clone — so it reads the same advance in the same style context; what changed is that nothing is written between one paragraph's reads and the next's. `collectWords` runs after the probes are gone, as it did, or a probe's character would be a word. The space widths and every width the Compositor reads then follow one layout, the one the probes' removal forces.

**The scan reads the feasible window.** The premise, stated so it can be broken: **for a fixed end, a span that starts earlier is never narrower.** A Range's bounding rect over a single nowrap line is the union of the rects of the characters and inline boxes it holds, and a span that starts earlier holds everything the later one does. So for each `k`, `compose` walks `j` downward from `k − 1`, reading each span's width once, and stops at the first overfull span, since every `j` below it is at least as wide against the same measure. It then evaluates the feasible spans **in ascending `j`**, with the widths already read, under the same badness, the same demerits and the same `total < choice.total − 1e-9` comparison as before: ascending order is what keeps the tie-breaking identical, and identical tie-breaking is what keeps every chosen line identical. One edge: only `j = 0` carries the first line's indent, so with a negative `text-indent` it has more room than every other `j`, and a scan that stopped above it could miss a feasible first line. Where `indent < 0` and the scan stopped above `j = 0`, `j = 0` is read and checked on its own. A positive indent needs nothing, since `j = 0` then has less room and is overfull wherever the `j` above it is. The overfull fallback — nothing fits from any node, take the shortest line from the nearest — is untouched.

**The counter tells the truth.** `benchmark.js` wraps `Range.prototype.getBoundingClientRect` beside `getClientRects`, one column, `rangeReads`, counting both, and a line under the table says so. The composition's reads then appear in the table. The key changes name with the meaning, so a report written before this round cannot be read as if it counted both.

**Refused, and parked with the measurement that would settle it: per-word widths, summed.** Knuth–Plass measures each box and each glue once and prices a line by their sum, which would take the reads from quadratic in a paragraph's candidates to linear. It is not taken, because it changes what is measured. The architecture's measurement policy is the whole-line Range — exact to 0.031 px against the rendered line where summing drifts four times worse, with inline padding and borders inside the reading by construction — and a sum is an estimate of a span, not a measurement of it. I5 lets an estimate bound and never decide. Before it can even be proposed, someone must measure on a real document whether the sum of a span's word and space widths equals the span's own Range width within the Compositor's `EPS` of 0.05 px, and on how many spans it does not. This round runs that measurement once, as information, over the spans the fixed scan reads on the manual, and reports the counts and the largest difference in the verdict. No decision is taken on it here.

**Filed, not taken: the composed feed's hints.** The composed feed applies every paragraph's hints to the whole clone on every pour (`applyRecordsUnder`, about 55 ms a page on the manual, ten times the default mode's feed). It belongs to the whole-clone entry already on the checklist's frontier, and the sentence saying so goes there.

## Predictions, frozen on write

1. **Every standing gate comes back unmoved, and a complete run re-records the pin.** BREAK on any record that moves, in either mode — the composed tallies above all (composed-v1, composed-v2, v3-composed, v4).
2. **BelType's manual in composed mode: `cicero:compose` under 13 s** (84 s before) **and the run under 40 s** (108 s before), by the benchmark's `--url`, the second of two runs. BREAK on either.
3. **The BGD manual in composed mode runs under 7.0 s** (7.37 s before) **and its `cicero:compose` falls below 2.0 s** (2.6 s before). BREAK on either.
4. **The synthetic book's cold compose pass, `bench:compose-cold`, falls under 37.9 s** (56.8 s before, the second of two runs on this branch with `src/` as on main; the bound is two thirds of it). BREAK at or above the bound.
5. **The manual's composition reads fewer than 5.0 million Ranges** by the widened counter (6.2 million before, counted by the scratch). BREAK at 5.0 million or more.
6. **A DOM-free test pins the scan**: over random paragraphs with injected widths, monotone by construction, the fixed `compose` returns exactly what a brute-force reference evaluating every `j < k` returns — breaks, lines and filler — with hyphenation nodes and emergency breaks, with `indent` positive, zero and negative, and with paragraphs where nothing fits. The reference lives in the test, not in `src/`.
7. **What the instrument cannot say.** The presentation and the composed feed are untouched, and their totals are reported without a claim. And the fixed scan's identity with the old one is proven only where the premise holds: the test proves it for monotone widths, and on a real document the proof is the gates and the page-for-page verdicts, which see pages and not lines. A scratch before-and-after diff of every composed fragment's text, the Compositor's breaks and fillers marked, is taken on both manuals to see lines; it is information, and it is reported either way.

## Consequences

The architecture's measurement-policy paragraph says the probes are seated before the one layout and the scan reads the feasible window, and nothing else changes there. The checklist gains a closed frontier entry for the composition's cost with its before and after, the parked per-word question under Design opens, and one sentence on the whole-clone entry. `packages/essay/RESULTS.md` gains the observation with the tables. The tablet, the driver, the Stonehand, `fragments.js` and the presentation are untouched, and *measurement takes no shortcuts* holds as written: every width that decides a line is still one Range over the whole span.

## Verdict

Measured on the round's branch, Apple M1 Max, Chromium 153.0.8010.12, nothing else running, each figure the second of two runs; before at `9e1ac5f` (`src/` as on main, only the benchmark's widened counter added), after at `a18ce11`. The tables are in `packages/essay/RESULTS.md`, *The composition lays out once, before and after*.

**Prediction 1 held.** The complete battery ran once, alone, with `src/` committed: 25 gates in 7.2 minutes, 25 ok, every standing record reproduced — composed-v1, composed-v2, v3-composed, v4 and the two composed acceptance and view gates among them — and the pin re-recorded against `a18ce11`.

**Prediction 2 held.** BelType's manual composed: `cicero:compose` **10.4 s** (83.4 s in this round's before run) and the run **34.2 s** (107.6 s).

**Prediction 3 held.** The BGD manual composed runs **5.64 s** (7.63 s before) and its `cicero:compose` is **0.77 s** (2.68 s before).

**Prediction 4 held.** The synthetic book's cold composition is **11.0 s**, against a bound of 37.9 s and 56.8 s before.

**Prediction 5 held.** The manual's composition reads **4 838 584** Ranges by the widened counter, from 6 208 236 counted the same way before; the BGD manual's 240 482 → 148 329, the book's 5 194 508 → 1 039 017.

**Prediction 6 held.** `test/compositor.test.js` pins the scan against the old one, copied into the test as the reference, over 3 000 seeded paragraphs — hyphenation nodes, emergency breaks, all four alignments, indents of −30, −8, 0, 12 and 25 px, and measures narrow enough that 860 of them reach the overfull fallback — reading 420 879 spans where the reference reads 1 534 323 — plus one hand-worked paragraph whose only fitting first line is reachable only through the negative-indent check. Two mutants fail both tests: dropping that check, and pricing the window in descending order.

**Prediction 7 stands as written.** The presentation read 5.29 → 5.18 s on the manual, 2.36 → 2.27 s on the BGD manual and 0.39 → 0.39 s on the book; the composed feed 14.8 → 14.5 s, 2.04 → 2.08 s and 3.83 → 3.70 s. Nothing is claimed about either. The lines were seen after all, by scratch: every composed fragment's text with the Compositor's breaks and fillers marked — 84 319 fragments and 90 298 breaks on the manual, 166 912 and 89 631 on the BGD manual — is identical before and after, byte for byte, once the Mermaid diagrams' generated ids are set aside. So on these two documents the premise held wherever it was asked. Every page's verdict, grab, report and feed details are identical too, page for page, on all three documents in both runs of each pair.

**The parked question, measured once as information.** Over the 4 816 986 spans the fixed scan read on the manual, the sum of word runs and space runs differs from the whole by more than `EPS` on 1 163 779 and by more than 0.5 px on 625 206, the largest by 3 072 px; in prose with no code in it, 102 701 of 132 951 beyond `EPS`, 4 678 beyond 0.5 px, the largest 86.4 px at a KaTeX formula. The breakdown is in the checklist's parked entry. No decision is taken on it.

**What was too good to be true was the plan's picture of what is left.** It read the 4.8 million remaining reads as the whole-line policy's own price, 56 a word, and parked the per-word move as the way under it. Breaking the parked measurement down by block said otherwise: **3.88 million of the 4.82 million, four in five, are spent composing the inside of the manual's Mermaid diagrams** — a `<pre>` holding an SVG, and the SVG's `<style>`, `<g>`, `<text>` and `<tspan>`, which `isComposable` accepts because their children compute `inline`. Positioned text has a narrow union of rects, so nothing is overfull and the feasible window is every predecessor; the scan's stop never fires there, and the Compositor wrote no break into any of the 75 diagram fragments. The largest lever left in the composition is therefore not a question about the measurement policy but one about what a paragraph is, and it is filed on the checklist's frontier as that. The parked question stays parked, and its own numbers are the second surprise: even plain prose disagrees with its sum beyond `EPS` on three spans in four, which is the architecture's *summing drifts four times worse* measured again on a real document. And the context's own arithmetic was loose in one place — it put about three whole-region layouts on each paragraph, where the code as read forces at least two per paragraph and the profile's third was not traced.
