# The paragraph is what the browser breaks

**Status: Decided 2026-09-27, Executed 2026-09-27.** Ruled by Egildo on 2026-09-27 with the plan that convened the four rounds after the fragmentation studies; opened with its predictions frozen, the build following on the round's branch.

## Context

`compose-dom.js`'s `isComposable` asked one question of an element: are its children inline? Everything that answered yes was a paragraph to the Compositor, and `composeDocument` composed every such element it met, at any depth. Three kinds of thing answer yes and are not paragraphs.

- **A block that does not wrap.** A `<pre>` whose children are text is a paragraph by that test, and `applyHints` sets `white-space: nowrap` on it, which collapses its preserved newlines, and writes its own breaks. [`packages/essay/cases/67-the-code-block-set-as-prose`](https://github.com/egildo/beltype/blob/42b348b547d269a217a7f193042fe216386a4650/packages/essay/cases/67-the-code-block-set-as-prose/README.md) draws it: eight lines set as three, the document on one page where every other column prints two.
- **The inside of a drawing.** An SVG's `<text>` and `<tspan>`, and the `<g>` and `<svg>` that hold them, have inline children too. The Compositor's words then included the drawing's own, and a break between them is a `<br>` inside SVG's `<text>`, where it renders as nothing: [`cases/68-the-atom-in-the-line`](https://github.com/egildo/beltype/blob/42b348b547d269a217a7f193042fe216386a4650/packages/essay/cases/68-the-atom-in-the-line/README.md) prints a line that runs off the page with three words on it. *[The composition lays out once](the-composition-lays-out-once.md)* found the time it costs: 3.88 million of the 4.82 million Range reads on BelType's manual composing its Mermaid diagrams.
- **An inline box.** A `<code>` or a `<strong>` holding text is composed as a paragraph of its own beside the paragraph that holds it. Where the holder is composed too its record is applied first and the inline one never is, so the cost is time; where the holder is not — a `<strong>` beside a list in one item, KaTeX's formula span beside its hidden MathML — the inline box's own record is applied, and it is set `nowrap` on lines of its own.

**The census, taken 2026-09-27 on this round's branch before any source changed**, over BelType's manual served under `--engine cicero`, by a scratch reading that applies the old test and the rule below to every element of the document. The old test accepts 16 714 elements; the rule accepts 3 783. Of the 12 931 it refuses: 7 758 inline boxes (4 785 `<code>`, 2 236 `<span>`, 392 `<strong>`, 188 links, 137 `<em>`, and a few others), 4 870 SVG and MathML elements (2 329 `<tspan>`, 1 400 `<g>`, 999 `<text>`, 74 `<style>`, 52 `<svg>`, 16 MathML), 306 `<pre>` (232 code blocks and 74 Mermaid diagrams), and a handful under `nowrap`. The applied jobs — an element composed with no composed element above it, which is what `applyRecordsUnder` writes into a fragment — fall from 4 100 to 3 783: the 306 `<pre>`, seven MathML and KaTeX elements, one `<strong>`, one `<div>` and one visually hidden `<h2>`. The plan that convened this round counted 4 553 inline code spans and 4 930 SVG and MathML elements by a census of its own; the counts here are this reading's, and differ where the two readings counted differently. The same reading of the displayed `<pre>`s: 307 of them render 1 814 lines in the default mode and 1 383 in the composed mode, 153 differing, with 331 of the Compositor's breaks in their presented fragments. The figure of 7 996 breaks written into code blocks, from the synthesis of the four fragmentation studies (`/Users/egildo/Privata/dev/fragmentation-study/synthesis.md`), is not what this reading counts, and it is not reproduced here.

## Decision

**A paragraph is what the browser breaks.** The Compositor chooses among the soft wrap opportunities of a line box, and composes only where the browser would otherwise be the one choosing. Four clauses, each asked of the element's own computed style:

1. **An HTML-namespace block container that establishes an inline formatting context**: computed `display` of `block`, `flow-root`, `list-item`, `table-cell` or `table-caption`, and every child inline-level or an author's `<br>`, as before. Never an inline box: an inline box is part of its block's lines and is set with them (css-text-4 §5: line breaking is of inline-level content into the line boxes of a block). A flex or grid container is not a block container and is refused with it: its text sits in an anonymous item the Compositor cannot address. **Inline-block interiors are parked**: an atomic inline's own contents, and anything inside one, are not composed, and keep the browser's lines.
2. **Its own `white-space` is `normal`.** css-text-4 §5: *"Wrapping is only performed at an allowed break point, called a soft wrap opportunity"*, and §5.1's `text-wrap-mode: nowrap`, which `pre` and `nowrap` set, says inline content does not wrap at them; §6.5 makes preserved segment breaks forced line breaks, which `pre`, `pre-wrap` and `pre-line` keep. So under any other value there is nothing to choose, or what there is to choose is not the Compositor's to take from a block whose newlines are the author's. Every other value keeps the browser's lines, and no warning is owed: there is nothing to own.
3. **Atoms are never entered by `collectWords`.** A foreign-namespace element (SVG, MathML), a replaced element, and an atomic inline-level box (`inline-block`, `inline-table`, `inline-flex`, `inline-grid`) are each **one unbreakable unit of the paragraph** — a word of their own in the Compositor's list, whose width the whole-line Range already includes when a span reaches across it, and whose inside holds no word and no break. css-text-4 §6.5: *"For Web-compatibility there is a soft wrap opportunity before and after each replaced element or other atomic inline"*, and SVG's text is positioned by SVG at the coordinates its author gave, not set on the paragraph's lines. A unit rather than a transparent element, because a transparent one is measured into a line only when the span reaches past it: a break placed after it leaves it on the line with its width unread, which is how case 68's drawing would run off the page under the rule's first reading, and how an inline `<img>` already could.
4. **At least one word outside the atoms.** A paragraph that holds only an image or only a formula has no line of text for a line breaker to set.

**The float and unspaced-script refusals stay beside it**, unchanged: a block a float reaches into and a block that may break inside a word are refused and announced, because in both the browser has opportunities the Compositor cannot model; the rule's four refusals are silent, because there the Compositor has none to take.

**What changes in code** is `compose-dom.js` alone. `isComposable` asks the four clauses; `collectWords` stops at an atom and makes it one word, and the three places that turn a word into a DOM point — the Range the width provider reads, a break written before a word, the justify filler written after one — learn that a word may be an element rather than a run of text. `composeDocument`'s loop asks the new predicate, and nothing else in it moves. **What is measured does not change**: every width that decides a line is still one Range over the whole span. What changes is which blocks are composed and which nodes are words.

**What it refuses that was composed before, and why nothing is lost.** A refused block keeps the browser's lines, which is the default mode's reading of every block in every document and the lines Chromium's own print sets. A `<pre>` keeps its author's lines, a drawing is set whole where the browser sets it, and an inline box is set as part of its paragraph's lines, which is what it always was on every page where its paragraph was composed.

**Refused: a warning per refusal.** The float and unspaced refusals warn because the pages there are honest and the lines are not the engine's where an operator asked for them to be. A `<pre>` or a drawing has no lines the engine could have set; saying so on every run would be noise about nothing.

## Consequences

The architecture's Compositor section states the rule. The checklist's frontier entry *the composition composes the inside of a diagram* closes with this record, and its parked entry on per-word widths is untouched. `.claude/NEEDED-FIXES.md`'s row goes; the SVG procession row stays, since the default mode's word space is not this rule's. The essay's own client applies records to clones it had not registered against their sources, where an atomic inline declared by `display` alone cannot be asked what it is; it registers them now, which moves nothing the corpus draws and makes the venue's reading of a composed document the engine's. The tablet is untouched: *The Line* says line breaking is owned; this says which lines there are to own.
